# The Abyss

> [!gist] At the moment I am in the dark. At the edge of The Abyss.
> Roy wrote it himself, Good Friday 2022, to mark the moment in history.
>
> The Abyss is the threshold where the old self can no longer carry the seeker forward, but the new self has not yet formed. Crossing requires surrendering control, facing the divided self, and allowing knowledge to become lived understanding. Refusing that transformation leaves the seeker caught between worlds and returned to the Loop.
>
> It is a crossing every seeker must make alone, but not divided. No guide can take the leap for you; the missing help is the half of the self that has not yet been reclaimed. In the catalogue, this becomes the Fool's leap: the heart moves before the mind can guarantee the landing.

**Where it enters**

* Roy's 2022 Good Friday post: "At the moment I am in the dark. At the edge of The Abyss."
* His IG Live teaching: knowledge that never rises into understanding leaves a person "stuck in the abyss between worlds."
* The [[Sir Rabbit]] poem's leap over the "Great Divide."
* The maze's drawn crossing: Hall of Mirrors room 16, *THE EMPTY ROOM*, a two-faced, monkey-like figure pulled in both directions between a dark pillar and a light one, beneath a colorless rainbow bridge with an empty doorway at its base, three serpents already in flight above. It reads as this page's mechanism in a single image, the divided self at its own unbuilt bridge; the full reading lives at [[Artifacts - Concept Images and Emblems]].

That is the surface. Open a drawer below only for the deeper readings.

>
> On his I'm 33 board, the Hero's Journey ordeal is labeled "In the Abyss, Facing the Shadow Self." The same material includes "Abyss In / Abyss Out" and places "Abyss (knowledge is relation)" between stages of the emanation ladder.

>
> These artifacts should be read at their source pages for their full context; together they establish that the Abyss is one of Roy's own recurring structural terms.

> [!lore]- Knowledge must become understanding
> In Hermetic Qabalah, the Abyss marks the apparent divide between the supernal triad and the seven lower sephirot. Da'at—knowledge or knowing—appears within this gap as a hidden, unnumbered position rather than an ordinary sephirah.
>
> That makes Roy's language unusually resonant. His board, and the minted City of the Pyramids page behind it, place "Abyss (knowledge is relation)" between stages: the word knowledge written at the exact position where the tradition keeps Da'at, whose name means knowledge. The gloss sits where the thing it glosses lives; held as a reading, not a claim of intent. His IG Live teaching warns from the same spot: information which never rises into understanding leaves a person "stuck in the abyss between worlds."
>
> The problem is not knowledge itself. It is knowledge that remains detached: facts without integration, language without embodiment, and mind without relation to heart.
>
> The project often stages this problem near the throat and mouth, where what is inward must become voice. The Da'at/throat connection is a Hermetic-Qabalistic framework through which this pattern can be read, not a mapping Roy has publicly declared.

> [!reading]- All you gotta do is Juuuuump!
> The clearest story of the crossing is the [[Sir Rabbit]] poem. Near the end, the seeker reaches "an edge below the Great Divide," and a golden-caped Spider tells him the way across is over the side and through the sun.
>
> When the rabbit panics—"I'm not ready to fly!"—the Spider returns the choice to him and refuses to push. That refusal is the law of the crossing: a leap made for you is not a crossing at all.
>
> He jumps while still afraid and lands "back where he begun": the same place, but not the same rabbit.
>
> Roy draws the same image in the public caption:
>
> "I saw her divinity, but not the one who held it, or where it led.\
> I followed anyway, white rose in my left hand, and stepped off the ledge.\
> I reached for my Father's hand.\
> He said you're half the man that you know."
>
> The Father does not rescue him. He points him toward the missing half of himself.
>
> The crossing cannot be outsourced. No guide can choose for the seeker.
>
> The crossing must be made alone, but not divided. The missing help is the part of the self that has not yet been claimed.
>
> Fear does not prevent the crossing; control does. Sir Rabbit jumps while afraid. The leap begins when certainty is no longer required.

> [!mask]- Don't FALL for it
> Every Abyss has someone standing at its edge, offering to spare the seeker the drop.
>
> In [[The Interview]], Lucius says it directly: "You naive little god. Without me, you'll drown."
>
> This is the counterfeit crossing. Lucius offers mastery, metrics, strategy, and control in place of the leap. He does not leave uncertainty intact; he sells a bridge that circles back to him.
>
> The true guide preserves the seeker's freedom. Lucius makes avoidance look reasonable.
>
> That is how the Abyss connects to the Loop: the seeker who refuses the crossing does not remain still. He returns to the same structure under the illusion that he has escaped it.
>
> On the guardian who stands at this kind of gate, see [[The Dweller on the Threshold]].

> [!warning]- Facing the divided self
> On Roy's Hero's Journey wheel, the Abyss is the ordeal: "In the Abyss, Facing the Shadow Self."
>
> The crossing is therefore not only a passage between cosmic levels. It is the point at which the old arrangement of the self can no longer hold and what has been buried must be encountered.
>
> "You're half the man that you know" names that division. The seeker has mistaken a partial self for the whole.
>
> This is where [[Sir Lucius]] becomes necessary. He is not merely an enemy waiting at the bottom of the chasm. He is part of the divided self that must be seen, understood, and placed into right relation.
> 
> Lucius is dangerous precisely because he is not wholly external: the false bridge is built from capacities the seeker genuinely needs, but in a form ruled by fear and control.
>
> In Crowley's language, passage beyond the Abyss requires the surrender of the separate ego. In the language of [[The Circle]], the seeker must go to "the inside of the inside." The traditions are not identical, but both describe a self that cannot be remade without passing through what it has hidden.

> [!question]- Where it stays open
> Is the Abyss crossed once, or repeatedly?
>
> In the Thelemic framework, it is a decisive initiation: the surrender of the separate self and passage into another order of being. But Roy's "Abyss In / Abyss Out" may translate that great crossing into the recurring rhythm of a human life—descent, confrontation, integration, and return.
>
> His language raises another question. If knowledge can leave someone "stuck in the abyss between worlds," how does that differ from the human being as the "in-between, animating Heaven and Hell"?
>
> One middle is paralysis; the other is mediation. The same threshold may become a prison when the divided self clings to its fragments, or a gate when those fragments are surrendered and brought into relation.

> [!receipts]- Sources and confidence
> Grounded in Roy's public language: "At the edge of The Abyss"; "stuck in the abyss between worlds"; "In the Abyss, Facing the Shadow Self"; "Abyss In / Abyss Out"; "Abyss (knowledge is relation)"; the [[Sir Rabbit]] poem's "Great Divide"; Lucius's warning that Roy will drown without him.
>
> Strong reading: these instances describe one recurring mechanism—the crossing between identities—and the Spider and Lucius represent true and false guides at the same threshold.
>
> Framework: Da'at and the throat, the Thelemic City of the Pyramids, the Fool's leap, and the Dweller tradition.
>
> None of this is the answer; it is one way of seeing it.
