# Flattened Identity

> [!gist] Television on your frame
> Everybody's all the same.
>
> Flattened Identity is what the machine does to a living self: it presses a whole person down into a usable surface, a brand, a face, a role, a statistic, and then insists the surface is all there ever was. Everybody keeps a few faces on hand; you wear one to the job interview, a softer one for your mother, and a whole person can take the face off when the room empties. Flattening is what happens when the room never empties. The backstage disappears, the role stops being a choice and becomes the rent, and you end up valued only for how useful your surface is to the feed. Different paint jobs on the same soul.
>
> This is the machine-pole sickness of the whole cosmology, and the catalogue moves *against* it, never past it: whether the machine ever gets re-subordinated to the heart is the project's central unresolved question, and no released work has answered it. The sharpest way to put the concept, held as a strong reading built on grounded vocabulary and not as something the songs state: flattening is not the mask. It is the system insisting the mask is all there is.

**Where it enters**

* Roy's Manifesto on mirrors.jagwartwin.com hands over the apparatus in a substitution table: internet culture quietly swaps *Immersion → Volume of content*, *Purpose → Reach*, and, the killer, *"'Feeling' has been replaced with 'Algorithm.'"*
* [[The Interview]] EP3: Roy asks what happened to music that was created from the soul, and [[Sir Lucius]] answers without blinking: *"the algorithm, baby."* The machine-logic spoken out loud, by its best student.
* [[Online]] (*33*, 2022) sings the database directly: *"there's an army up against me and they got my face"* · *"a bug inside your database"* · *"Internet is like a web and we're the Spider's food."* Not users. Prey.

That is the surface, and it stands on its own. The drawers go one reading deeper at a time.

> [!map]- The flat version scales
> The move repeats all across the machine side of the catalogue, and it always runs the same direction. Call it the flattening chain:
>
> person → profile · voice → content · witness → audience · artist → product · soul → star · self → utility.
>
> Every arrow is a reduction. A *person* has depth; a *profile* is a database row. A *voice* is somebody trying to say something true; *content* is a unit of engagement. The right-hand side is always smaller, flatter, and, crucially, more useful than the left. That is the engine: the system doesn't flatten you out of malice, it flattens you because the flat version scales. This chain is this guide's synthesis, one way of seeing it, built on grounded anchors and labeled as interpretation. Its place in the larger map (the closing term of a three-step descent through [[The Mirror]]) lives in the Deep End.

> [!mask]- The best-dressed reduction in the room
> On this reading, [[Sir Lucius]] is the glamour of flattening: not simply a fake, but the self *optimized* for the machine, the persona that wins precisely by becoming legible to the spectacle. That is why "is Lucius real, or a bit?" is the wrong question; flattening is exactly the state where that distinction stops paying rent.
>
> You can hear him run the logic in public canon. In EP3 he mimes a ballet move and lands the knife: *"Do you know what your lowest performing song of all time is? Ballerina Boy. When you're vulnerable you LOSE!"*, the flattening logic (vulnerability doesn't stream) delivered as a punchline with the stream count for proof. And in EP7 he flattens all of [[No. 1 Champion (la Conquistadora)|No. 1 Champion]] into *"this is another song about ME,"* when the whole chorus of that song is [[Mary]]'s voice; he has no line of his own and takes the title only by drowning her out. Appropriation you can hear happening.
>
> But hold all of him in frame. The same EP3 scene has the mask slip for a second, almost pleading: *"A good LIFE, Roy. For you and everyone around you."* The protective register speaking in public canon, the love inside the cruelty. The armor started as protection, and the truest read of him still reflects the child he was built to guard. Flattening can *feel* protective; that is part of how it takes hold.

> [!reading]- The Spider's food, sung as a jingle
> The clearest single doorway is [[Online]]. It names database identity almost clinically (*"they got my face"*), names predictive profiling outright (*"they know what you like in bed before you come in two"*, counterfeit intimacy, statistical prediction standing in for being known), and says the whole thing in one image: the web, the Spider, the food.
>
> The trick of the song is that it *is* the sedation it describes: a horror about being metabolized by the feed wrapped in an upbeat consumer jingle. This music is like when you hide your dog's medicine in steak, and Online is the whole project's method in one track. The comedy is load-bearing, and it is fully lyric-grounded.

> [!reading]- Can't compress this
> Set against the flattening chain is Roy's stubbornly uncategorizable stance: *"I consider myself no thing."* In the Mirror Sessions interview, when Briana Cuoco reaches for a box, a hiker? sober?, he keeps deflecting into no-thing-ness, even finishing her apology (*"Pigeonhole me"*) as a punchline. Anti-flattening played as a running gag, and it recurs across years of interviews, alongside the stable doctrine: *"we are all programmed by the world around us"* (Billboard, 2019), *"we all walk around with these masks on... remove those masks we find that we're all human underneath"* (Prelude Press, 2019).
>
> And the catalogue names a cure: [[Weirdness]], the part of you the machine can't compress. The Algorithm wants repeatability, categorization, and marketable identity; weirdness resists all of it, not out of opposition but because it is alive in a way dead structure can't contain. The glitchiness is what makes you human. To be no thing is to be unflattenable: you cannot reduce to a surface a self that refuses to be a type in the first place. The deeper (and stranger) reading, where the no-thing turns generative, lives in the Deep End.

> [!lore]- Post-it notes and string across a decade
> The concept is distributed; it shows up long before it has a name.
>
> * [[Loser]] (*STF*, 2018) holds the earliest explicit diagnosis: *"Television on your frame / Everybody's all the same."*
> * [[Superhuman]] (*STF*, 2018) enacts the erasure it protests: the collective march (*"Left, right, left, it goes"*) flattens individuals into interchangeable units, which makes *"Ain't gonna be no face in the crowd"* the song's protest against the erasure its own "we" is performing.
> * [[Happy Face]] (*33*, 2022) names the algorithmic version: *"Hollywood on your timeline / telling you what to wear / and what to like and how to be,"* building to *"death of my generation."* The 2020 launch asset seals the smiley over a globe, the minimum-viable mask scaled to a planet.
> * The Sir Lucius "One Hit Wonder" Instagram rant (Halloween 2024, on the artist's own verified account) is the flattened metric weaponized as comedy: *"Charlie xcx is cool, the 1975 are cool, Chappell Roan...cool, Jagwar...well..."*, signed *"Your loyal servant, Sir Lucius."* The shadow trashing the artist by the industry's own scorecard, on the artist's own account.
>
> There are so many strings; the fuller census, the clone-thread, and the early-arc reading are in the Deep End.

> [!warning]- The problem was never the mirror
> The single most important thing not to get wrong: the antagonist here is the *severing*, not the machine. Get this backwards and the whole cosmology collapses into generic anti-tech mysticism. A machine subordinated to the heart is creation; the same machine cut loose from the heart becomes [[The Algorithm]]. The line to hold: the problem was never the mirror. It was the mirror with nothing behind it. (The full duality lives at [[Machine vs Human]].)
>
> One more guard: "we are one" is not "we are interchangeable." Communion holds distinct voices together; flattening deletes the distinctions and calls the sameness unity. That fork lives at [[Communion vs Spectacle]].

> [!question]- Forks we don't get to pick
> **Is Lucius the Algorithm's agent, product, or reflection?** The load-bearing one, because it decides whether flattening is done *to* him or *by* him. The catalogue supports all three at different moments, and *"the algorithm, baby"* is deliberately ambiguous about which. **Is even Roy a face?** The "I'm no thing" stance is itself performed on camera; the Roy-persona may also be a curated surface, an overcorrection rather than the clean unflattened self. It doesn't deflate the resistance, but it keeps us honest about where the resistance is staged (see [[The Speaker Problem]]). **Does the arc actually resolve**, machine re-subordinated to the heart, or end with Lucius still the final boss? Never pre-decide it. **When was the Manifesto written?** If *Feeling → Algorithm* predates the songs it is a 2018-coexistent diagnosis; if later, the apparatus may be grounding applied after the fact; the date would decide it. **Does [[Mary]] model the antidote, or only gesture at it?** Whether the work enacts the non-ranking gaze or just names the longing for it is unsettled. **And how does flattening actually happen**: imposed by the industry, adopted as survival, invited by the audience, or co-produced by a person seeking legibility? No single mechanism explains every case, and this guide keeps it open.
