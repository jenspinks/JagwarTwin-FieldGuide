# The Protector

> [!gist] I never danced again
> I killed that ballerina boy, I buried him inside.
>
> Everybody builds one of these. Something hurts early, too early to fight back or even understand, and the psyche does the only sensible thing: it grows a structure to keep that hurt from happening again. Label yourself before they can. Seal a perimeter around a good mood. Keep a list of who's safe. That structure is the Protector, and the first thing to hold about it is that it is not a villain at its origin. It is adaptation that worked. The Protector is the function, the survival-armor itself; [[Sir Lucius]] is what the function becomes once it hardens into a named character, a moment he became more visible, not a moment he began.
>
> The arc runs wound → armor → calcification → re-ordering, and the resolution the work keeps pointing at is correct ordering, not elimination: heart over mind, "my mind must serve my heart." Roy gives us the endpoint plainly: Lucius is the mind, a tool, a force that leads astray when it stops serving the heart. Whether the order ever actually gets set right, or Lucius stays "the final boss," is the central open question of the whole project, so hold everything below as approaching, never as arrived.

**Where it enters**

* [[Ballerina Boy]] (2025): the wound itself, in the lyrics. Age seven, ballet, "I came alive when I was on that stage," and then "I never danced again / I killed that ballerina boy / I buried him inside." The most exposed doorway in, and the song Lucius later mocks by name.
* [[Loser]] (2018, with [[Good Day]] and [[No Fake Friends]]): the armor caught mid-operation before it has a name. "I'm a loser, baby, just like you" claims the low-status label before anyone else can throw it.
* Roy on the public Discord: "lucius is the mind. the mind is a tool, but that force can lead us astray if it is not subservient to the heart." The doctrine the armor hardens into, said in his own voice.

That is the surface, and it stands on its own. The drawers go one reading deeper at a time.

> [!map]- Roy hands us the doctrine
> The published LOOP single teaser runs the whole thesis in two beats: "Sir Lucius is the mind divorced from the heart… self-betrayal dressed as logic. Self-sabotage disguised as strategy," and the gate-mantra alongside it, "My mind must serve my heart."
>
> Two more beats fix the figure's nature. First, the Protector is internally generated, not an invading demon; the theatrical staging says it point-blank: "I made you." Second, the move against him is dis-identification, not a duel. Roy again, on the Discord: "we need to see his shadow and then say these thoughts are not my own. becuase what we fight we become, and we become what we fight."
>
> And the moral floor under everything: the shadow is not the enemy. Roy posted Dion Fortune's line as a quote-card, "The shadow is not evil. It is power unrecognized and therefore misused," and gives us his own aphorism on X: "Nothing is evil in the beginning."
>
> One honesty note before the drawers below run with it: Roy publicly defines Lucius as mind-divorced-from-heart, a tool, a force that must serve. He does not publicly define him as a trauma-protector. The survival-self reading that titles this page is this guide's synthesis, built on grounded material and labeled where it stands.

> [!reading]- Catch it in the act (2018)
> The clearest way to watch the Protector work is to catch it running before it has a name. *Subject to Flooding* (2018) is its operating manual: each defense a real solution to a real danger that then forgets to clock out.
>
> **[[Loser]]: shame-preemption.** "I'm a loser, baby, just like you." If humiliation is inevitable, control the humiliation yourself: self-label first, laugh before they laugh at you. It has a documented origin, older kids once ran a blog mocking Roy's singing, and the track is the answer, turning the slur into solidarity. Don't mistake the brightness for dawn: "sunshine in my mind" reads as bunker-light, not morning.
>
> **[[Good Day]]: mood-protection.** "Can't kill my vibe / Won't kill my vibe" seals a perimeter around a good mood. Watch the verb on that one word. In 2018 the vibe is defended, keep the threat out; in [[Not Your Homie]] the same word turns predatory, a mood said *to* a mark. On this reading, defended-vibe to weaponized-vibe is the whole Protector-to-Lucius transition in a single lexeme: survival-armor becoming self-sustaining spectacle.
>
> **[[No Fake Friends]]: social filtration.** "I made a list and I kept all the names." It begins as anti-erasure and hardens into radar, hypervigilance, access control: the speaker does not recover trust, he recovers altitude. The wound underneath is partly financial, a perimeter built around scarcity, and in the theatrical series Lucius later presses exactly that nerve, taunting Roy about money and housing ("even a studio apartment at this point would be a start"). Same wound, repurposed into a sales pitch.
>
> Three faces, not the only three. [[Superhuman]] is the armor in martial, collective mode, the vulnerable solo voice swelling into a roaring we; [[Hell Of A Night]] and its sequel are momentum-as-defense escalating into outlaw velocity, a song that sounds victorious because it is terrified of stopping. And one track wears none: the Arrowhead voice memo is the bare threshold-fear before any armor exists, the wound visible before it learned to defend itself.
>
> One guard travels with all of it: this is continuity of *function* read backwards, a retrospective rhyme, not proof that the Sir Lucius architecture was consciously authored in 2018.

> [!reading]- One grave, many rooms
> The wound itself. [[Ballerina Boy]] is the most exposed nerve in the catalogue: age seven, a Christian school, ballet, "I came alive when I was on that stage," then a teacher tells his father *we think your son is gay,* the boy overhears, reasons *gay must mean bad… I prayed to God to make me good,* and the girls close in: *you're not a real boy.* The burial is explicit in the lyric, and the song's resolution is reunion, not achievement: "do I come back for that kid I let go?", not *do I become successful.* The cure was never the outer game.
>
> But hold the grave as the central chamber, not the whole cave. The armor has more than one room feeding it: the scarcity and housing fear, the perfectionism Roy names publicly ("the perfect child all the time" as a persona laid over the real self), industry and metrics pressure, inherited ancestral voices, and shadow traces running back to 2008, before any of it had a name. A single clean origin story is more satisfying and less true. The armor can be read as having protected the boy; it was also fed from every direction at once, and it grew.
>
> And the song carries the counter-move to armoring. The spoken outro blessing arrives in the mother's, divine-feminine voice, generative rather than acquitting: "you are one of a kind, God's design… let your light shine." Not a pardon, a claiming. Lucius protects by armoring the child; the blessing protects by claiming his nature. One builds a wall, the other dissolves the courtroom. See [[Mary]].

> [!mask]- The Bargain (no one wants this love stuff)
> The Protector's defining public act, staged across the theatrical Roy v Lucius series on public YouTube. It shows the armor doing the one thing it was built to do: refuse sincerity. Roy brings the Song of Love, "my favorite song I've ever written," and starts to sing. Lucius cuts him off mid-line: "STOP!!! No one wants this love stuff." Then the pitch, and the pitch is the wound named out loud: the label won't release it, the money, the housing, the people he employs. When Roy asks what he has in mind, Lucius presses a browning banana like a button, and out plays [[Not Your Homie]]. "This is what the people want." Roy: "It doesn't mean anything." Lucius: "Exactly. It's nonsense. It's all nonsense." And Roy concedes: "Fine, I'll give you this one." The trade is not a one-off: in the next episode the sincere song is overridden again, the banana pressed a second time, and out plays [[Welcome to the Circus]]. "The algorithm, baby."
>
> Two guards keep the scene honest. First, theatrical versus biographical: Lucius speaking is the *character* speaking, never Roy admitting something. The whole exchange is a demonstration the artist built so we could watch the mechanism run, not a leaked confession. Second, no external hijack: character Roy *consents.* "Fine, I'll give you this one" makes the bargain belong to the whole person. There is no innocent heart and guilty mind to split it between; the Protector is internally generated, so its trades are ours.
>
> And notice that the comedy is not decoration, it is the armor's working surface. A love song answered with a rotting banana is slapstick that is also the entire thesis: sincerity met with a sound effect. When Lucius mocks the buried child, he doesn't argue the point, he pirouettes, miming ballet to land "Ballerina Boy. When you're vulnerable you LOSE!" For the Protector, mockery isn't a side effect of the defense. Mockery *is* the defense.

> [!warning]- The machine works
> The easiest way to get Lucius wrong is to read him as panic wearing a crown. The harder truth is that his competence is part of the danger. The nonsense song he pressed the banana for became a real breakout, and Roy fused the theater and the biography himself, conceding on the public Discord "I guess Lucius was right again" in the same minute he marveled at what *welcome to the circus* was doing. So the conflict was never whether his method works. It's what working costs: what self it produces, what has to be traded to keep it running. He is a real risk model, not a strawman.
>
> The limit runs the other way at the Song of Love. SOL is a boundary on his power, not a defeat of him: Lucius can silence it, mock it, interrupt it, but he cannot metabolize it. Nothing in the released work delivers a completed integration, and this page won't promise one. On this album, resolution means survival, not triumph.
>
> His contempt pole is public too, played for laughs: the in-character Instagram rant declaring Jagwar Twin "a one hit wonder" and signing off "Your loyal servant, Sir Lucius." The value system the music refuses, voiced on Roy's own account by his shadow.

> [!reading]- Reordered, not killed
> Here is the cruel knot at the center: a protector formed around trauma craves healing and sabotages it in the same breath, because healing dissolves its job. If Roy becomes whole, Lucius no longer rules the system. And you can't simply think your way out, because the thing demanding your attention is a thought from the inside; it uses your own materials, resonates at your exact frequency. Which is why the answer the work keeps giving is ordering, not war. Dis-identify, don't duel: "these thoughts are not my own." Two poles make a single person; you adjust the degree, you don't eliminate a side. The end the story keeps pointing at is an embrace, not a funeral.
>
> Reordering even has a concrete grammar in the catalogue. In [[Weirdness]] the pronoun flips: "your weirdness" becomes "my weirdness is my sunshine." One word of ownership and the split-off material comes home. That flip is what makes reordering more than an ethical instruction, because the Protector holds real living treasure: comic timing, boldness, strangeness, the buried child's creative life. Integration means reclaiming that material, not discarding the armor. Same on the other side of the ledger, a reordered Protector keeps its gifts: discernment, boundaries, strategy, speed, confidence, performance. It just loses the throne.
>
> The theatrical series stages the small version of the same move. Lucius shows up eating an apple and, asked what he even is, says "I'm your weirdness" (the "you're strange, and that's alright" line the scene plays on is the song's own bridge). Roy plays along, "your weirdness is the sunshine," and off he wanders, visibly relieved. The bluster is thin on purpose; you can see the need under it. The armor caught, now and then, reflecting the child it was built to protect.
>
> And in an ordinary public conversation, the same instinct with no armor on it at all: asked if he's a hiker, Roy just says "I'm no thing," lightly, no weapon needed. Same allergy to being flattened, two completely different temperatures. The re-ordering in miniature.

> [!lore]- The shadow that isn't evil
> In Jungian terms the Protector reads as the compensating shadow and the persona hardened under trauma: a protector complex that destabilizes the moment the wound starts to heal, and an ethic of integration over transcendence, exactly the reordered-not-killed shape above. Held as the nearest analogue, not a source claim, with one grounded exception: Roy publicly curated a Jung Trickster card, "forerunner of the savior… bestial and divine," so the Trickster strand is an influence he showed us, not a resonance imported from outside.
>
> One vocabulary guard: protector, shadow, Dweller, mask, manager are overlapping *functions,* not interchangeable identities. This page keeps them as lenses on one mechanism. The wider cross-tradition apparatus, the Tree of Life seating, Jacob and Esau, the lion's skin, lives in the Deep End.

> [!question]- Threads I haven't tied off
> - **Is Lucius protecting Roy or training him to rule the Circus?** The lore tilts from pleading toward teaching, sharpened by the real-world "I guess Lucius was right again."
> - **Does the arc actually resolve**, mind re-subordinated to heart, loop into circle, or end with Lucius still "the final boss"? The central unresolved question of the whole project.
> - **Can the Dweller become a genuine guide**, re-ordered rather than only contained? (See [[The Dweller on the Threshold]].)
> - **How early was the armor activated?** Age seven is the grounded wound; a childhood photo in the puzzle world shows a younger child already in toy armor. Held as early-and-temperamental, a resonance, not a clock (the full note is in the Deep End).
> - **Can the armor grieve?** Cornered with the Song of Love, Lucius doesn't rage; he gets unsettled, has Roy taken from the room, then sits alone and lets it finish. The villain's one soft spot, or the guard remembering it was once a child? Held open, and gently.
> - **Whose is the Test Pilot lighter?** A named relative's heirloom, or an emblem acquired for the maze? Decides whether the protection-as-going-first layer promotes to grounded.
> - **Does Roy ever name Lucius as "the mask" outside the character's theater?** A confirmation would tie the masks doctrine directly to the figure.
