# Consumption

> [!gist] I'll chew you up, spit you out
> Carnivores and cannibals, I want it on demand-able.
>
> Consumption is how [[The Circus]] processes whatever comes near it. Performers, audiences, attention, meaning: taken in, broken down for fuel, and ejected once the fuel runs out. [[Sir Lucius]] is the digestive voice, and he says the quiet part out loud in [[Welcome to the Circus]]: *"I'll chew you up, spit you out."* The line lands twice, and the vocals are credited to Roy English and Sir Lucius both. The useful thing about a digestion figure is that it scales in every direction. The industry eats the artist, the Circus eats the audience, the algorithm eats attention, the mask eats the face. The figure on the menu changes from song to song; the move never does.
>
> The other thing to know up front is what Consumption is *not*. It is not a sermon against eating, wanting, or screens, and it is not a transformation. The old stories are full of being swallowed and coming back changed; the Circus uses the same verbs and reverses the outcome. Chewed and ejected as waste, no return. And the villain is never the mouth itself. Receiving, listening, taking something in: that is how anything living participates in anything else. The danger is the severing, the moment the taking-in stops giving anything back.

**Where it enters**

* The naming line in [[Welcome to the Circus]]: *"I'll chew you up, spit you out."* Twice.
* The [[I Like to Party]] video (dir. Kyle Vorbach, 2021): Roy force-fed cake by a jeweled, ringed hand from off-screen, escalating until it is smashed across his face and body. Roy cast as the thing being eaten, and buried in the lyric, *"we don't want 'em awake."*
* [[Pay Attention (feat. little luna)]]: *"Everybody's gotta pay me / You gotta pay Attention."* The currency you spend and the resource being harvested are the same substance.

That is the surface, and it stands on its own. The drawers below go one mouth at a time.

> [!map]- The Cake Before the Circus
> The mechanism shows up on camera about five years before it gets its name. In the 2021 [[I Like to Party]] video the force-feeding is the whole plot, and the lyric writes the recipe: *"Devil's Food is a cake… bake a truth with a lie / then we sell 'em the cake."* The clap-along cheerfulness is not relief from the horror; the cheerfulness *is* the force-feeding. The official cover art plates the cake as Saturn devouring, the child-eating god ringed by skulls, which reads as the decoder for the video: the party that eats you. (That is the Greco-Roman devouring-parent figure, a reading the art invites rather than a claim about intent.)
>
> The paratext was already stamping the critique around 2020. The Happy Face manifesto card runs an infomercial parody, *"ARE YOU CONTENT? CALL NOW"* under a *"BROUGHT TO YOU BUY"* banner, and the Happy Face video flashes the literal words *"consume"* and *"BUY BUY BUY"* between frames, the pitch driven in faster than the eye can track. And the earliest node is older still: [[Good Day]], back on *Subject to Flooding* (2018), consumption as anesthetic, *"spending the money that I should save"* spent on numbing rather than changing anything. None of this proves a plan drawn up in 2018. It is long-form continuity, the kind of rhyme you can only see looking backward: the Circus did not teach the catalogue to consume, it industrialized what was already on the menu.

> [!reading]- One Mouth, Many Menus
> Once you have the frame, the catalogue lights up as variations on a single mouth:
>
> * **The crowd as appetite.** [[Bad Feeling (Oompa Loompa)]]: *"Carnivores and cannibals, I want it on demand-able / Mandible, jaw split, hit me with the gossip."* People consumed as content, scandal on demand. The internal rhyme has the words eating each other; the joke is load-bearing.
> * **Attention as the meal.** [[Pay Attention (feat. little luna)]] personifies Attention as a deity that offers everything and eats the one thing it actually wants: your awareness. And the personifying move is on the record, on the public I'm 33 board, in Roy's own hand: *"How to anthropomorphize attention? → Marduk."*
> * **The web that eats the browser.** [[Online]]: *"Internet is like a web and we're the Spider's food."* You think you are the one browsing. And the system's *"Buy! Buy! Buy!"* turns, by homophone, into *"Bye! Bye! Bye!"*, purchase and farewell as the identical sound.
> * **The sweet that clones you.** [[The Watchers]]: *"now they want candy / but I'm not Mandy Moore."* The crowd wants the edited pop-commodity, and to be consumed sweetly is to be cloned.
> * **The cheer-machine.** [[Great Time to Be Human]] hands you *"bloodsuckers with crocodile shoes"*, predator and consumer fused in one status-figure. Even the goodbye gets swallowed back into the refrain.
> * **The swallowed pitch.** [[City of Angels]] rotates the pronouns, your dream, my dream, your dream, as the city's sales pitch is aimed at him, swallowed, and re-aimed at the next walker.
> * **The parasocial bite.** [[Not Your Homie]], and the [[Bounce]] line that lands as *"you just love me for my lore"*, a word still being verified by ear (lore against lure, even Lord; a different song depending on the vowel you trust). The audience devours the mythology and never meets the person.
>
> All of this is pattern-reading built on grounded lines, and the contrast pole keeps it honest: the Circus does not own the only mouth in the catalogue. There is also the mouth that testifies (see [[The Voice]]). *Welcome to the Circus* is brutal precisely because the body that should sing is the body being eaten.

> [!map]- Jonah Came Back Different
> This is the page's interpretive spine, and it works as a comparison the songs invite, not something they assert. Scripture is full of being swallowed: Jonah (Jonah 1–2), taken down into the dark for three days and returned changed, and the Eucharist (*"this is my body,"* Luke 22:19), a consumption that is voluntary and nourishing. Those are descent-and-return structures. The Circus uses the same verbs and reverses the outcome: *"I'll chew you up, spit you out"* reads as the anti-Eucharist, the shape of Christ-speech loaded with predatory content.
>
> | The descent-and-return (Jonah) | The Circus |
> |---|---|
> | Swallowed whole | Chewed, active mastication |
> | Three days in darkness | No pause, immediate ejection |
> | Prayer from inside the belly | No prayer noted |
> | Returns transformed | Returns as waste |
> | Obeys his mission after | Discarded, show over |
>
> The two rows people skip are the load-bearing ones: the prayer in the belly and the obedience after. Jonah's descent works because something happens *in* the dark and changes him on the way out. The Circus offers neither. That is the difference between an initiation and a meal.

> [!props]- The third case: the snake that eats itself
> Two outcomes sit in that table, swallowed and changed, or chewed and discarded. The catalogue carries a third, and it is where this page joins [[The Loop and the Circle]]: swallowed and *recirculated*. Nothing leaves the system, because there is no outside for it to leave to.
>
> Roy comes at the old ouroboros symbol three ways. There is one tattooed among the forearm pieces, which he glosses as "a circle with arrows, like the snake eating itself but simpler," inked near the **WAKE UP** that carries the Gurdjieff line. On his *Mirror Sessions* podcast, talking with Paris Berelc about animals eating animals, he runs "life begets life begets life" outward until it takes in everything: "we're all eating each other… it's like that ouroboros snake, we're just eating everything," then closes it, "we're eating ourselves, that's what it is." And the aphorism cells of [[The I'm 33 Board]] carry the same figure in prose: "Man devours God, God devours Man. One Life feeding another end of its Self."
>
> Read next to the Jonah table, the ouroboros is the case where the appetite becomes the architecture. The Circus at least ejects you; a closed ring keeps you circulating as its own fuel, which is why it reads as the Loop's engine rather than a separate idea. What keeps a loop turning is that it is feeding on itself. That also sharpens the open question two drawers down: the Circus and the ouroboros both fail to transform, but for opposite reasons, one ejects too early and the other never ejects at all.
>
> Worth saying plainly, since his tone matters: in that conversation Roy is not indicting anything. He says it as oneness, everything folded into a single self-feeding life, closer to wonder than to warning. He never calls it a loop. Hearing it as one is this guide's reading, and the fact that the same ring can sound like communion or like a trap is the point rather than a problem.

> [!reading]- It's Not a Poster About Your Phone
> The guardrail that keeps this concept from flattening: the whole drama runs on one fault line, the heart versus the machine that was supposed to serve it. A machine kept under the heart is creation. The same machine cut loose becomes [[The Algorithm]], optimization severed from feeling, now optimizing *you* into fuel. So the project is not against eating, or attention, or the internet, and plenty of incorporation in this world is the good kind: the meal that nourishes, the song taken to heart, the communion where what is received becomes part of you and gives something back. The adversary is the severing, the loss of reciprocity, not the appetite as such.
>
> Roy draws the same line from the other side: he publicly rejects the word *"fan"* and calls listeners co-creators of the world. The Circus's move is to collapse that co-creator back into a consumer. Lose this guardrail and Consumption becomes a poster about putting your phone down. Keep it and it stays a diagnosis of a severing. See [[Machine vs Human]].

> [!question]- Still Chewing On These
> * **Who is the actual consumer?** The song pins the eating on Lucius-as-ringmaster, the self-crowned *"final boss"* who answers to no one he acknowledges. But not because there is some boss hidden behind him; the stronger read is that the true higher power is the champion he denies, the heart-self he refuses to admit outranks him. The appetite may run *through* him to a pattern he serves without naming.
> * **Is being chewed up the *start* of something?** Is this the Jonah-moment *before* the prayer in the belly, a transformation-through-consumption the *Welcome to the Circus* timeframe simply has not reached? The Song of Love is the standing candidate for the belly-prayer.
> * **Why can't Lucius metabolize the Song of Love?** In the theatrical series he has Roy hauled out when it plays, then lets it finish, unsettled. Is it an object his appetite genuinely cannot digest, or something he remembers and cannot admit? Indigestion, or grief?
> * **Diagnosis without exit?** Juvenal named bread and circuses and prescribed nothing. Is the project the same, or is there a way to bear witness *within* the Circus rather than escape or be eaten? ([[Down to You]]'s *"one cannot live on bread alone"* is the cleanest built-in answer.)
> * **Audience: failure or feature?** Is the crowd's love of watching you lose control a moral failure, or an engineered feature of a system the crowd cannot be blamed for?
> * **What can't be metabolized?** Is there a song built to *fail* by the Algorithm's metrics, anti-consumption by design? An unconsumable object would be the exact shape of the answer. So, quietly, would a mouth that stops chewing long enough to listen.

> [!descend] The Deep End
> The heavier apparatus behind this page, the long tradition of getting eaten, the empty husks, the two fires, and the alchemy that stops mid-rot, lives in [[Consumption — The Deep End]]. Dense and speculative by design. The page you are on stands without it.

> Grounded in released music and public artifacts: the *"chew you up, spit you out"* line and its credits; the *I Like to Party* video (dir. Kyle Vorbach, 2021), lyrics, and Saturn-cake cover art; the quoted lines from *Bad Feeling*, *Pay Attention*, *Online*, *The Watchers*, *Great Time to Be Human*, *City of Angels*, and *Good Day*; the Happy Face card and video text; the I'm 33 board notes in Roy's hand (*"Marduk,"* *"Let Them Eat Cake"*); Roy's public co-creators framing.
>
> Strong readings, held as readings: the Circus as a digestive system; the cake video as the mechanism rendered before it was named; the one-move-many-directions pattern; consumed-sweetly-is-cloned. One that needs its layers kept apart: in the theatrical series (EP3), Lucius answers the question of who wrote *Welcome to the Circus* with *"the algorithm baby."* The line existing on camera is fact; reading it as the Circus confessing its own authorship is interpretation, and it is Lucius speaking in a scripted scene, not Roy explaining himself.
>
> Framework, attributed to its traditions: the Jonah and Eucharist comparison, the devouring-parent read of the cover art, and everything in the Deep End. The songs invite these comparisons; they do not assert them.
>
> Still open: the [[Bounce]] *"lore"* word, pending the ear; the *Pay Attention* vocal roles (Roy voicing Attention, little luna the guide) read that way but held as a reading until the credits settle it. None of this is the answer; it is one way of seeing it.
