# Gatekeeper Saga

> **One-line reading.** For roughly four months a real record label sat on a real song, and Roy narrated the standoff to the Circle as a Harry Potter serial, house-elf and all, right up until the label *"gave dumbledore a sock"* and the song walked free. It is the catalogue's gatekeeping critique, performed live, played for laughs.

Every theory page in this guide says some version of *"the Circus decides what gets seen."* This is the one stretch where you get to watch it happen. Usually the lore leaks *into* reality with a shattering subtlety, a lyric just sitting there on Spotify. Here the leak runs the other way: a real business fight leaked up into the myth, and Roy live-blogged the whole thing in wizard-speak while it was still happening. The Gatekeeper Saga is the receipts.

## At a glance

- **When**: November 2024 to February 28, 2025 (public Discord, running commentary), on a floor laid two years earlier.
- **The song**: [[dumbledore]], codename and eventual title. Roy's stated read, *"it's a love song,"* the mentor register, not the plot.
- **The players**: Roy; Michael Morgan (co-narrator and house-elf correspondent, who opened the storyline and handed Roy the frame); *"the gatekeepers"* (the label); and the fellow *"potter heads and wizard nerds."*
- **Confidence**: the saga facts are grounded (public Discord permalinks, a dated release, an on-chain drop). The gatekeeping-critique frame is a reading, and a good one.

## The saga

Start with the floor, because the gatekeeper frame did not arrive from nowhere. Roy had already been dropped by the majors, twice, and said so plainly: *"Warner dropped me and said Happy Face wasn't good sooo haha"* (2023-10-01), and *"I was dropped from atlantic records and they thought the song sucked"* (2023-11-19). The ethic he pulled out of it is the whole saga in advance: *"And be willing to fight for it. Even if that means 'losing' in the short term."* By the time a label sat on *dumbledore,* the story of the artist versus the gate was already ready-made.

Michael Morgan opened it. Two weeks before the famous burst he was already narrating the blocked release to the Circle, *"Between you and me (and the rest of the circle) still waiting on exact date confirmation from the label but hopefully soon"* (2024-11-01), and he was the one who handed Roy the signature line first: *"U sure you're not feeling like dumbledore?"* By mid-November he had drawn the shape of the whole conflict, *"the realm needs to be ready for dumbledore before it can drop"* and *"Ohhh the circle is ready... we just need the realm."* So the co-narrator billing is not a courtesy. He set the table.

Then, on the night of 2024-11-15, the entire mythology arrived fully formed in about ten minutes. It opens as a complaint, *"I'm trying to release dumbledore but the gatekeepers are blocking me from getting it to the realm,"* and by the next message it is already a genre. The fantasy framing does real analytical work: label-as-gatekeeper, the-world-past-the-gate-as-realm, song-as-magic the mundane powers cannot evaluate. Watch the order, because the truth is better than any invented escalation. *"they couldnt even pull the sword out of the stone"* (23:21). *"the realm will prevail tho"* (23:22). *"idk about you but i feel like fucking dumbledore"* (23:23). The Arthurian sword and the Potter wizard land two minutes apart, one riff, not a months-long climb. And it closes on the line that is arguably the saga's thesis in his own words: *"thats how my days going. battling with gatekeepers but im gunna pull some magic"* (23:30).

The gatekeepers' actual demand was almost too on-brand: *"they said the song needs to go viral on tiktok for them to release the song and im like wtf haha"* (23:25). A song has to prove itself to [[The Algorithm]] before it is allowed to exist, [[The Circus]]'s house rule stated as policy, and he later reported the label had also called the song *"problematic."* So he did the only move the frame allowed. He pulled some magic.

The magic was a blockchain end-run, and the correct way to tell it is the way it actually happened: Michael carried Roy's message to the Circle, *"…dropped it on the blockchain becuase [sic] the label has no rights there! Haha go cop it"* (Michael relaying Roy, 2025-01-12). The words are Roy's, and so is the misspelling, kept here on purpose. The song escaped to [[The Realm]]'s actual architecture, on-chain via Coop Records, while the official version stayed jailed. Michael turned the community into the campaign: *"the more you all comment and ask for it, the more ammo we have to prove to the label that it should be released."* The January 2025 LA fires added a genuinely somber delay, the one stretch where the bit went quiet. And when the standoff finally broke, Michael delivered the resolution in the only correct voice, the house-elf's:

> *"Master gave dumbledore a sock, dumbledore is finally free."*

The label cast as the cruel master, the song as Dobby, freedom by sock. On February 28, 2025, *dumbledore* released through the same official channels that had been sitting on it, on Roy's label (Big Loud), by all appearances the very gatekeepers the saga had been jousting with, though the saga never names them and the identity is a deduction, not a stated fact. The song came out in two simultaneous versions, one of them literally titled *DARK ARTS.* (Whether the release is a standalone single or a six-track EP is its own tangle; see [[dumbledore]] for the genuinely messy paperwork.)

## Why it matters

**The critique, enacted.** Every song about the machine is theory until the machine does the thing to a real release, out loud. That is what this is. [[The Algorithm]] only amplifies what feeds it, and the gate said so directly: prove yourself to TikTok first, or the song does not get to exist. That is the machine talking, not Lucius, and it is worth keeping straight, because Lucius is the machine's best student and would-be usurper, not the machine itself (see [[The System]]). His own pitch, staged later in [[The Interview]], is that nothing you make gets seen unless you let him run the game for you: *"happy face, bad feeling, hundreds of millions of streams. Those are me. You need me."* The saga is that pitch demonstrated by reality before the character ever says it. It is [[Welcome to the Circus]] as lived experience, with jokes.

**The tone is the teaching.** In every archived post of this saga the register stays play. Roy fought a real industry standoff and did not file a single bitter dispatch about it (elsewhere his industry-fight voice is not always this light, see the radio war below). The comedy is not avoidance. It is the same wise-fool disguise the song keeps in play: the fool who steps into the abyss grinning, blank slate, tarot 0 (see [[The Fool]]). Reverence smuggled inside idiocy, which is the whole design, a spoonful of wizard-speak so the medicine goes down.

And for the record, this is the *only* documented direct Harry Potter engagement in the whole catalogue. Every deeper Potter resonance is convergence through shared older material, not influence (see [[Jagwar Twin and Harry Potter]]).

> [!reading]- To Escape the Circus, You Run the Circus
> Here is the honest complication, and it is what keeps the comedy from curdling into smugness. The end-run looks like pure defiance, the artist routing around the gate. But look at how the fight is actually fought: with the machine's own tools. Michael's *"the more you all comment and ask for it, the more ammo we have"* is algorithm-gaming in miniature, a coordinated engagement campaign to move a corporate metric. It is not the exception, it is the method. The **Bad Feeling radio war** (2024-01) ran the same play: *"it really is time to go to war. becuase [sic] we're at war with the forces of darkness in pop music,"* organizing the Circle into a request campaign. So did the **Spotify mission** (2025-11-16), Roy asking the Circle to loop *not your homie* *"171 or more time"* overnight because *"having a few other songs... makes spotify think that its a real playlist too."*
>
> To *escape* the Circus, the Circle *runs* the Circus. Roy is both the spectacle's fiercest critic and its operator, and that is not hypocrisy, it is the trap stated honestly: the artist who hates the machine still has to win inside it. Using the spectacle to dismantle the spectacle: the on-chain workaround is the machine's own logic turned back on it. (The mechanics are grounded, all public and permalinked; the irony-reading is a reading. But it is the reading that makes *"Lucius was right again"* land as tragedy instead of triumph.)

> [!feed]- Behold, a Wizard 🧙
> The saga's vocabulary outlived the saga. The wizard bit runs back to 2023, when Michael posted *"You're a wizard Luke"* (a Hagrid riff dropped over a literal *Harry_Potter.mp4*), so the frame was already in the water years before *dumbledore.* And it kept moving forward. That spring a Circle member built a batch of new server emojis, a wizard 🧙 among them (it arrived in a set, not as a saga trophy, so read the timing gently). Then, six weeks later, Roy reached past everything else in the tray and picked exactly that one to unveil the Inner Circle: *"Behold the Inner Circle 🧙."* The vocabulary becoming community furniture is the grounded part. Why he chose the wizard is the wink.

> [!mask]- The Label Does a Lucius
> Lucius eventually turns the *dumbledore* era into ammunition against Roy, but be precise about the target. In [[The Interview]] he weaponizes the *music's* tonal pivot, not the release fight: *"One minute your music is a twisted circus, the next it's Dumbledore. You don't stand for anything. The darkness or the light?"* He is attacking the swing from twisted circus to wizard ballad as proof Roy stands for nothing. The saga is our context for that pivot, not something Lucius cites; he never mentions the label at all.
>
> But hold the two side by side and a rhyme surfaces, and it is only a rhyme. In the theatrical Roy v Lucius series, the shadow's whole function is gatekeeping. EP1 is the pitch itself, nothing you make gets through unless you let him play the game. EP2 is the bargain: Lucius shutting down Roy's sincere love song, *"No one wants this love stuff,"* and pushing the commercial track instead, *"this is what the people want."* That is the staged twin of the real ultimatum, a song must go TikTok-viral before it is allowed to live. The label behaves the way the shadow argues. The saga even hands you the pun for free: freedom-by-sock is the Dobby beat, and Dobby's cruel master is named *Lucius* Malfoy, so the bit itself quietly stamps the name onto the gatekeeper. Keep it at exactly that. The label behaves *like* Lucius argues; Lucius is not the label, and he is not [[The System]] either, he is its sharpest student trying to seize its throne.
