## What the rewrite improves

The rewrite gives visual evidence the same seriousness the vault gives lyrics and interviews. It traces recurring houses, fire, mirrors, reversed lettering, costumes, colors, rooms, masks, and staging across eras without requiring every recurrence to be verbalized by Roy.

Its best contribution is continuity without overclaiming blueprint. Early assets can acquire later significance without having been designed with the completed mythology already known.

## What is grounded

The visual assets and their recurrence are directly demonstrable. Dates, publication contexts, and exact repeated features can be documented. Interpretive meaning and original intent remain separate questions.

## Strong readings worth preserving

Keep visual rhyme as evidence of sustained artistic preoccupation. Keep the possibility that later work clarifies earlier images retrospectively.

The page should preserve distinctions among exact reuse, transformed reprise, common design language, and broad thematic similarity.

## Required calibration

Do not claim that every early image anticipated the final system. Retrospective coherence can be real without total preplanning.

Avoid treating common music-video motifs as distinctive unless the recurrence is unusually specific or functionally consistent.

Do not let image captions silently harden interpretive claims into factual descriptions.

## Missing nuance

Visual continuity may come from collaborators, archived assets, aesthetic preference, budget, or brand recognition as well as mythology. These possibilities do not erase meaning but affect intent claims.

Absence and discontinuity also matter. A motif that disappears may signal change rather than oversight.
