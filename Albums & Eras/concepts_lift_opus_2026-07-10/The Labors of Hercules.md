# The Labors of Hercules

> [!gist] But if you choose to tame The Beast
> You've got to prove yourself to be a champion.
>
> Most of the esoteric scaffolding in the Jagwar Twin world you have to reverse-engineer from hints, string and post-it notes on a board in the brain. This one Roy hands you in plain sight. On a livestream, over morning cacao, he names Alice Bailey's *The Labours of Hercules* outright and teaches its core idea as his own: Hercules is the soul, and the twelve labors are twelve trials we all carry inside us, one per sign of the zodiac, each a power gone distorted that has to be returned to the heart's rule.
>
> The labor that matters most here is the Lion of Leo, the ego grown tyrannical, and the whole project turns on how that labor ends. The lion is tamed, never killed. Its energy is kept; who holds the reins is what changes. *My mind must serve my heart.* An embrace, not a funeral.

**Where it enters**

* [[Not Your Homie]]: *"But if you choose to tame The Beast / you've got to prove yourself to be / a champion."* Roy has connected this song to the Leo labor himself, the Nemean Lion, and to the Strength card, the woman gently closing a lion's jaws.
* The IG Live: Roy names the book and teaches all twelve labors as an internal initiation, *"we all have these inside of us… in different aspects of our life,"* then walks the Cancer labor in full.
* Bailey in Roy's own hand, twice more: a hand-underlined page of the Libra labor posted to his Story, captioned "Alice Bailey," and Bailey transcribed onto the "I'm 33 this year" board.

That is the surface, and it stands on its own. The drawers go one reading deeper at a time.

> [!map]- He tells you where he bought the furniture
> Roy installed this framework himself, on four independent public surfaces, and that is what makes the page unusual: no reverse-engineering required.
>
> On the Live he describes *"a book by Alice Bailey called The Twelve Labours of Hercules, and Hercules, which is the soul, which is us, the initiate, goes through all twelve signs of the zodiac essentially, but we all have these inside of us."* Not adventure stories: twelve faculties inside one person.
>
> He keeps three Bailey quotes in his publicly curated influences, all on the axis of illusion: *"Illusion is the first product of the mind when it is divorced from the soul"*; *"The path of liberation leads through the midst of the world of illusion"* (through, not around); *"The world is today the victim of illusion, propaganda, and mass suggestion."*
>
> He posted the hand-underlined Libra page to his Story, and he rendered Bailey onto the "I'm 33 this year" board in his own hand: money as externalized energy *"transmuted by appropriate alchemy,"* the race-mind material beside "Coding Consciousness," a ten-stage positive/negative virtue-and-vice scale. Bailey sits there as one named pillar among his others (Walter Russell, the Kybalion, Kabbalah, Jung, Plato). One lens among many, but a lens he bought, annotated, and screwed to the wall.

> [!lore]- Not the lion you remember from school
> The Twelve Labors everyone half-remembers are Greek adventure stories: the lion, the hydra, the stables. The version Roy reads is stranger and more useful. In Alice A. Bailey's *The Labours of Hercules* (Theosophy; Lucis Publishing, 1974) the labors are a map of the soul's path, and Hercules is not a strongman but the prototype aspirant, an earnest, erring, deeply flawed human being who learns through trial and error.
>
> Three working rules from Bailey's reading, kept explicit because they differ from the schoolbook myths. First, the order is **zodiacal**, Aries I through Pisces XII, so the Lion is Labor V (Leo), and this guide numbers the labors Bailey's way because that is the way Roy does. Second, the soul must **choose** the trial; election comes first, and the whole cycle runs *against the tide* of material instinct. Hold the word *choose*; it comes back load-bearing in the lyrics. Third, a labor is a change in *capacity*, not a trophy: the seeker often fails, repeats, or learns a new relation to the thing (the doe of Cancer must be released, not clutched). Capturing the symbol is not the same as integrating its lesson.

> [!reading]- Three lines, one lion
> The clearest anchor in the catalogue is the [[Not Your Homie]] chorus turn, and it is the one place the mapping does not rest on our pattern-finding: Roy has tied this song to the Leo labor and the Strength card himself.
>
> With that pin in place, watch how much the three lines carry. ***The Beast***, capitalized and definite-articled: a specific, named entity. ***Tame***, not *fight*, not *slay*: taming through direct contact is the operative move of both Bailey's Leo and the Strength card, mastery through gentleness rather than combat at a distance. (Swap in *slay* and the reading collapses; *tame* is the load-bearing word.) And ***champion***: in a body of work built on ordeal, descent, and return, it reads as the technical term for one who has completed the trials, not gym-floor motivation. *"Choose to"* is Bailey's election requirement hiding in plain sight; the trial is one you volunteer for.
>
> The champion ethic is also lived, not just sung. Talking about being dropped by his major label, Roy puts it in his own biographical voice: *"be willing to fight for it. Even if that means losing in the short term."*

> [!map]- Chart first, catalogue second
> Roy's method, and the cleanest tell of how he actually uses the framework: natal chart, then sign, then Bailey labor, then soul-lesson. He taught the Cancer labor and has Venus in Cancer. He posted the Libra labor and is Libra Rising. He blames his mid-lecture tangents on his Gemini Sun. (Those three placements are what he has given publicly; fuller charts circulate in fan readings but are not sourced, so this guide leans only on the three.)
>
> His unit, in other words, is *the soul's lesson per sign, lived through his own chart*, not "song X equals labor Y." Whether the catalogue also maps track-by-track onto the wheel is a separate and still-open question, with exactly one grounded pin so far (Not Your Homie at Leo). The full speculative wheel, string and thumbtacks and all, lives in the Deep End, labeled row by row.

> [!mask]- The lion loose outside the cave
> In the myth, before Hercules ever enters the cave, the lion ravages the countryside in the open. Two songs read as that stage, and both readings are held as readings, not stated facts.
>
> [[Welcome to the Circus]] as the lion operating publicly, un-confronted: *"every day is a circus all in my brain,"* *"no exit sign in my maze,"* the dominant ego running the show rather than being met. Whether that song sits at Leo is a question Roy has left open, so the guide holds it open too. [[Superhuman]] reads as the other failure mode: the brute-force misreading, *over* rather than *through*, an army performing more-than-human adequacy instead of walking into the cave unarmed.
>
> By June 2026 Roy's own feed goes martial: *"the time has come to slay the lion,"* and earlier, quoting Blavatsky, *"The mind is the slayer of the real / We must slay the slayer."* Note the shape: *slay the slayer* is not *slay the lion and be done.* What gets slain is the mind's claim to run the house, not the mind. Even at full volume, the register reads as demotion, not execution.

> [!warning]- An embrace, not a funeral
> The arc this framework hands the project, and the place it would be easiest to get wrong. Bailey's answer to a powerful part of the self that has stopped serving is never *destroy it*, and it is not quite *merge with it* either. The move is quieter and stranger: bring the thing back into right relation. The lion's energy is preserved; its *authority* is what dies. Hercules wears the skin afterward. The Protector, the wound-born armor, becomes the coat you walk the rest of the road in.
>
> This is why [[Sir Lucius]] reads as the Nemean Lion, Bailey's "coordinated dominant personality," the ego built to survive, now grown too strong to share the house, and why the cure is never amputation. It is also near-identical, in architecture, to what Jung calls shadow work: the disowned material holds real value, and the task is relationship, not elimination. That is Jung's parallel, named as a parallel, not Roy's stated intent.
>
> And mark where the public work actually stands: nothing in it kills the lion, and nothing in it finishes the taming either. The catalogue names the trial, circles the cave, shows the lion loose. The endpoint the framework points at is polarity held in right order, not one twin swallowing the other, and the work is still approaching it.

> [!lore]- Cacao, matcha, and a browning banana
> Worth saying plainly, because the material is genuinely funny: this is a man teaching ancient soul-initiation over a cup of hot chocolate, losing his own thread mid-lecture (*"what was I saying… Sometimes I'll forget where I was going with the thing"*) and blaming his sun sign. He signs off the whole esoteric broadcast like a radio DJ: *"I am at peace, in peace, in pieces… alright, Elroy sounding off. Love you guys."*
>
> And in [[The Interview]], adjacent to the tame-the-Beast song, [[Sir Lucius|Lucius]] lifts a browning banana from the fruit bowl and presses it like a play button to drop *not your homie*, then cheerfully concedes the press means nothing: *"It's nonsense. It's all nonsense."* The framework is dead serious about the wound underneath. It is also not above letting the ego strangle itself on a banana.

> [!question]- Left open on purpose
> **One lens or the whole wheel?** Roy's own framing on the Live ("all twelve… inside of us") favors the whole cycle as internal initiation, but that is his reading of the *book*; whether the *catalogue* maps track-by-track to labors is a separate question, still open, with one grounded pin. **Which song carries Libra?** The labor Roy underlined most heavily has no song anchor yet. **What is [[No. 1 Champion (la Conquistadora)]]?** A completed-Leo victory, a Virgo failure-labor (seizing a sovereignty freely offered), or Lucius naming a destination he cannot occupy? Its grounded tarot anchor is the Fool, and its chorus is in [[Mary]]'s voice, which complicates any tidy reading. **How far back does Bailey go?** Whether the earlier albums (*Subject to Flooding*, *33*) carry Hercules anchors would establish how long this has been a working reference. **And the name:** does Roy ever connect Sir Lucius to Bailey's Lucis Trust lineage, or is the light-root convergence pure substrate? (The Deep End takes that one up properly.)
