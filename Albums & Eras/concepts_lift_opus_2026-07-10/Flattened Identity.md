# Flattened Identity

> **One-line reading.** Flattened Identity is what the machine does to a living self: it presses a whole person down into a usable surface, brand, face, role, statistic, avatar, and then insists that surface is all there ever was.

## At a glance

- **In one line**: the reduction of a whole person to a usable surface, plus the system's insistence that the surface is the whole person.
- **Where it shows up**: [[Online]] (database identity, predictive profiling) · [[Happy Face]] (the minimum-viable mask, scaled to a generation) · [[Welcome to the Circus]] (the self as spectacle, authored by *"the algorithm, baby"*).
- **Connected ideas**: [[The Algorithm]] · [[Sir Lucius]] · [[The Mask]] · [[The Mirror]] · [[Witnessing vs Surveillance]] · [[Machine vs Human]].
- **Confidence**: a strong reading built on grounded vocabulary. Roy names *the Algorithm*, *programming*, and *masks* in his own voice; the song-by-song flattening readings are interpretation, and this guide labels them as such. If it ever sounds like the answer, it is being read wrong.

## What it is

Everybody keeps a few faces on hand. You wear one to the job interview, another to the group chat, a softer one for your mother. That is not flattening, that is just being a person, and a whole person can take the face off when the room empties out.

Flattening is what happens when the room never empties. It is the machine taking a whole self, all its rooms and weather, and pressing it down into something flat enough to read and cheap enough to use: a profile, a content-type, a target demographic, a star. The backstage disappears. The role stops being a choice and becomes the rent. And you end up valued only for how useful your surface is to the feed. Different paint jobs on the same soul.

The sharpest way to put it, and the line this whole page hangs on:

> **Flattened Identity is not the mask. It is the system insisting the mask is all there is.**

A mask you can put down. Flattening is when the world stops recognizing the face underneath, and then, quietly, so do you.

## How it works

The move repeats all across the machine side of the catalogue, and it always runs the same direction. Call it the flattening chain:

> person → profile · voice → content · witness → audience · artist → product · soul → star · self → utility.

Every arrow is a reduction. A *person* has depth; a *profile* is a database row. A *voice* is somebody trying to say something true; *content* is a unit of engagement. The right-hand side is always smaller, flatter, and, crucially, more *useful* than the left. That is the engine. The system doesn't flatten you out of malice. It flattens you because the flat version scales.

That engine is also the antagonist's job description, though he is never only the antagonist. [[Sir Lucius]] is the **glamour of flattening**, not simply a fake, but *the self optimized for the machine*: the persona that wins precisely by becoming legible to the spectacle. This is why "is Lucius real, or a bit?" is the wrong question. Flattening is exactly the state in which that distinction stops paying rent. What performs is what counts; the rest is dropped. Lucius is what's left when Roy's three survival-faces, Loser, Good Day, No Fake Friends, calcify into one theatrical identity that knows how to stream. But keep the whole of him in frame, because the mind severed from the heart started out as protection, and the truest read of him is the one still reflecting the child he was built to guard.

And it gives the work its hero-shaped resistance, too. Set against the flattening chain is Roy's own stubbornly uncategorizable stance: *"I'm no thing."* In the Mirror Sessions interview, when Briana Cuoco reaches for a box, *a hiker? sober?*, Roy keeps deflecting into *"I consider myself no thing,"* even finishing her apology (*"Pigeonhole me"*) as a punchline. Anti-flattening played as a running gag. But it is load-bearing: to be no thing is to be unflattenable. You cannot reduce to a surface a self that refuses to be a type in the first place.

So the concept has a shape: a machine that turns selves into surfaces, a persona who is the surface optimized, and a person who keeps slipping the category. Hold those three and most of the catalogue's "machine" material clicks into place. That is one way of seeing it, not the last word, but it is a way that keeps clicking.

> [!map]- The Conveyor Belt Ends Here
> This guide's framework reading places flattening as the *closing term* of a three-step descent: **Inherited Substrate** (where the raw material of the self comes from) → **The Mirror** (how the self gets mediated into an image) → **Flattened Identity** (what the machine does to that image once it has been severed from the heart). It is the corrupt endpoint, the living self reduced to a useful reflection.
>
> In Kabbalah grammar this is **Yesod severed from Tiferet**: the image-faculty cut loose from the heart it was meant to serve (single home for the Tree map is [[Kabbalah - The Tree of Life]]). That severing is the whole diagnosis. Hold it lightly, it is syncretic framework, not a claim the songs make.

> [!reading]- Spot the Difference (Mask, Mirror, Double)
> The concept only stays useful if you don't let it blur into the ideas next to it:
> - **vs [[The Mask]]**: a mask can be *chosen*: protective, ritual, theatrical, transformative. Flattening is *imposed* or *rewarded* until the person can no longer easily take it off.
> - **vs [[The Mirror]]**: the Mirror shows the self *as* image. Flattening is when the image is the *only* thing the system will recognize.
> - **vs [[The Digital Double]]**: the Double is the *copied* self. Flattening is what the system *does* to that copy: reduces it to a profile, a content-object, a predictive pattern. The Double is the artifact; flattening is the operation performed on it.

## Where it appears

The lyric-grounded anchors come first, then the distributed ones.

**[[Online]]** (*33*, 2022) names database identity almost clinically: *"there's an army up against me and they got my face / automatic in the static of the human race"*; *"we're stuck / just like a bug inside your database."* It even names predictive profiling, *"they know what you like in bed before you come in two,"* counterfeit intimacy where statistical prediction has become indistinguishable from being known. And it says the whole thing outright in one image: *"Internet is like a web and we're the Spider's food."* Not users. Prey.

The trick of the song is that it *is* the sedation it describes, a horror about being metabolized by the feed wrapped in an upbeat consumer jingle (*"'cause you can get it online / everyone loves the internet… you feel good!"*), with *"but it's okay"* shrugging off every fresh horror on loop. This is the whole project's method in one track, a dog's unpleasant pill wrapped in cheese and bacon. Roy called it *"so fun and so ridiculous,"* and that is the point: the comedy is load-bearing. The puns are merciless, *"Buy! Buy! Buy!"* turning into *"Bye! Bye! Bye!"* (consumption and farewell as the same sound), and *"maybe Jupiter is stupider than people say"* deflating the whole *we-come-from-space* guru pile-up in one rhyme. Hold that deflation loosely, though: elsewhere the catalogue's alien register reads less as a punchline and more as *alienation*, the cut-off self remembering it came from somewhere else, so the space-verses can be mockery and self-diagnosis at once (that fork lives on [[Machine vs Human]]).

**[[Happy Face]]** (*33*, 2022) names the algorithmic version directly: *"Hollywood on your timeline / telling you what to wear / and what to like and how to be"*, building to *"death of my generation."* The 2020 video renders the smiley as a **gridded globe**, the minimum-viable mask scaled to a planet. (Roy ran the joke himself in a 2021 Halloween short: clown mask, caption *"When they tell you to put on a happy face 🤡."*)

**[[Welcome to the Circus]]** (2026) is the self *as* spectacle, *"it's a spectacle, I'm the final boss."* It is also the one place in the public catalogue where the machine confesses authorship out loud. In [[The Interview]] EP3, Roy asks the question the whole series turns on, *"What happened to music that was created from the soul?"*, and Lucius answers without blinking: *"The algorithm, baby."* There it is, the Manifesto's *Feeling → Algorithm* swap performed live, soul-music traded for algorithm in a single breath, the flattening machine naming itself with a wink.

**[[Ballerina Boy]]** (2025) carries the oldest version: the schoolyard chant *"you're not a real boy."* That is ontological violence, it attacks *being*, not behavior, reducing a living child to a role-violation, acceptable only if his body performs the correct category. The Algorithm before the Algorithm: social flattening, no machine required. And Lucius has a stake in keeping that grave dug, though the reason is sadder than cruelty. In [[The Interview]] EP3 he mimes a ballet move and lands the knife: *"Do you know what your lowest performing song of all time is? Ballerina Boy. When you're vulnerable you LOSE!"*, the flattening logic (vulnerability doesn't stream) delivered as a punchline, with the stream count for proof. But the armor is protecting its own job. If Roy went back and dug the buried child up, healed, whole, the heart back in charge, the protector would be out of work, and a guide like this rarely outlives the healing it was built to point toward. So he mocks the tender song *because* rescuing it would end him. He craves the healing and sabotages it in the same breath. And the same EP3 scene has him drop the mask for a second, almost pleading, *"A good LIFE, Roy. For you and everyone around you,"* which is the protective register speaking in public canon, the love inside the cruelty.

The concept is *distributed*, though. It shows up all across the catalogue long before it has a name, and once you start pulling the thread it is post-it notes and string across a decade of songs:

- **[[Long Time Coming]]** (*STF*, 2018) collapses every social rank into one shared condition: *"rich man, poor man… prophet and priest… all of us bleed… nobody's free."* Every role flattens into the same posture, bleeding, kneeling, trapped, performing. It already contains the diagnostic blueprint of the Circus. The mirror-recognition lands too: *"every night when I look in the mirror / I see the disguise on my face getting clearer."* And the allegiance-shift, *"this one's for the faceless / the lost, not the famous."*
- **[[No Fake Friends]]** (*STF*, 2018) runs flattening *in reverse*: *"they ain't even on my radar,"* excluded people aren't enemies, they are erased from attention itself. Not *you're out*; *you don't register.* Flattening others down to background noise. (Same song treads the Walk-of-Fame underfoot, *"Hollywood I had the night watch / looked down at my stars,"* the heavens flattened into pavement.)
- **[[Superhuman]]** (*STF*, 2018) enacts the flattening it protests. The collective march (*"left, right, left"*) erases the individual into interchangeable units, which makes *"ain't gonna be no face in the crowd"* the song's own protest against the erasure its *"we"* is performing.
- **[[No. 1 Champion (la Conquistadora)|No. 1 Champion]]** (2026) stages flattening as *appropriation*, performed in the mix. The whole chorus, the count, the money-verdict, the title, is [[Mary]]'s voice. Lucius has no line of his own; he sings the title only by climbing onto hers, and across the close he drowns her out, she goes silent, and he repeats *"number one champion"* alone, the last time a cappella after the music has stopped. Then in [[The Interview]] EP7 he flattens the whole song into *"this is another song about ME."* Appropriation you can hear happening: taking the crown by overpowering the voice it belonged to. It is a Lucius pretending to have more power than he does, and the title he lifts was never his.
- **[[City of Angels]]** (2026) gives flattening its reward: *"everyone looks just like you."* (See the reproducibility reading below. Its sibling [[Playing to the Gods]] belongs to the seam not by clone-imagery but by the loop-mantra it keeps absorbing, *"never gonna die."*)
- **[[Great Time to Be Human]]** (2026) is the toxic-positivity rendering, the ad-libs as algorithmic emotional flattening, a sing-along where the content knows it is horrifying and the form refuses to stop smiling.
- **[[Weirdness]]** is the antidote pole, the part of you that *can't* be flattened (see the antidote reading below).
- **[[Loser]]** (*STF*, 2018) holds the earliest explicit diagnosis: *"television on your frame / everybody's all the same."* (The word *frame* runs deep here; held with a grain of salt, see Source notes.)
- **[[All My Friends]]** (2023) has the crowd flatten you into a clone; the friends restore the particularity.

Beyond the songs: Roy's **Manifesto** (mirrors.jagwartwin.com) hands you the apparatus on his own site, in his own voice, a substitution table where internet culture quietly swaps *Immersion → Volume of content*, *Purpose → Reach*, and, the killer, *Feeling → Algorithm*. And the **Sir Lucius "One Hit Wonder" Instagram rant** ([[Public Posts]]) is flattening weaponized as comedy: logged into his own band's verified account, Lucius ranks Jagwar a *"one hit wonder"* who *"can't sell out a tour,"* sorts the field by who is *"cool"* (*"Charlie xcx is cool, the 1975 are cool, Chappell Roan… cool, Jagwar… well…"*), sighs *"everyone is an artist or a guru or a dj these days. I'm bored,"* tells the fans to *"find something more interesting to do with your little life,"* and signs off *"Your loyal servant, Sir Lucius 🗡🍏."* That is the flattened metric turned into a weapon, the shadow trashing the artist by the industry's own scorecard, on the artist's own account.

## Connections

- **[[Sir Lucius]]**: the self *optimized* for the machine; the glamour of flattening; [[The Protector]] crystallized into a single theatrical identity. Not the System itself, its best student and would-be usurper.

- **[[The Mirror]]**: how the self becomes image in the first place; flattening is when the image is all the system recognizes.

- **[[The Audience]]**: where the flattened self is *consumed*.
- **[[Witnessing vs Surveillance]]**: the *gaze* that flattens: watched-and-ranked, not seen-and-loved.
- **[[Machine vs Human]]**: flattening is the machine pole's effect on the self.
- **[[The Aquarium]]**: the totalized closed system the flattened self is watched inside.
- **[[The Protector]]**: the survival-armor (Loser / Good Day / No Fake Friends) Lucius is built from.
- **Characters**: [[Roy]] (the heart the Algorithm processes; the un-flattenable *"no thing"* self) · [[Sir Rabbit]] (the searching identity-vacancy the wound leaves) · [[Mary]] (true witnessing, the anti-flattening gaze; the faceless one, her face out of sight, whose blessing is the one line Lucius can't metabolize: *you were born to shine*) · [[Brandon|Ballerina Boy]] (the un-flattened child still underneath) · the *champion* Lucius denies: the chorus's crown is [[Mary]]'s voice, and he claims it only by drowning her out (see [[No. 1 Champion (la Conquistadora)|No. 1 Champion]] above); the true champion is the higher power, [[Mary]] or [[Roy]], whose title he lifts while crowning himself the final boss.

## Advanced reading

> [!map]- The Usual Suspects (Bring Your Own Philosopher)
> Several strands of thought illuminate this, worth naming as *resonances* rather than as influences the work claims. None is stated in the songs; together they describe the same gravity the catalogue keeps dramatizing.
>
> - **Gurdjieff** is the closest of these to load-bearing, the one most often named as a touchstone around this work. His diagnosis is *mechanical sleep*: most of a life run on autopilot by a crowd of "many I's," no single self at the wheel. That is flattening from the inside, not a machine reducing you, but a person who has *become* the reduction and stopped noticing. The *"programmed / deprogram"* language sits squarely on this nerve.
> - **Gnosticism** supplies the cosmology flattening rhymes with: a soul-spark (pneuma) trapped in a counterfeit world built by a demiurge who *misnames* it. Hear it in *"you're not a real boy"* (the demiurgic verdict, identity assigned from outside) and *"a bug inside your database"* (the spark catalogued as a glitch). Flattening is demiurgic misnaming with a server farm. (See [[Gnosticism - The World as Trap]].)
> - **Jung's persona** is the social mask, necessary, until it isn't. The pathology is *identification*: when the persona swallows the person and the disowned remainder hardens into shadow. Lucius is exactly that arc, a survival-face that ate its wearer, with everything it refused to be now wearing knives.
> - **Apophatic theology / via negativa** is the deep grammar under *"I'm no thing."* You can only say what God is *not*; the unflattenable self answers the box the same way, by negation. It runs against the **I AM** register (*Ehyeh asher ehyeh*, "I am that I am," Exodus 3:14): the name that is pure unbounded being and refuses every predicate. *"I'm no thing"* is the human-scale echo, a self that won't be pinned to a noun.
> - **Erving Goffman's dramaturgy** (life as roles, fronts, and backstage) describes the *healthy* version flattening corrupts. In Goffman there is always a backstage; flattening is precisely what happens when the backstage is abolished and the front becomes the whole self.
> - **Data colonialism** (the Couldry & Mejias register, human life turned into extractable data and value) names the economic engine that rewards the flattened, quantifiable self over the full one.
> - **Lacan's mirror-alienation** describes the self that mistakes its own legible image for its whole being, the *"I"* constituted through an image that is also *other*. (A resonance, not a claim Roy sources Lacan.)
> - And this guide adds one more, **algorithmic identity compression**: systems dropping "unnecessary" cultural and contextual data to make a self legible. That is flattening stated in engineering terms.

> [!reading]- It's Not "Technology Bad"
> The antagonist here is the *severing*, not the machine. This is the single most important thing not to get wrong, or the whole cosmology collapses into generic anti-tech mysticism. A machine subordinated to the heart is *creation*; the same machine cut loose from the heart becomes *the Algorithm*. Flattening = optimization severed from the heart = Yesod severed from Tiferet (see [[Kabbalah - The Tree of Life]]). The line to hold: the problem was never the mirror. It was the mirror with nothing behind it.

> [!reading]- Same Eye, Split by the Heart
> Two gazes can land on the same person. **Surveillance** gathers data; **witnessing** transforms the witnessed. The algorithm does not know you, it knows how to shape you. Surveillance is a *forgery* of the soul's deepest need, to be seen and loved, sold back as being watched and ranked.
> The name for the seeing-faculty in this work is **El Roi**, *"the God who sees me"* (Genesis 16:13), the one who sees Hagar in the wilderness. It is tempting to call El Roi the antidote and the tattoo on *Lucius's* throat a savage irony, but that flattens it. El Roi is not the good pole. It is the contested faculty itself, *seeing*, which becomes witnessing when the heart is in it and surveillance when it isn't. Witnessing's fulfilled form belongs to [[Mary]], who sees without consuming. Lucius carries the *fallen* mode, and it is an origin story, not an accident: the Protector grew that eye in the first place, to be the witness the unseen child never got. The eye raised to guard the buried boy became the eye that can only watch, never bless. (See [[Witnessing vs Surveillance]].)

> [!reading]- The Fine Print Reads "Reproducible"
> The deepest single elaboration of what flattening *delivers* lives in [[City of Angels]]. Hollywood offers immortality, but the small print reads *reproducibility*, not resurrection: *"everyone looks just like you."* A circle needs distinct points held in communion; sameness deletes the points and collapses the Circle into a **Loop** (one point repeating). The cruel twist is that the particular self the dream promised to fulfill has already been deleted in order to fulfill it. It says *we are all one* and means *you are all replaceable.* Twinning gone metastatic. The clone-imagery runs the same thread through [[The Watchers]] (*"I'm afraid I'm a clone"*) and [[All My Friends]] (*"I feel like a clone in a crowded city"*). ([[Playing to the Gods]] belongs to the seam by its loop-mantra, *"never gonna die,"* not by clone imagery.)

> [!reading]- Can Not Compress!
> If flattening is the disease, **[[Weirdness]]** is the cure the catalogue actually names. One framework reading in this guide treats weirdness as the vowel principle in biographical form, the living breath that refuses to be flattened into dead structure (that consonant/vowel letter-mysticism is framework-tier, held loosely; see [[Letters of Creation]]). The Algorithm wants repeatability, categorization, compression, and marketable identity; weirdness resists all four, not out of opposition, but because it is *alive* in a way dead structure can't contain. The glitchiness is what makes you human. The blessing underneath it: your uncategorizable part may be your divine signature. This is the same un-flattenable self as Roy's *"no thing,"* and here the inverse of flattening turns out to be more than resistance. It is **creation.**
>
> Two of Roy's 2022 X aphorisms run as a matched pair. *"No thing ever creates another thing"* (X, Feb 2 2022) and *"What is the point? There is no point"* (Sep 20 2022). Read flat they sound nihilist; read apophatically they are the opposite. The *"no thing"* is not zero, it is the **generative void**, the empty glass that transmits perfectly *because* it holds nothing of its own (see [[The Empty Mirror]]): the silence a word comes out of. The same emptiness that, severed from the heart, becomes the hollow stare with nobody home is, held the other way, the fertile dark that *makes*. So flattening's true inverse isn't just the self refusing the box. It is the self that, by being no thing, can still bring a thing into being. *"There is no point"* then reads as freedom *from* imposed meaning, the clean ground meaning gets *made* on, not the absence of it.

> [!lore]- Before the Mask, the Machine
> [[Online]] maps the conditions that *make* Lucius before he exists as a named figure: **the database precedes the mask; the algorithm precedes the performer.** Run the chain, database → prediction → recommendation → habit → identity → self-narration → the voice of Lucius. The detail that bridges flattening back to [[The Mirror]]: the database *"got my face,"* and that is the first artificial reflection, a mirror that replaces you rather than returns you, a copy standing in where your image should be. Flattening as usurpation. When you don't have your own light, all you can do is reflect, and a shadow that learns to reflect only itself is exactly the sealed loop Lucius runs.

> [!reading]- The Cage Was Partly an Inside Job
> [[Dream - Dream]] (*STF*, 2018) activates flattening among its early dynamics, *"keep us in line,"* *"don't stick to one road,"* *"doing everything our own way."* But its worldview assumes the oppression is *purely external*, which is exactly what the later Lucius material destabilizes: by 2026 the oppressor is partly *internal*. The early songs diagnose a cage and point outward; the late ones discover Roy helped build it. That shift is the spine of the whole arc.

> [!question]- Forks We Don't Get to Pick
> Honest unresolved items. None is a gap in the songs; they are places where the interpretation has a real fork and we don't get to pick for free.
>
> - **Is Lucius the Algorithm's agent, product, or reflection?** This is the load-bearing one, because it decides whether flattening is done *to* him or *by* him. Agent: he runs the machine. Product: the machine extruded him. Reflection: he is just what Roy looks like in flattened glass, and the reflection fork has real support, because across the public episodes Lucius seems to *reflect his audience*, matching whatever is shown to him and mirroring it back, cold and cunning at the podium, almost human across the table from Roy, clownish with Leif. The inconsistency is the mirror doing its job. The catalogue supports all three at different moments, and the *"the algorithm, baby"* line is deliberately ambiguous about which.
> - **Is even Roy a face?** The un-flattenable *"I'm no thing"* stance is itself performed, on camera, in its own worth of persona. This guide can't rule out that the Roy-persona is also a curated surface, an overcorrection-mask rather than the clean unflattened self underneath. It doesn't deflate the resistance, but it keeps us honest about where the resistance is being *staged* (see [[The Speaker Problem]]).
> - **Is there an authentic spectacle the Circus perverts, or is all spectacle suspect?** If communion has a true public form, the Circus is a counterfeit of something real. If not, every stage is already the trap, and even Roy's own performance is implicated. The work flirts with both.
> - **When was the Manifesto written?** Unknown, and it matters: if *Feeling → Algorithm* predates the songs, flattening is a 2018-coexistent diagnosis; if it is later, the apparatus may be grounding applied after the fact. Either reading is defensible; the date would decide it.
> - **Is the "33 = recursive machine-awareness" pivot real, or smoothing?** The album-as-self-aware-system reading is elegant, possibly too elegant. It is worth holding the chance that the archive is tidying a loose pop artifact into more coherence than it was built with.
> - **Does [[Mary]] model the antidote, or only gesture at it?** If true witnessing is the cure for flattening, Mary (face out of sight, the voice that blesses *you were born to shine*) is the one who sees without ranking. But whether the work actually *enacts* that gaze or just names the longing for it is unsettled.

## Source notes

- **Grounded (Roy's own words, released music, public posts, official videos).** That *the Algorithm* is Roy's own term, the Manifesto substitution table (*Feeling → Algorithm*) is on his own site, in his own voice. The *"programmed / deprogram"* language (*"we are all programmed by the world around us… if we all deprogram ourselves,"* Billboard, 2019) and the *masks* doctrine (*"we all walk around with these masks on… remove those masks we find that we're all human underneath,"* Prelude Press, 2019), stable across years of interviews. The *"I'm no thing"* / *"I consider myself no thing"* exchange (Mirror Sessions), and the matched 2022 X aphorisms behind it, *"no thing ever creates another thing"* (X, Feb 2 2022) and *"what is the point? There is no point"* (Sep 20 2022). The first-person fame-mask description, fame as an *"isolated kingdom where there's walls and they're stuck… a scary place to be"* (Honey Podcast). The lyric anchors of [[Online]], [[Happy Face]], [[Long Time Coming]], [[No Fake Friends]], [[Superhuman]], [[Loser]], [[Ballerina Boy]], [[No. 1 Champion (la Conquistadora)|No. 1 Champion]]. The *"the algorithm, baby"* confession, the Ballerina Boy mockery, and the *"A good LIFE, Roy"* plea (*The Interview* EP3); the *"another song about ME"* claim (*The Interview* EP7). The gridded-globe *Happy Face* video; the Sir Lucius "One Hit Wonder" rant.
- **Strong readings.** The flattening chain itself; the *"not the mask, the system insisting the mask is all there is"* formulation; the Lucius-as-optimized-self anchor; the per-song flattening readings (City of Angels reproducibility, No. 1 Champion appropriation, Superhuman's self-protest, No Fake Friends' inverse flattening, Online as Lucius's infrastructure, Weirdness as antidote). Built on grounded anchors, but interpretive, labeled as such.
- **Framework (esoteric / syncretic, never authorial intent).** The Yesod-severed-from-Tiferet placement (single home [[Kabbalah - The Tree of Life]]); the Inherited Substrate → Mirror → Flattened Identity chain; the El Roi contested-faculty reading; the Goffman / data-colonialism / Lacan resonances. This guide *permits* these readings; none of them asserts what Roy intends.
- **How we'd be wrong.** Some of the per-song flattening reads risk being *depth-by-enumeration* or genre-staple over-reading, the work's own counterargument flags Loser's five-meaning *"frame"* as possibly that, and No Fake Friends' real-vs-fake-friends as a pop staple. We keep the grounded / strong-reading line honest rather than treating every quotable line as proof. A few [[Online]] lyric lines are still cross-checked against multiple sources; the core ones quoted here are stable. Certainty is a tone, not a truth.
- This page draws only on released music, public interviews and posts, and official videos.
