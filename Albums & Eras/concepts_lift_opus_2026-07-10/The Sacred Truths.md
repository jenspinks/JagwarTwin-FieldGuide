# The Sacred Truths

The old currents the work drinks from: the traditions that were asking Roy's questions a long time before he was.

These aren't decorations bolted onto the songs. They're the deep grammar underneath them: a single source and its reflections (Kabbalah), a world that runs as a beautiful trap you wake up out of (Gnosticism), one law repeating at every scale (Hermeticism), and a cosmos that breathes worlds out and draws them back (Theosophy).

Roy names three of them outright. Kabbalah is on the board. Hermeticism is on the board too, and printed on a music video years before anyone was looking. Theosophy he says in his own captions, Blavatsky by name, Bailey underlined in his own hand. Gnosticism is the odd one out: he never names a Gnostic school, text, or teacher. What's his there is the idea, not the label, the plain venue-bio wish to show *"a way out of this collective fog."* The rest of the Gnostic machinery, the Demiurge and the archons and the trapped spark, is our lens laid over that public posture, and we keep it flagged as a reading, not a receipt. Naming a tradition doesn't decode a song. It just means the door was already open.

And if you're wondering why four different old maps keep landing on the same piece of territory, the honest answer isn't that Roy secretly encoded four schematics at once. Three of these are genuinely ancient; the fourth, Theosophy, is a nineteenth-century upstart whose whole job was to braid the older ones (and a dozen more) into a single binder. The traditions were already reflections of one shape long before the songs found it, so the correspondences are what you'd expect from shared ancestry, not proof of cross-encoding. Truth is best discovered from many angles, but four voices agreeing is not the same as four voices conspiring.

Kept in front as readings, not verdicts. You don't need a word of any of this to feel the music; honestly, the fastest way in is to put on an audiobook about Hermeticism or Kabbalah, then just play the songs and let the meaning happen on its own. The reason these old currents keep earning a chair at all is simple: the answer here is stranger, deeper, and more layered than strict materialism fully explains, and these traditions are where centuries of people stored their accumulated psychological and spiritual insight. If you want the machinery, here it is. And if I ever sound sure, I'm doing it wrong.

## The Usual Suspects

- [[Kabbalah - The Tree of Life|Kabbalah]]: a single source of light and a row of vessels that only reflect it; the shape of Roy, Lucius, and Mary.
- [[Gnosticism - The World as Trap|Gnosticism]]: the oldest version of the project's most public idea, the world as managed illusion, the self a buried spark, the way out remembrance.
- [[Hermeticism - As Above So Below|Hermeticism]]: the opposite of the trap, one coherent law repeating at every scale, whose maxim Roy printed on a music video years early.
- [[Theosophy]]: the cosmos as a Great Breath, worlds exhaled and drawn back; *"the mind is the slayer of the real,"* and what Roy does with that line.

> [!door]- The Bottom of the Well (Mind the Drop)
> These four are the currents the work names out loud. They aren't the bottom of the well. Underneath them run the compilers and the mystery schools, the Rosicrucians, the lodges, the secret-society material that treats all of the above as one inherited system with the serial numbers filed off. This guide leaves that door shut, on purpose. The songs don't need it, and the deeper you go the easier it is to mistake a pattern you brought for one you found. Finding a place to hang a concept you already know, over a career's worth of songs, is not hard; that ease is the whole trap. But if you insist on the threshold, there's a name for the person who spent a lifetime standing in it: [[Manly P. Hall]]. Start there, and mind the drop.
