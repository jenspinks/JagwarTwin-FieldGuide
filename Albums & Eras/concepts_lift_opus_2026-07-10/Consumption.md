# Consumption

> **One-line reading.** Consumption is the Circus's digestive system: performers, audiences, and meaning taken in, metabolized for fuel, and spat back out as waste. [[Sir Lucius]] says the quiet part out loud in [[Welcome to the Circus]]: *"I'll chew you up, spit you out."*

## At a glance

- **In one line:** the Circus doesn't house you, watch you, or sell to you. It *eats* you, and the algorithm does the digesting.
- **Where it shows up:** [[Welcome to the Circus]] (*"I'll chew you up, spit you out"*) · [[I Like to Party]] (the force-feeding video) · [[Bad Feeling (Oompa Loompa)]] (the attention economy as a mouth).
- **Connected ideas:** [[The Circus]] · [[The Algorithm]] · [[The Audience]] · [[The Circus#What it produces (the Spectacle)|the Spectacle]] · [[Flattened Identity]] · [[Inversion]] · [[The Mouth]].
- **Confidence:** the naming line and the video are grounded fact, the digestion is a strong reading, the biblical and esoteric apparatus is framework, labeled throughout.

## What it is

Most critiques of fame reach for the same tired nouns: the machine, the spotlight, the marketplace. The Jagwar Twin world picks a ruder one. The Circus is not neutral architecture and it is not a shop. It is a *stomach.* Things go in, get broken down for energy, and come out the other end no longer useful. Lucius doesn't threaten to defeat you or sell you out. He threatens to *digest* you: *"I'll chew you up, spit you out."* Cheerful, dental, final.

The handy thing about a digestion metaphor is that it scales in every direction. The industry eats the artist. The Circus eats the audience. The algorithm eats attention. The mask eats the face. The figure on the menu changes from song to song, sometimes it's Roy, sometimes the crowd, sometimes *you,* the person reading the lyric, but the mechanism never changes. Something is taken in, metabolized, ejected. Consumption is the name for that one move, performed over and over on whoever is closest.

And it has been on the menu since the start. This is not a Circus-era invention. The earliest node is [[Good Day]], back on *Subject to Flooding* (2018): consumption as *anesthetic,* the post-scarcity high (*"now we're getting to the peak years,"* *"spending the money that I should save"*) spent on numbing rather than transforming. Not transmutation. Distraction. The Circus didn't teach the catalogue to consume. It just industrialized it, added better lighting, and sold tickets.

## How it works

The single most useful thing to know about Consumption is that it is a *counterfeit* of something real, and the real thing is everywhere in the same body of work. The whole drama runs on one fault line: the heart versus the machine that was supposed to serve it. A machine kept under the heart is creation. The same machine cut loose becomes [[The Algorithm]], optimization severed from feeling, now optimizing *you* into fuel. So Consumption is not "eating is bad," and it is not generic anti-consumerism. The villain is the *severing,* not the mouth. (More on that guardrail below. It is what keeps this page from collapsing into a poster about screen time.)

Once you have that frame, the catalogue lights up as variations on a single mouth:

- **The crowd as a mouth.** [[Bad Feeling (Oompa Loompa)]] renders the same appetite as a buffet, one track further along the album: *"carnivores and cannibals, I want it on demand-able / mandible, jaw split."* The attention economy as appetite, people consumed as content, scandal, outrage, on demand. (The internal rhyme literally has the words eating each other. The joke is load-bearing.)
- **Attention as the meal.** [[Pay Attention (feat. little luna)]] personifies Attention as a deity that offers you everything you want in exchange for the one thing it actually eats: your awareness. The pun does the whole job, *"Everybody's gotta pay me / You gotta pay Attention."* The currency you spend and the resource it harvests are the *same substance.*
- **The web that eats the spider.** [[Online]] flips the obvious metaphor: *"Internet is like a web and we're the Spider's food."* You think you're the one browsing. You're the one being digested. And the system's *"Buy! Buy! Buy!"* turns, by homophone, into *"Bye! Bye! Bye!,"* purchase and farewell as the identical sound.
- **The parasocial bite.** [[Not Your Homie]] (*"Stick it in your eye, bury you alive"*) and the [[Bounce]] line that lands as *"you just love me for my lore"* (a word this guide is still verifying by ear) name the consumption that hits closest to home: the audience devours the mythology, the wound, the *content,* and never meets the person. Roy's own framing of *Not Your Homie* as *"the ultimate introverted extrovert anthem"* is the polite version. The bite is that the line is *accurate.*
- **The cheer-machine that eats the exit.** [[Great Time to Be Human]] hands you *"bloodsuckers with crocodile shoes"* throwing *"a party with diamonds and spoons,"* predator and consumer in one figure, death worn as status. And even leaving gets eaten: the *"goodbye"* is swallowed and reabsorbed by the refrain. *"The crooks and believers all sing the same song."* You cannot even quit the show. The show consumes your resignation letter.
- **The pitch you swallow and re-transmit.** [[City of Angels]] runs a pronoun rotation, *your* dream, *my* dream, *your* dream, as the city's sales pitch is aimed at him, swallowed (*"my"*), and re-aimed at the next walker. He doesn't escape the beautiful stranger. He *becomes* one. The promise (*"never gonna die… live forever in the bright lights, godlike"*) is the upload-immortality counterfeit, resurrection downgraded to taxidermy.
- **The sweet that clones you.** [[The Watchers]] welds consumption to the clone-fear: *"now they want candy / but I'm not Mandy Moore."* The crowd wants the sweet, edited, manufactured pop-commodity, and *to be consumed sweetly is to be cloned.*

What ties these together, and what keeps the concept honest, is the contrast pole. The Circus does not own the only mouth in the catalogue. There is also the mouth that *testifies,* Roy's song, Ballerina Boy's voice, the Song of Love (see [[The Mouth]]). [[Welcome to the Circus]] is brutal precisely because it packs three mouth-*failures* into one song, *"teeth falling out,"* *"screaming, but no words come out,"* *"shut your mouth,"* alongside the chew-you-up line. The body that should sing is the body being eaten.

## Where it appears

- **[[Welcome to the Circus]]:** the primary naming. *"I'll chew you up, spit you out"* (the line lands twice). The Ringmaster's fourth-wall snark, *"did you like the show?,"* asks the audience to applaud the harm they just consumed, and *"they love it when you lose control"* is the thesis: the spectacle is powered by the performer's collapse. *"Step right up, shut your mouth"* is the participation-and-suppression contract in five words. Released as a single Jan 16, 2026, track 4 of 15 on [[Lucius Lullaby (album)|Lucius Lullaby]] (March 27, 2026), writers Cody Tarpley, Edan Dover, Rachel Kanner, Roy English, vocals credited to Roy English *and* Sir Lucius.
- **[[I Like to Party]]:** *the cake before the Circus.* The 2021 video (dir. Kyle Vorbach) shows Roy force-fed cake by a jeweled, ringed hand from off-screen, escalating until it is smashed across his face and body, the consumption mechanism rendered *visually* roughly four and a half years before *Welcome to the Circus* names it lyrically, with Roy cast as the thing being eaten. The lyric writes the recipe: *"Devil's Food is a cake… bake a truth with a lie / then we sell 'em the cake,"* and buried in the same run, the part that says the quiet bit out loud, *"we don't want 'em awake."* The whole upbeat party-anthem surface is the camouflage, a clap-along banger about people who *"stay in and pretend that we're in the club."* Heartbreaking stuff, trapped and disarmed by light sweet melodies. The cheerfulness *is* the force-feeding. (*33*, 2022, track 4 of 10, the album's most-streamed.)
- **[[Bad Feeling (Oompa Loompa)]]:** on-demand cannibalism, the attention economy as a mouth. *Lucius Lullaby* track 5, originally a Dec 2023 single timed to the *Wonka* film. The nursery bounce is the chocolate over the dread, the one un-jingled rupture is the line *"Are you fucking serious?"* Roy's own modest gloss: *"a chocolate covered reminder to trust your gut."*
- **[[Pay Attention (feat. little luna)]]:** Attention as the consuming deity. *33* (2022), track 3. *"Everybody's gotta pay me / You gotta pay Attention."*
- **[[Online]]:** the spider's food, *"Buy! → Bye!"* The song is itself the sedation it diagnoses, an earworm about being metabolized by earworms, using the spectacle to dismantle the spectacle.
- **[[The Watchers]]:** the consumed-and-cloned self, *"they want candy."*
- **[[Great Time to Be Human]]:** bloodsuckers, the swallowed goodbye.
- **[[City of Angels]]:** swallowing the pitch, the upload-immortality counterfeit.
- **[[Good Day]]:** the earliest node, consumption-as-anesthetic.
- **[[Not Your Homie]]** / **[[Bounce]]:** parasocial consumption, the line that lands as *"love me for my lore."*
- **The manifesto cards** ([[The Manifesto Cards]]): the consumerism critique stamped on paratext since ~2020. The Happy Face card frames an infomercial parody: *"ARE YOU CONTENT? CALL NOW"* under a *"BROUGHT TO YOU BUY"* banner (with a ® and a *(TICK)…(TOCK)*), carrying a *"death of my generation"* payload in TV-pastiche register. And the Happy Face video flashes the literal words *"consume"* and *"BUY BUY BUY"* between frames, the pitch driven in faster than the eye can track.

> [!reading]- Content (adjective, noun, and sedative)
> One infomercial line holds the entire consumerism-versus-seeker critique. *Content* = happy, satisfied (the seeker's question). *Content* = media-to-be-consumed (*are you the content?*), which is exactly what the *"BROUGHT TO YOU BUY"* frame implies. *Content* = complacent, contained, pacified. The card asks all three at once and means all three: are you at peace, are you the product, and are you sitting still while it happens? Routes straight to [[The Algorithm]], Consumption, and [[The Circus#What it produces (the Spectacle)|the Spectacle]].

> [!reading]- Eating in Both Directions (the chorus is also the meal)
> *Bad Feeling* eats in both directions. V2 turns the audience into appetite, *"carnivores and cannibals… mandible, jaw split… gossip,"* but the Oompa-Loompas doing the singing are *themselves* consumed labor: imported workers paid in cacao beans, used as test subjects, one of them turned into a blueberry. The chorus that sings the warning is the factory's own product and its victim. The crowd that sings is the crowd that's eaten. (The chorus phrase *"Everything's Perfect Here"* later became the title of Roy's first gallery show and pop-up, Lauren Powell Projects, LA, opened Jan 20, 2024.) A counter-flag worth keeping: not every devouring image encodes Consumption. You can make everything either meaningful or derivative, and the candy-horror wordplay may just be vivid candy-horror wordplay, with Roy's own read the modest *"trust your gut."* Hold the systems-critique and the gut-feeling at once. The gap between them is itself a wink.

> [!props]- Let Them Eat Cronus
> The official *I Like To Party* cover art plates a cake as **Saturn / Cronus the devourer,** the god who eats his own children, ringed by a circle of skulls (*memento mori*). The tagline reads *"A NEW DIRECTION AFFECTING THE STATE OF YOUR MIND"* in flat infomercial register. This is the decoder for the video: the force-feeding is not random cruelty, it is the *devouring-parent / time-the-devourer* archetype made literal. A birthday cake plated as the child-eating god and garnished with skulls is also, deadpan, the best one-line summary of the whole concept: *the party that eats you.*

> [!map]- Jonah Came Back Different (the round-trip versus the meal)
> This is the interpretive spine of the page, and it works as a *framework comparison,* something the songs invite, not something they assert. The biblical tradition is full of being *swallowed:* **Jonah** (Jonah 1–2), taken down into the dark for three days and returned *changed,* the **Eucharist** (*"this is my body, broken for you,"* Luke 22:19), a consumption that is voluntary and nourishing. Those are descent-and-return structures: you go in, and you come back transformed. The Circus uses the same verbs and reverses the outcome. *"I'll chew you up, spit you out"* is the **anti-Eucharist:** the Christ-speech *form* (this is my body) loaded with predatory *content* (chew, spit). Predatory consumption, ejection without nourishment, the body broken *for nothing.*
>
> | The descent-and-return (Jonah) | The Circus |
> |---|---|
> | Swallowed whole | Chewed, active mastication |
> | Three days in darkness | No pause, immediate ejection |
> | Prayer from inside the belly | No prayer noted |
> | Returned to the surface | Spit out |
> | Returns transformed | Returns as waste |
> | Obeys his mission after | Discarded, show over |
>
> The two rows people skip are the load-bearing ones: the **prayer in the belly** and the **obedience after.** Jonah's descent works because something happens *in* the dark and *changes him on the way out.* You can't come up unless you go down first, but the going-down has to do something to you. The Circus offers neither, no pause to pray, no self to send back on a mission. That is the difference between an initiation and a meal.

> [!lore]- A Brief History of Getting Eaten
> The image of consumption-as-adversary runs deep and wide:
> - **Sheol as a mouth:** *"Sheol enlarges its appetite and opens its mouth beyond measure"* (Isaiah 5:14), the underworld as the consuming void.
> - **The roaring lion:** *"the devil, as a roaring lion, walketh about, seeking whom he may devour"* (1 Peter 5:8), predation as the adversary's mode.
> - **The beasts that consume kingdoms:** Daniel 7 / Revelation 13, empire itself as a consumption-system.
> - **Moloch:** the god children were sacrificed to (2 Kings 23:10, Jeremiah 7:31), consumption as false-god worship. Allen Ginsberg's *Howl* revives Moloch for industrial capitalism, the machine-god fed on the living.
> - **The Walrus and the Carpenter** (a literary lens, not a claim of intent): Lewis Carroll's two hosts weep over the oysters, sorting them and sympathizing and dabbing their eyes, while eating every last one. Predation dressed as hospitality, the tears folded into the meal. It rhymes twice: the cheerfulness that *is* the force-feeding, and the fact that it comes from *Through the Looking-Glass,* the mirror-world this whole project already lives inside.
> - **The shells (the qliphoth):** in the Lurianic telling the vessels break and become the shells, and the hunger is the memory of a relationship to the light that was lost. Once they received and transmitted, now they only protect, and the protecting runs through capture, imprisonment, and consumption. A husk eats because it is empty. When you have no light of your own, all you can do is take in someone else's. The Circus reads as exactly that kind of husk-system, a mouth where a maker should be. It has a public anchor, too: the Hall of Mirrors artifact [[Artifacts - Gallery 3 (75-112)#84 · David Star — "Where The World Was Crossed"|David Star, "Where The World Was Crossed"]] (code **TWISTED LADDER**), a bedtime-story cosmology that sinks the Kings and Queens of Time as sparks under the shells before the long climb back. The songs invite the reading, they do not assert it, but shell-as-hunger is the cleanest name for the sterile, generate-nothing quality the whole page circles.
> - **Capital / commodity:** Marx's labor consumed and expelled as surplus value, the worker chewed up and spat out with minimal return. The Circus is that factory floor with better lighting.
> - **The Roman arena:** *Panem et Circenses* (Juvenal, *Satires* X, c.100 CE), the crowd sedated not by force but by pleasure, and the literal Christian martyr consumed by the entertainment apparatus for the spectators' enjoyment. The chains are fun. See [[The Circus#Where it comes from (bread and circuses)|Panem et Circenses]].

> [!reading]- The God You're Already Paying
> *Pay Attention* is the attention-consumption prototype, and the structure is exact: Attention is the hidden god of the modern world, and what it consumes is *awareness,* the very faculty you would need to notice it eating you. The currency you *spend* (*"pay me"*) and the resource it *extracts* (*"pay Attention"*) are one substance, which is why the temptation works through consent: the mark already wants what is offered. The grounded vocal roles read Roy as **Attention** and little luna as the guide/introducer. And the working method is on the record: Roy's *33* board, in his own hand, asks *"How to anthropomorphize attention? → Marduk,"* personifying Attention-as-deity (after the Babylonian creator who orders the cosmos by *word*) as an explicit move, not a critic's projection. (The same board's *"Let Them Eat Cake"* tracklist line was later realized as the Hall of Mirrors artifact *"Caution, All Kings End,"* code **CAKE,** tying the board back to *I Like to Party*'s cake.)

> [!map]- All Rot, No Gold
> In alchemy the **nigredo** (the blackening, the rot, the dissolution) is the *first* stage. The prima materia is broken down *in order to* be reconstituted as gold. The Circus specializes in the blackening that never completes: prima materia endlessly processed, exhausted, and never reconstituted. *"I'll chew you up, spit you out"* is dissolution with the gold-step amputated, Jonah digested but not returned, the Eucharist inverted, nigredo arrested mid-rot. Consumption *without* transformation is the whole signature. See [[Nigredo]].

> [!flame]- Two Fires (only one leaves ash)
> Two fires sit at the edge of this concept. *"Our God is a consuming fire"* (Hebrews 12:29), the same fire that purifies *also* consumes whatever cannot endure it, so consumption is not always the adversary's, sometimes it is the refiner's. Against it, the burning bush of Exodus 3 *that is not consumed,* presence that radiates without being used up. The Circus's fire only ever takes the first form and never the second: it burns, and there is nothing left. See [[Fire]] and [[Moths to the Flame]], where the moth gets the death without the rebirth, the counterfeit of the phoenix.

> [!reading]- It's Not a Poster About Your Phone
> Consumption is the Circus's mode of a much larger split, Source versus Reflection, Machine versus Human. The antagonist is **optimization severed from the heart,** not machinery and not appetite as such. A machine subordinate to the heart is creation. The same machine cut loose becomes the Algorithm that consumes. This matters because it stops the concept from flattening into generic anti-consumerism: the project is not against *eating,* or *attention,* or even *the internet.* It is against the moment the tool stops serving feeling and starts feeding on it. Lose this guardrail and Consumption becomes a poster about putting your phone down. Keep it and it stays a diagnosis of a *severing.* And the fix is never "kill the machine." Harmony comes from adjusting the degree, not eliminating one side: mind serving heart, machine under the maker, the polarity held in order instead of one pole running the whole house. See [[Machine vs Human]].

And see the open questions below on *who is actually doing the eating.* Certainty is just a tone, not a truth, and if this guide ever sounds sure it is doing it wrong.

> [!question]- Still Chewing On These
> - **Who is the actual consumer?** The song pins the eating on Lucius-as-ringmaster, and he believes he is the top of the chain (the self-crowned *"final boss,"* answering to no one). Whether he actually *is* the top is the real question: the appetite may run *through* him to the industry, capital, or the Algorithm itself, the pattern he serves without admitting it. A husk eats because it is empty, and the emptiest mouths are rarely the ones on stage. ([[BMRC]] is his own corporate signature, now decoded from site metadata as the *Black Magic Rainbow Circus.*) The consumption may be further upstream than it looks.
> - **Is being chewed up the *start* of something?** Is there a transformation-through-consumption reading the *Welcome to the Circus* timeframe simply has not reached yet, is this the Jonah-moment *before* the prayer in the belly?
> - **Diagnosis without exit?** Juvenal diagnosed *panem et circenses* and prescribed nothing. Is the project the same, naming the Circus without naming a way out, or is there a way to *bear witness within* it rather than escape or be eaten? ([[Down to You]]'s *"bread alone"* is the cleanest candidate for a built-in answer.)
> - **Audience: failure or feature?** Is the crowd's love of watching you lose control a *moral failure* of the audience, or a *designed feature* of a system the audience cannot be blamed for?
> - **What can't be metabolized?** Is there a song built to *fail* by the Algorithm's metrics, anti-consumption by design? And the standing puzzle: why can't Lucius absorb the Song of Love? In the theatrical Roy v Lucius series he has Roy hauled out when it plays, then sits alone and lets it finish, unsettled. So the question forks. Is the song something his appetite genuinely cannot metabolize, an object that will not go down, or is it something he *remembers* and cannot admit, indigestion versus grief? An unconsumable object would be the exact shape of the answer, and so, quietly, would a mouth that stops chewing long enough to listen.
