# Pronoun Audit (I-You) — Independent Review

> [!review] Review status
> **Verdict:** Targeted revision  
> **Scope:** Compared against the live page, the Opus lift, lyric counts and attribution methods, and the Speaker Problem, Voice, Audience, and character pages.

## What the rewrite improves

The rewrite gives the vault a reproducible method for noticing changes in “I,” “you,” “we,” “they,” and impersonal voice across songs and eras. Quantitative attention can expose patterns that impressionistic reading misses, especially changes in intimacy, command, blame, identification, and collective address.

Its strongest contribution is functional reading: pronouns do not merely count participants; they organize relation. A “you” may accuse, seduce, split, mirror, command, or become the self addressed as other.

## What is grounded

Pronoun counts can be grounded when based on verified lyrics and a transparent counting method. Changes in grammatical person are directly observable.

Speaker identity, character attribution, and psychological meaning are interpretations built from those counts.

## Strong readings worth preserving

Keep the “shrinking you” and changing-address analyses where the song-level evidence supports them. Keep catalogue-wide tendencies as hypotheses that can be tested against counterexamples.

The audit should retain separate fields for performer, credited feature, quoted speech, crowd response, sample, and uncertain voice.

## Required corrections

Counts must not imply stable speaker identity. “I” can change speakers within a song; “you” can address several targets; “we” can be inclusive, coercive, ironic, or quoted.

Separate catalogue-wide patterns from local song readings. A global tendency should not override contradictory context in one track.

Document lyric source, handling of repeats, ad-libs, overlapping vocals, quoted dialogue, and uncertain transcription.

Do not use pronoun frequency alone to classify a song as Roy-voiced or Lucius-voiced.

## Missing nuance

Production can change pronoun function. A line whispered, chanted by children, echoed by a crowd, or distorted through character effects may occupy a different position from the same words on paper.

Absence of pronouns can also matter, especially in slogans, imperatives, wordless passages, or depersonalized system speech.

## Cross-page implications

- **The Speaker Problem:** governing attribution discipline.
- **The Voice:** performer and metaphysical source are distinct questions.
- **The Audience:** “we” and crowd address require mode analysis.
- **Lucius/Roy pages:** pronouns are evidence, not verdict.

## Exact revision instruction

Keep the reproducible audit and functional categories. Add a methods box explaining source text, counting rules, repeats, voice uncertainty, and limits. Revise any identity claims derived from counts alone.

Recommended thesis:

> **One-line reading.** Pronoun patterns can reveal how Jagwar Twin organizes relation, but they cannot settle who speaks without vocal, narrative, production, and character context.

## Final assessment

The page is valuable but requires methodological tightening before it should govern catalogue-wide claims.
