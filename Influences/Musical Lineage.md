# Musical Lineage

> **One-line reading.** At the far end of Roy's own thread stand the artists his myth says *"completed the circle"* — and the honest news is that the four of them lend the work four very different things. One is a signed collaborator. One is a ghost in a borrowed hook. Two are heroes named out loud, which buys a lens and not a decoder ring.

[[The Circle]] closes *33* with a small cosmology and one throwaway roll-call: the ones who remembered the song and sang it back — *"a Jesus, a Bowie, a Prince."* On the album that shares Christ's age, that line is doing quiet theological work, and it tells you exactly who Roy files at the top of his lineage: not a reading list, a **line**. This page keeps the *musicians* — Joni Mitchell, Michael Jackson, David Bowie, Prince — and, more importantly, keeps them honest. Naming a hero licenses a way of hearing; it does not turn a resemblance into a receipt. (For that rule in full, and the affectionate house motto *not all resemblance is reference,* see [[Inherited Substrate]].)

One name from that roll-call is deliberately missing here. Jesus isn't a musician, and the tradition he anchors lives in its own pages rather than in a lineup of records. So four artists, four honest paragraphs, in descending order of how much you can actually prove.

## At a glance

- **In one line** — the four musicians at the end of Roy's thread, ranked by how solid the tie really is: Joni (a real credit), Michael Jackson (a real interpolation), Bowie and Prince (named heroes plus a reading).
- **Where it shows up** — [[Soul Is A Star]] (Joni's co-write) · [[The Watchers]] (the Rockwell / Michael Jackson hook) · [[The Circle]] (the *"a Jesus, a Bowie, a Prince"* line that frames the whole thing) · [[Sir Lucius]] (the Bowie-shaped mask) · [[Moths to the Flame]] (the Prince thread, thinner than it looks).
- **Connected ideas** — [[Inherited Substrate]] · [[Roy on Influences]] · [[The Circle]].
- **The order of confidence** — Joni is documented. Michael Jackson is documented, once removed. Bowie and Prince are named exemplars wearing an interpretive coat. The page never lets the strong end vouch for the soft end.

## Joni Mitchell — the one that's actually on the record

Start with the strongest tie in the entire catalogue, because it sets the bar the other three have to clear (and mostly don't).

Joni Mitchell is a **credited co-writer** on [[Soul Is A Star]], track five of *33*. This isn't a fan's ear-read or a clever weld built out of two rhyming lyrics — Roy has said it in his own words, and he still sounds a little stunned about it: *"the fact that JONI MITCHELL gave me her blessing and is a cowriter is still mind blowing."* A blessing *and* a credit, from the artist herself. That is as grounded as a musical influence ever gets in this project: not an homage pointed at a hero across a canyon, but the hero's name in the songwriting credits and her permission in Roy's mouth.

There is a whole ecosystem under that one fact — the "Woodstock" weld, the transmission thesis, the far more slippery question of whether Roy positions himself as a *prophet* in her line — and it deserves the long form, not a cameo. So this section does the disciplined thing and keeps its hands off. The tie is real; the tie is huge; the analysis lives where it belongs.

**Read the full story:** [[Joni and the Prophet Lineage]] — the co-write, the blessing, and the careful firewall between what's on the record and what's just tempting.

## Michael Jackson — the ghost in the hook

Michael Jackson's presence in the work is real, but it arrives *through* another record, which is a nice bit of pop-archaeology on Roy's part.

[[The Watchers]] carries a credited interpolation of Rockwell's 1984 paranoia-anthem *"Somebody's Watching Me"* — that hook, *"I always feel like somebody's watching me,"* is one of the most recognizable in pop, and the reason is Michael Jackson. He sang it. **Uncredited.** Rockwell was Kennedy Gordy, Berry Gordy's son, working under a stage name partly to escape the shadow of being a Motown scion's kid; the biggest thing on his biggest song is the unlabeled voice of the most famous man alive. So when Jagwar Twin borrows that hook, he's borrowing an object with a ghost already sealed inside it — the icon of pop-surveillance, the voice everyone knows and nobody's crediting, standing exactly where a song about being watched, copied, and unsure whose voice is whose would want him. Roy even waves at him inside the lyric: the crowd, he says, is *"dancing like Michael Jackson."*

It gets better the longer you look, and this is where the pick starts to feel deliberate rather than lucky. Rockwell wrote *"Somebody's Watching Me"* out of genuine paranoia in a famous father's house — Berry Gordy dismissed the demo, then took the credit; the son buried his own surname to be heard as himself and not as a Motown copy. That is uncannily close to the machinery of this project: a buried name (English becoming Jagwar Twin), a self split into a persona to survive being watched, and the question of *whose voice is this* sitting under everything. Michael Jackson's uncredited hook is the neatest possible emblem of it — the other voice inside your own song, the one nobody labels. Roy didn't have to reach for a surveillance record with a buried-son parable and a ghost-voice baked in. He reached for that one.

Two honest calibrations, so the enthusiasm doesn't run off with the facts. The interpolation is documented and load-bearing — that part is solid. But whether the buried-Jackson-voice reading is *the point* or a happy accident is a question the song leaves open, and a genuinely elegant coincidence would look identical from here. And this is fundamentally participation in an older record, the same method that runs through the whole catalogue, sitting alongside Joni, Johnny Mercer, Norman Rockwell's namesake and the rest of the borrowed songbook (see [[Inherited Substrate]]). Michael Jackson is really here. He's just here as a haunting, not a handshake.

## David Bowie — the mask-maker

Now the ground softens, and the page says so plainly.

Bowie earns his spot the moment Roy names him — he's the *"Bowie"* in *"a Jesus, a Bowie, a Prince,"* filed among the artists who completed the circle. That's grounded: Roy put him on the list. What Bowie *lends* the work, though, is not a credit or a sample but a **template**, and template-talk is interpretation, so hear it as interpretation. Bowie is the patron saint of the pop self that splits into named characters — Ziggy Stardust, the Thin White Duke, a wardrobe of alter-egos worn like weather. Once someone has made that move canonical, the shape *"Jagwar Twin & Sir Lucius"* reads as standing squarely in his lineage: the artist who is also, officially, a character; the self that fractures into a name you can put on a marquee and blame for the bad parts.

What makes the lens fit so snugly is the *function* the mask performs, which is the same on both sides. Bowie's characters gave him somewhere to put the parts of himself that were too much, too strange, or too exposed to sign his own name to — a self you can send out onto the stage and, when it turns monstrous, hold at arm's length. That's the exact job [[Sir Lucius]] does here: the crowd builds the spectacle and then condemns the performer for it, and a named character is precisely the thing that lets you carry a shadow in public and still say *that wasn't me, that was him.* Bowie is the pop precedent for making the split official — for treating the mask not as a disguise but as a working part of the art.

Still, a lens is a lens, and the page won't dress it up as more. There's no Bowie song in the credits, no sample cleared and buried, no lyric that reproduces his. What there is: Roy naming him as an exemplar, plus the plain observation that the persona-as-armor apparatus of [[Sir Lucius]] rhymes with the most famous persona-machine in rock. So this is a **resonance grounded by a name-check** — which is a real thing, and a smaller thing than the two sections above it. Bowie hands the project a way of understanding its own masks. He does not decode a single track. See [[Sir Lucius]] for the mask itself, and [[The Circle]] for the line that puts Bowie in the lineup.

## Prince — the completed-circle exemplar (and a thread not to pull too hard)

Prince is the third name in the roll-call — the *"Prince"* of *"a Jesus, a Bowie, a Prince"* — and there the solid ground mostly ends, which is worth being upfront about, because there's a tempting thread nearby that doesn't hold the weight people want to hang on it.

Near [[Moths to the Flame]], the phrase *"I would die 4 U"* floats in the air, and it is impossible to hear those exact words without thinking of Prince's 1984 track. Tempting. But this one has to be said flatly: that's Prince's line, not a Jagwar Twin lyric — the resemblance is thematic weather, not a credit, not an interpolation, not a documented borrowing. The vault's own working notes retired it for exactly that reason. So we don't inflate a shared phrase into a citation. What survives is the honest part: Roy names Prince among the ones who completed the circle, which makes him an **exemplar** — a figure the myth points to as having pulled the same thread all the way through — rather than a source you can trace line by line.

That's genuinely meaningful in Roy's cosmology, where completing the circle is the whole aspiration of [[The Circle]]: remember the song that was hidden in your wings and sing it back out. Prince is one of the people the myth says *did that.* It's just a myth-level tribute, not a songwriting debt, and the page keeps those two things apart. Named hero: yes. Documented influence on a specific track: not established. See [[The Circle]] for the exemplar list, and [[Moths to the Flame]] for the thread we're deliberately not overselling.

## The honest clause

Here's the throughline, stated once so no reader walks away over-reading it. Roy names his heroes out loud — which is generous, and rare, and the reason this page can exist at all. But a named hero **licenses a lens; it does not decode a song.** Joni is a credit. Michael Jackson is an interpolation. Bowie and Prince are names on a list plus a way of hearing — and hearing a resemblance is not the same as proving a reference. Not everything that rhymes was placed there on purpose, and saying so out loud is the discipline that lets the real ties (Joni, the Rockwell hook) keep their full weight instead of getting diluted by every echo we'd *like* to be true. The brake on all of it, affectionately known in-house as the page that stops the inflation, is [[Inherited Substrate]].

Four artists at the end of one thread. One you can sue over, one you can hear the ghost of, and two you can only stand near — and the guide's job is to tell you honestly which is which.
