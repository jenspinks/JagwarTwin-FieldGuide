# The Crowd

> **One-line reading.** The Crowd is the collective made audible: the same gathered voices that can rise into communion or curdle into spectacle, depending entirely on how they're sung. A unison is not a communion.

## Start here

Every other figure in this world is a private weather system: a shame-voice, a ruling mind, a mask worn in a mirror. The Crowd is the one that lives out here, in the room, in the feed, in the comment section. And it is the only one you are already standing inside.

That is what makes it the strangest figure in the catalogue. It is the single one that can be the best thing in the room or the worst thing in the room, and the songs will not tell you which until you hear *how* it is singing.

A crowd returning a song in its own thousand distinct voices is the holiest image the project has.

The very same crowd, ordered to scream on cue, is its most damning.

Same people. Same singing. Opposite poles. The Crowd is what is at stake every time the music asks you to join in, which is exactly why it implicates you and not some abstraction. When [[Roy]] refuses the word "fan" and calls his listeners *"co-creators of the world,"* he is making a claim about you specifically: that the same people who could be marks are also makers, and what separates them is not who they are but how they arrive.

It is also, conveniently, the funniest place in the whole project, because the machinery of mass attention is genuinely absurd, and [[Sir Lucius]] is more than happy to narrate it.

## Where the Crowd sits

The Crowd has two poles, and they wear the same face.

**Communion** is many *distinct* voices singing truth together, difference held inside unity. This is [[The Loop and the Circle|the Circle]]'s chorus: *"the crowd sings it back,"* voices that remember and return the song, themselves even in harmony.

**Spectacle** is many voices flattened into the *same* song, difference deleted. This is [[The Circus]]'s crowd: synchronized, not awakened, measured by reach and conformity instead of by return.

Here is the load-bearing line, and the cleanest key to the whole figure: **a unison is not a communion.** Both look like a crowd singing together. That is exactly the problem. If you read every collective-singing image in the catalogue as sacred, you would be wrong about half of them.

So the test is a single question. **Is difference held, or deleted?** Held, communion. Deleted, spectacle. The people never change. Only the direction does. The Crowd is never neutral as a verb; it is only ever neutral as a noun. The fuller treatment lives at [[Communion vs Spectacle]]; what matters here is that the Crowd is the body that distinction is *about*.

Which is also how it sorts the cast. [[Roy]] sings *with* the crowd. [[Sir Lucius]] sings *at* it. The Circle receives voices; the Circus extracts them. One gathering, pointed two ways.

## Meet the Crowd here

The Crowd's signature move across the catalogue is the **echo**: it takes a phrase and sends it back. It is one of Jagwar Twin's clearest participatory devices, and it is also where the two poles split most visibly, because the direction the echo runs tracks *who is holding the mic.*

When [[Roy]] voices a song, the crowd tends to sing *with* him, distinct voices returning the song, a communion-echo:

- [[Long Time Coming]]: the album opens on a crowd *already* singing (*"they're singing…"*). You arrive mid-chorus.
- [[Loser]]: the bridge turns *I* into *we*, *"we're singing."*
- [[Superhuman]]: *"it goes, we sing / raise up your voices."*
- [[Dream - Dream|Dream/Dream]]: *"dream that dream with me."*
- [[Shine]]: *"so sing it if you know you're gonna shine."*
- [[Imagine A World]]: *"just imagine,"* the line left open for you to finish.

When [[Sir Lucius]] voices a song, the crowd tends to be *ordered, synchronized, or reduced to noise*, a commanded and harvested echo:

- [[I Like to Party]]: the Pied Piper, *"follow me… sing along… to the beat of my drum,"* and *"one, two, three, four."*
- [[Pay Attention (feat. little luna)]]: *"what's my name? / Attention."* To answer is to pay.
- [[Welcome to the Circus]]: *"take your seat, scream and shout."* The crowd scripted as *noise*, not song.
- [[Bananas]]: the id-permission chant, *"do you ever wanna break shit?" → "well, alright."*
- [[Not Your Homie]]: *ring-around-the-rosie* weaponized into a plague-march.
- [[Great Time to Be Human]]: *"all sing the same song."*
- [[City of Angels]]: an echo-economy with no communion left in it.

One caution belongs here, because call-and-response is endemic to pop and the mere *presence* of sing-along lines proves nothing. The claim survives only on the **directional split by voice**, who sings *with* the crowd versus who commands it, never on the fact of a crowd. That is also exactly what would break it: a clearly Roy-voiced song scripting the crowd as consumable noise, or a clearly Lucius-voiced one genuinely inviting distinct-voiced return, would falsify the pattern. So far the split holds.

## The thesis in the lyrics

<img src="https://plumcreekranch.com/jagwar/GTTBH.jpeg" style="float: left; width: 150px; margin-right: 16px; margin-bottom: 8px;">

For a long time this reading rested on staging and interviews: how a room was lit, how Roy described a live show. Then one line on [[Great Time to Be Human]] put it inside a sung text.

> "the crooks and believers all sing the same song"

Crook and believer are *supposed* to be opposites. The entire moral order depends on telling them apart. The spectacle dissolves the difference, and under the cheer the con man and the true believer end up in the same choir, and neither one notices, because what counts is not what you mean but that you are singing along. That is the spectacle pole stated plainly, in the music, and [[Communion vs Spectacle]] was seeded from this exact line.

Its visual twin is on [[City of Angels]]: *"everyone looks just like you."* The same collapse, moved from the moral register into the visual one. Sameness offered as the forgery of oneness: *"we are all one"* curdled into *"you are all interchangeable."* One operation, two registers.

## The ringmaster's crowd

<img src="https://plumcreekranch.com/jagwar/HappyFacePoster.jpeg" style="float: right; width: 150px; margin-left: 16px; margin-bottom: 8px;">

The spectacle pole gets its fullest performance in [[Welcome to the Circus]], where Lucius steps to the front and works the room with a showman's contempt. The instruction is the collapse itself: *"take your seat, scream and shout / 'cause I'm the one they're talking 'bout."* In one line the co-creator is seated, handed a role, and converted into ambient noise around the star. The crowd is not gathered here. It is *processed.*

The operating principle gets stated without apology:

> "they love it when you lose control"

That is not a complaint about audiences. It is the marquee. The system is not hiding that it runs on your breakdown; it is printing it on the sign out front, cheerfully. Then the fourth wall does not break so much as it asks for a tip: *"when the curtains close, did you like the show?"* The ringmaster turns to the camera and asks the crowd to rate its own consumption. The loop closes with *"I'll chew you up, spit you out,"* the Circus as a digestive system that consumes audience and performer alike.

Here is the twist that makes the Crowd load-bearing: it is why Lucius exists at all. He is the most audience-dependent figure in the project. Without watchers to play to, he does not cohere. **No audience, no Lucius.** The Crowd is his power source, which means his contempt and his dependency share one address. *"You don't know me,"* screamed at the exact crowd buying the merch, is the whole parasocial joke compressed to four words.

The single richest crowd-image sits in [[Not Your Homie]], and it is an inversion so clean it is almost a thesis. The song's spine is a playground rhyme, *"ringing rosies, my pocket's full of posies… ashes, ashes, we all fall down,"* lifted from *"Ring a Ring o' Roses,"* which is to say from the most communal chant-form there is: a children's *circle-game*, the literal shape of [[The Loop and the Circle|the Circle]], hands joined, everybody in. Lucius hands that gesture to the plague-voice and marches it out, *"coming in like a plague from the dark side."* The Circle's own communal gesture becomes the vehicle of contagion. Take the most communion-shaped thing you have, and weaponize it.

The plague-voice crowns itself, too, and the boast is the whole pathology in one line: *"might run for president, I'm so presidential."* The appetite for the room scales straight to the top office, and the comedy is that he is not wrong about the mechanism, only shameless about it. Mass attention and political power are the same harvest; *"so presidential"* just says the quiet part with a grin.

Fittingly, the most engineered piece of crowd-manipulation on the record was not a thesis. It was a joke. Roy's collaborator MERCY sent him a voice note singing the chorus after getting back from a party, and Roy knew immediately it had to be a Lucius song, calling it *"the ultimate introverted extrovert anthem."* The shallowness is the point, and it is funny on purpose.

## Co-creator versus spectator

This is the figure's moral center, and the project keeps it open.

The same listeners are both the *co-creators*, Roy's deliberate word, chosen over "fan," and the *marks*, the ones the Circus performs for and processes. What separates them is not who they are; it is how they arrive: as people doing their own interior work, or as objects consuming someone else's spectacle. [[The Circus]] exists to collapse the first into the second, and *"take your seat, scream and shout"* is that collapse performed on the listener in real time.

Roy's stated position raises the stakes past entertainment. The quality of consciousness a listener brings shapes the shared reality the work belongs to; in his framing, *the world you build inside your own head is the world everyone then has to live in.* That is a transfer of responsibility, not a flattery. It asks something of the Crowd instead of offering it a show. The whole arc can be read as the recovery of the co-creator *from* the spectator: not escaping the Circus, but refusing to be reduced to *audience* inside it.

Which leaves the question the page should hold rather than resolve. Is the crowd's love of watching you lose control a **moral failure** of the crowd, or a **designed feature** of the system the crowd cannot be blamed for? The honest answer is that the project diagnoses both and convicts neither. That ambiguity is the ethical fulcrum of the whole figure, and it is why the Crowd is a *force*, not a villain.

## The comedy of the herd

The attention-economy comedy is not incidental to this figure. It is the texture.

[[I Like to Party]] (the most-streamed song on *33*, around 12.6 million plays) is a party anthem for people who *"don't go outside / stay in and pretend that we're in the club,"* a crowd *"running around like chickens with no head / in circles, they go,"* the Circle inverted into pure dissipation. *"Give me attention, attention / the people are mine"* is the Pied Piper saying the quiet part: the crowd herded, not gathered, counted in like a drumline. Everyone is at the party. Nobody is at the party.

[[Bananas]] runs the same move in a nursery key: the cradle-rocker as a corrupted children's-TV host green-lighting arson. *"Hey kids, do you ever wanna break shit?… well, alright,"* and the crowd echoes *destruction* back. (Roy's public gloss on the fruit sharpens it: *"most bananas are sterile and cannot reproduce by seed ;)"* ([[Public Posts]]). The Cavendish is a clone, all identical, all wiped out together precisely *because* identical. A monoculture you can chant.)

[[Great Time to Be Human]] is the darkest joke of the set, because its ad-libs are children's-show cue-buttons fired at exactly the wrong moments: a brother is smothered → *"oops!"*; kids dance to destroy → *"yay!"*; a made-god still barks → *"woof!"*. It is horror with a laugh-track, the algorithm laughing at the boy who lost his mother. The Crowd is funniest precisely where it is being trained not to feel, which is the whole indictment, delivered as a gag.

And [[City of Angels]] runs the bleakest version through the crowd's own reward system. The prize the Spectacle dangles is the Walk of Fame, your name in a star on the sidewalk, immortality you can step on, under a *never-gonna-die* survival-chant carried in from the track before. The punchline is what that deathlessness actually *is*: not resurrection but **taxidermy**. A phoenix burns and comes back genuinely new; taxidermy keeps the *shape* and takes the life out, a thing that looks alive only because something cleaned it out and propped it up. That is the celebrity the Crowd is taught to want: preserved, postured, hollow. And it rhymes exactly with *"everyone looks just like you,"* because a Walk of Fame is a row of interchangeable stars and taxidermy is sameness with the breath taken out.

## Relationships

- **[[The Audience]]:** the closest sibling, near a synonym. The Audience is the *seated* form of this same force; the Crowd is the broader figure that can still tip either way. The Crowd is the gathering; the Audience is the gathering once it has been seated and told to scream.
- **[[Communion vs Spectacle]]:** the keystone the whole figure runs on, the conceptual engine: *a unison is not a communion.*
- **[[The Circus]]:** the Crowd's habitat at the spectacle pole, the structure built to collapse co-creator into spectator. The Crowd is the Circus's fuel *and* its product.
- **[[The Loop and the Circle|The Circle]]:** the communion pole. *"The crowd sings it back"* is its signature gesture. The Circle *receives* voices; the Circus *extracts* them. Same singing crowd, opposite direction.
- **[[Roy]]:** voices the communion-echo, the crowd sings *with* him; author of the co-creator doctrine that rejects "fan."
- **[[Sir Lucius]]:** the most audience-dependent figure; voices the commanded echo (*"scream and shout"*); the ringmaster who collapses the crowd. *No audience, no Lucius.*
- **[[The Circus#What it produces (the Spectacle)|the Spectacle]]:** the broader condition the Crowd lives inside; it needs spectators to exist at all.
- **[[Witnessing vs Surveillance]]:** the sibling axis on the *being-seen* side. Communion and spectacle are what happen when the Crowd *sings*; witnessing and surveillance are what happen when it *watches*. To be witnessed is to be seen and held; to be surveilled is to be seen and *captured*. The Circus's *"did you like the show?"* sits right on that hinge: it asks to be witnessed and gets you surveilled.
- **[[The Realm|The System]]:** the apparatus, industry plus [[The Algorithm|algorithm]], that engineers and harvests the Crowd.
- **[[Mary]]:** the faceless source a communion might sing *toward*; her counterfeit is the *"beautiful stranger"* the Crowd gets pitched in [[City of Angels]].

### The deeper cuts

Everything above stands on its own. What follows is optional: the mechanics, the named influences, the framework held loosely, the guards that keep the page honest, and the open questions. Open what you want. Ignore all of it and nothing breaks.

> [!note]- The device runs deeper than the live room
> **The *Superhuman* wrinkle.** [[Superhuman]] (Subject to Flooding, 2018) looks at first like a counterexample: it is *command-cadenced*, *"left, right, left,"* yet unmistakably **we**-voiced. The commander sings *with* the crowd, not *at* it. That is not a break in the split; it is the Roy-pole borrowing a martial form for communion, which is exactly what separates it from Lucius's *"scream and shout."* The same drill-sergeant grammar can serve either pole, and the tell is whether the leader is inside the chorus or barking at it. Far from violating the rule, *Superhuman* shows why the rule has to be about *direction* and not surface form.
>
> **The production layer carries its own crowd.** The records do not wait for a live audience to manifest the Crowd; they build one into the mix. The official lyric texts are saturated with parenthetical background-echo: *(it's been a long time coming)*, *(death of my generation)*, *(watching me)*, *(pay attention)*, *(in a loop)*, *(side quest)*, *(bounce)*. The recording echoes its own claim back at itself, a built-in crowd at the production level. The dark pole of this is literalized in [[The Circle (Lucius Version)]], where Roy and child voices are *aligned to sound like one voice unless you listen closely*: unison-not-communion, engineered as a production fact. It is the [[Communion vs Spectacle]] thesis rendered at the level of the master.
>
> **The live event (2026).** The sing-back device was finally performed live: the BMRC underground event (announced on X) was staged with production specifically recording the *crowd's* voices, and the first public footage-artifact is the *"bounce (Live Underground)"* reel, captioned *"who's coming to the next one?"* Even the title card joins in, *"(bounce) BROUGHT TO YOU BY JAGWAR TWIN (bounce)"* reproducing the catalogue's parenthetical-echo device in *typography*. The crucial nuance: this is **not counterfeit communion.** The singing is genuine. What the footage captures is *real* communion **mis-directed**, true collective voice aimed at the reflection-pole (the show, the brand) rather than at the source. Genuine-but-misdirected, versus fake: that distinction is the whole point, and it is easy to get wrong.

> [!note]- One flattening, wearing three faces
> *"All sing the same song"* ([[Great Time to Be Human]]) is the collapse in the **moral** register; *"everyone looks just like you"* ([[City of Angels]]) is the same collapse in the **visual** one; the synchronized live crowd is the **sonic** one. One operation appearing three places at once: difference deleted. A circle requires distinct points held in communion, and a circle with no distinct points collapses into a single point repeating, which is the **Loop** (see [[The Loop and the Circle]], [[Flattened Identity]]). In a project named Jagwar *Twin*, built on the productive two and the mirror that *wakes*, sameness is the nightmare version of the figure: twinning gone metastatic, infinite doubling with no *other* left to meet.
>
> Held as resonance, not a claim the songs assert: *communion* in the religious sense is a body made of distinct members who remain themselves, many grains, one bread; many voices, one song, none erased. Its counterfeit is the crowd whose members are made interchangeable, the unison that *erases* rather than gathers. On that correspondence, the project's crowd-images sort into the real and forged versions of the same longing for oneness.
>
> A framework the project's shape supports, held at resonance level: Babel (Genesis 11) is the scriptural name for *one language collapsing into nonsense*, *"one language and the same words,"* then confusion, then scattering. Babel's crowd is a **forced unison**, the spectacle pole exactly, and that forced unison is precisely what gets scattered. The Circle's communion (distinct voices, mutually understood) is the *anti*-Babel, the Pentecost inversion of many tongues and one understanding. (Any stricter letter-by-letter wordplay is the project's own synthesis, not traditional teaching, and is kept off this page; the resonance stands without it.)
>
> **Where this sits in the bigger map.** Communion-versus-spectacle is not a one-off binary; it is the **collective species** of a much larger split the project runs everywhere, [[Source vs Reflection]]: does a thing return you to the real, or hold you in the copy? Source vs Reflection is the **genus**; communion-vs-spectacle is what that genus looks like *when it happens to a crowd.* Communion is Source at collective scale; spectacle is Reflection at collective scale. The same genus throws off other species elsewhere, [[Witnessing vs Surveillance]] is the being-*seen* one and the *Heart/Mind* split the interior one, and reading them as siblings under one parent is what keeps the page from treating "the Crowd" as an isolated theme.

> [!note]- The thinkers behind the Crowd
> **Kierkegaard: "the crowd is the untruth."** The deepest grounded backbone for a page titled *The Crowd* is a named influence: Kierkegaard's *"the crowd is the untruth,"* which Roy has cited and posted on his own quote-cards (alongside *"anxiety is the dizziness of freedom"* and *"people demand freedom of speech as a compensation for the freedom of thought which they seldom use"*). The crowd, in this frame, is not merely where untruth happens; it is the *condition* of untruth, the place individual responsibility dissolves into a mass that can hold a conviction no single member would own alone. It grounds the premise directly: the Crowd is the terrain [[Sir Lucius]] needs in order to exist.
>
> **Debord: why Lucius is bound to the Crowd.** Guy Debord's *Society of the Spectacle* (1967): the spectacle is not a collection of images but *a social relationship mediated by images*, and once life is lived as accumulated spectacle, the representation becomes more real than the thing it represents. The Crowd is what converts life *into* spectacle: *spectare*, to look at. Remove the watchers and the spectacle simply ceases to exist, which is the structural reason [[Sir Lucius]] is at once its operator and its product. He is the part of the self that only exists while it is being seen.
>
> **Bread, circuses, and the arena's second floor.** The historical antecedent is [[The Circus#Where it comes from (bread and circuses)|Panem et Circenses]], Juvenal's diagnosis (c. 100 CE) that the Roman public, which once *"bestowed commands, consulships, legions, and all else,"* came to *"eagerly yearn for just two things — bread and circuses."* The crowd is sedated not by force but by *entertainment*; the chains are pleasurable. The mapping onto the attention economy is almost too direct (bread → the dopamine of content; circuses → the optimized feed), and the modern comedy is that we would download the games voluntarily and rate them five stars. There is a darker second floor: the Roman circus and arena were the literal sites where witnesses were *killed* for crowd entertainment, so the Crowd is also a martyrdom-site jury, and *"I'll chew you up, spit you out"* reads at once as the industry machine *and* the arena's description of consuming those who refused to recant (see [[Consumption]]).
>
> **The long crowd-lineage** (gathered on [[The Audience]]): the **Roman arena crowd** whose thumb decides life or death; the **biblical mob** that screams *"crucify him"* (Matthew 27:23), collective emotion hardened into a verdict; **Elias Canetti's** *Crowds and Power* (the crowd as its own entity with its own physics of feeling); the **carnival barker and the mark** (P.T. Barnum's willing "sucker"); **Euripides' *Bacchae*** (the audience-as-mob that dismembers the king, the permitted scream that kills); and Paul's wry **1 Corinthians 4:9**, *"we are made a spectacle unto the world, and to angels, and to men."* The figure's modern delivery mechanism is [[The Algorithm]], which optimizes for exactly the appetite Juvenal named.

> [!note]- Split by voice, not by year
> It is tempting to read a clean chronological decay: communion in 2018, command in 2022, scream in 2026. Resist it. The split is **by voice, not by year**: *33* (2022) already carries *both* poles, since [[I Like to Party]] and [[Pay Attention (feat. little luna)]] are Lucius-pole crowd-handling sitting inside the same album as plenty of communion. Treating it as a timeline misses the mechanism. The Crowd does not degrade over the discography; it gets sung two opposite ways throughout, and the variable is always *who is holding the mic.*

> [!note]- Source notes
> **Grounded.** The sing-back device is a real, recurring feature of the catalogue, and its key lyrics are verifiable in the released songs: *"the crooks and believers all sing the same song"* ([[Great Time to Be Human]]); *"take your seat, scream and shout,"* *"they love it when you lose control,"* *"did you like the show?,"* *"I'll chew you up, spit you out"* ([[Welcome to the Circus]]); *"everyone looks just like you,"* the *"beautiful stranger,"* and the *never-gonna-die* survival-chant set against the Walk of Fame ([[City of Angels]]); the *ring-around-the-rosie* spine, *"coming in like a plague from the dark side,"* and *"might run for president, I'm so presidential"* ([[Not Your Homie]]); the herding and *"the people are mine"* ([[I Like to Party]]); the id-permission chant ([[Bananas]]). The *resurrection-versus-taxidermy* gloss on [[City of Angels]] is interpretation, not a sung lyric. Roy's rejection of "fan" for *"co-creators of the world,"* his Kierkegaard quote-cards, and his public sterile-banana gloss are from his own public posts and interviews ([[Public Posts]]). The live sing-back event and the *"bounce (Live Underground)"* reel are public official posts.
>
> **Strong reading.** The corpus-wide **voice-split**, that the echo runs toward communion when Roy sings and toward command when Lucius sings, is the page's central interpretive claim, offered as a strong reading, not a fact. It was built skeptical (call-and-response is everywhere in pop) and survives only on the directional split, with a clear falsifier named in the text. Reading *"everyone looks just like you"* and *"all sing the same song"* as one flattening-operation across registers, and *Not Your Homie*'s circle-game as the communion-gesture inverted, are strong readings consistent with the released material.
>
> **Framework / resonance.** The sacramental sense of communion, the crowd-as-Babel reading, Kierkegaard's *"the crowd is the untruth,"* Juvenal's *panem et circenses* and the arena-as-witness-site, the long historical crowd-lineage, and Debord's spectacle are framework correspondences and named influences, harmonics worth hearing, held as resonance rather than as claims the songs assert. A standing honesty guard, noted on [[Great Time to Be Human]]: that song is the most collaborative track on the album (five writers), so its single line may be dark-comedy irony rather than a single-author crowd-cosmology; hold the synchronized-crowd thesis as a strong reading and the gag-reading as live.

> [!question]- Open questions
> - Is the Crowd ever *neutral*, or is the difference entirely in the **mode**, how it is sung? (The lab leans: a force, neutral as a noun, charged by direction.)
> - Is the crowd's love of watching you lose control a **moral failure** of the crowd, or a **designed feature** of the system the crowd cannot be blamed for?
> - Can a member of the Crowd become a *co-creator*, and if so, what is the move? Does it require leaving the Circus, or refusing to be reduced to audience *within* it?
> - Is *"co-creators of the world"* a **warning** (the crowd is responsible for what it generates inside itself) or a **comfort** (the co-creator is never just a passive spectator, however the system seats them)?
> - Does communion require a *faceless* source ([[Mary]]) to sing toward, where spectacle sings toward a *faced* object (the *"beautiful stranger"*)?
> - Does the project diagnose the crowd-trap without naming an exit (as Juvenal diagnosed but did not prescribe), or is there a way to bear witness *within* the Circus rather than escape it or be consumed by it?
