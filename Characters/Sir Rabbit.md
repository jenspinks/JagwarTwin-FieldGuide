# Brandon & Sir Rabbit: the original self

<img src="https://plumcreekranch.com/jagwar/boyroy.png" style="float: right; width: 175px; margin-left: 16px; margin-bottom: 8px;">

Picture a boy alone in a bedroom, seven years old, a crayon rabbit on the floor and shadows pooling at the edge of the lamplight.

He loved to dance. At a Christian school he was the only boy in the ballet class, and for a while that was simply where he came alive. Then the adults around him started reading it through the wrong lens, and the room turned cold. The other children closed in with a verdict no child should ever have to carry: *"you're not a real boy."* He prayed to be made *good.* He came home crying. He never danced again.

What he did was quieter, and more terrible, than crying it out. He performed a small execution, and buried the part of himself that had been happiest in the light: *"I killed that ballerina boy / I buried him inside."*

But a grave does not stay one thing. This one sent two figures back up into the world. The central question is not whether he existed, but whether the system can become safe enough for him to return.

> **One-line reading.** One wound at seven sends *two* figures out of the grave. **The Ballerina Boy** (Brandon) is the buried *content,* the stage-alive child who went into the ground. **Sir Rabbit** is the searching *self* built over him, walking around identity-vacant but intact, looking for a name.

## Start here

The two figures on this page come from the same grave, and they are worth keeping distinct, because the distinction carries the whole story.

The Ballerina Boy is the child who was buried: the original expressive light, hidden after shame made being seen feel dangerous. He is not dead. He is preserved underground.

Sir Rabbit is what walks around afterward. The searching self, missing a name, saying in effect *"I do not know who I am, but I must be someone,"* and going looking.

The genealogy runs clean: wound → buried child (the Ballerina Boy) → searching rabbit (Sir Rabbit) → armor ([[Sir Lucius]]). One injury, three downstream figures, each a different stage of the same unfinished sentence.

They also sit at different levels of certainty, and this page keeps them apart on purpose. Brandon is literal, triple-confirmed autobiography. Sir Rabbit is a dream-and-poem figure, read as interpretation. Meet them below as two figures sharing an origin, not one figure with two names.

## Where they sit

Most of the Jagwar Twin cast are masks, voices, or faculties. This page holds the exceptions: the person at the bottom of it all, and the seeker who first went looking for him.

Everyone else is defined by their relation to the buried child. [[Sir Lucius]] is the armor that grows over the grave. [[Roy]] is the grown man deciding whether to climb back down for him. [[Mary]] is the voice that blesses him by name. [[Jagwar Twin]] is the container that holds them all together. Pull the child out of the center and the rest of the cast loses the thing it was organized around.

That is the load-bearing move, and it reframes the whole quest. The arc is not *"defeat Lucius and free the boy."* Lucius was built to guard the boy. The arc is subtler, and kinder: earn enough trust that the protector no longer has to bury what he loves.

## Meet them in the songs

You do not need the mythology to meet either figure. [[Ballerina Boy]] is where the wound is stated outright and the buried child is named for the first time. [[Good Time (1-08)]] is the dream where Roy becomes the rabbit and the clown-faced second rabbit slides into the passenger seat. [[Weirdness]] is the healing pair, a faceless feminine voice singing *"you were born to shine"* over the same dancing child. And the *Sir Rabbit* poem, published on Roy's own platform, is the searching self's full initiation, start to finish.

Read the songs to meet them. Read the rest of this page to understand why everything is organized around them.

## The buried child: Brandon, the Ballerina Boy

<img src="https://plumcreekranch.com/jagwar/shadowboy.png" style="float: left; width: 80px; margin-right: 14px; margin-bottom: 6px;">

> [!infobox] Brandon, the Ballerina Boy
> - **Also known as:** the *Ballerina Boy* · the buried child · the buried light · the pre-shame self
> - **Nature:** softness, embodiment, play, the part of [[Roy]] told *"you're not a real boy"*
> - **First named:** *"Ballerina Boy"* (single), June 13 2025
> - **Founding event:** the age-7 ballet wound, *"I buried him inside"*
> - **Key symbols:** [[The Moon]] · the stage · the dance
> - **Related:** [[Roy]] · [[Sir Lucius]] · [[Mary]] · [[Weirdness]]

In the language of the inner system, Brandon is the exile: the most vulnerable, most feeling part, sealed away because being seen once cost too much. Everything else the arc builds, the protector, the searcher, the grown interpreter, is organized around the fact of him being down there. Keep that tenderness in view. This is the sincere wound at the center, the thing the machinery was built to guard, not a bit to riff on.

He is also the rare figure here whose backstory is not mythology. The wound is literal autobiography, told publicly by [[Roy]] outside the lyric, and triple-confirmed: in the song itself, in a sit-down interview, and, independently, in a concert-venue artist bio that simply notes he *"embraced ballet as a child while his peers pursued sports."* When the work feels heaviest, it is usually because it has circled back to this kid.

### The wound, in his own sequence

[[Roy]] released the story himself, in order, both inside the song and out of it. Age seven, a Christian school, ballet: *"I came alive when I was on that stage."* Then rumors, and a teacher calls his father: *"We think your son is gay."* The boy overhears (*"I overheard cause he was mad"*) and reasons his way to the cruelest possible conclusion, *"Gay must mean bad or else they wouldn't call,"* and prays *"to God to make me good cause it wasn't my fault."* He is the only boy in the class. He stands *"back against the wall"* while the girls circle him with the chant that becomes the project's founding wound: *"you're not a real boy."*

In the interview telling, the picture is even more disarming: *"a little blonde bowl cut… tights, ballet shoes, tap shoes,"* the only boy in the room, who came home crying *"I'm never gonna dance again."* The comic tenderness of tiny tap-shoe Roy is exactly what makes the next part land, because the shame, he says, got *"pushed so deep into the subconscious you don't know where it came from."*

His father gives the one piece of love available: *"there's nothing wrong with you, my son."* It is real, and it is not enough. The boy does the thing instead. He buries himself. *"I never danced again."*

And here is the quiet, devastating fact the whole arc turns on: a seven-year-old's answer to being shamed was not to cry it out and move on. It was to perform an execution. *"I killed that ballerina boy / I buried him inside."* Everything after, the masks, the protector, the depression, the search, grew out of that grave.

### What he is

Strip the biography down to a function, and the Ballerina Boy is the embodied, undefended, stage-alive self: softness, play, feminine-coded expression, creativity that has not yet learned to protect itself. He is what a person is *before* they learn to perform a version of themselves the world will stop punishing.

The wound is not that he was hurt. It is that he was told he was *bad,* at a Christian school, where social shame curdles into moral and spiritual shame. *"You're not a real boy"* does not critique what he did. It attacks what he *is.* That is the difference between an injury and a verdict, and it is why the response had to be so total. You can recover from being hurt. Being declared incompatible with survival is a different problem, and solving it is what forces the psyche to manufacture a protector.

### The grave is the origin of everything else

Here is the reading that reframes the entire cast: [[Sir Lucius]] is not the Ballerina Boy. He is what grows around the grave. When softness is buried as unsurvivable, the psyche still has to live, so it builds the opposite of softness: strategy, performance, control, mockery-before-being-mocked, power-before-vulnerability. That armor is Lucius, formed at the precise moment the child went into the ground.

Which makes the relationship gentler than it first looks. The boy is not *destroyed* by [[Sir Lucius]] so much as *hidden behind* him. Lucius is the guard at the door of the wound: severe, clever, mocking, often cruel, but originally built to keep the most vulnerable part from being shamed again. You do not storm that door. You make the room behind it safe enough that the guard can finally stand down, because the thing he is guarding was always the thing he loved most.

Two claims there, and they sit at different levels, so keep them apart. That the burial *produced* Lucius is interpretation; the song never names him. But that Lucius is *invested* in keeping this grave closed is grounded fact, and it is the single most telling piece of evidence on the page. In a theatrical episode of the video series, Lucius mimes a little ballet move at the camera and sneers: *"Do you know what your lowest-performing song of all time is? Ballerina Boy. When you're vulnerable you LOSE!"*

That is not random cruelty. A shadow who fact-checks your streaming numbers to wound you, the inner critic with a Spotify-for-Artists dashboard, is dark comedy doing serious work. Lucius mocks exactly *one* song by name, and it is this one. If [[Roy]] rescues the buried child, Lucius loses his monopoly on protection. A figure with a demonstrable stake in keeping a specific grave closed tells you what is buried there matters most.

### The loop, and why the answer is not success

The structure of the song is the argument. Verse one is the schoolyard. Verse two leaps forward to seventeen, when [[Roy]] *"started a band and I was on a mission,"* and the childhood chant survives the jump, transposed into the music industry: *"Driving 'round the country / couldn't afford an egg McMuffin / music just wasn't landing,"* and there it is again, the same verdict in a new uniform, *"you're not a real boy."* Then it opens into the existential question outright: *"What makes a real boy?"*

Same mechanism across every venue: schoolyard, religion, label, algorithm, audience, money. The move is always the same: *sacrifice the true self so the outer world stops punishing you.*

That egg McMuffin is the whole figure in one detail. A man stands at the most extreme fork imaginable, and the emblem of rock-bottom he reaches for is that he could not afford a fast-food breakfast. It is genuinely funny and genuinely devastating at once: cosmic stakes measured in hash browns. The smallness is the point. Humiliation scaled down to breakfast is still humiliation.

Which is what makes the fork's resolution so pointed: *"Do I kill myself or come back for that kid I let go."* Notice the choice [[Roy]] does *not* offer himself. He never asks *do I become successful?* He asks *do I come back for that kid?* The chosen answer is integration, not achievement. It goes inward first, the exact inverse of a bargain that says win the outer game and maybe feel something later. *"This song's for Ballerina boy / This song's a prayer for hope."* The song is not a report of a rescue. It *is* the rescue act, a prayer with the kid's name in it.

But integration is not demolition, and this is the exile's hardest problem. The armor that buried him is the same armor that kept him, and the person, alive. So the whole arc compresses into a single, tender question:

> [!quote] The master sentence
> *How can the buried light be allowed to flow without destroying the structure that kept it alive?*

That is why the answer cannot be to win, and cannot be to fight. Winning leaves the child underground. Fighting the protector attacks the very thing that preserved the child through the years [[Roy]] could not. The task is to let the light back up *through* the structure, until the structure no longer has to be a wall.

### The two blessings

The song answers the world's hostility not with armor but with blessing, and it does so twice, in two voices that are easy to conflate and shouldn't be.

The **father's** blessing is narrated inside the story: *"there's nothing wrong with you, my son."* It is real love, and it fails anyway, because it is stated in the negative. It is a pardon that still operates inside the same right/wrong courtroom the teacher and the girls were using. It acquits the charge; it does not leave the court. Telling a shamed child *you are not broken* is simply not the same as telling him *you are, exactly as you are, good.*

The **outro** blessing is the other thing. Delivered by an older, motherly female voice, it is generative rather than exculpatory: *"You are one of a kind, God's design… so my son, let your light shine… The moon reminds us that even in the darkness there is light. There's no one else like you out there."* It does not answer an accusation. It claims the child's nature outright. Both lines address *"my son,"* which makes them easy to hear as one speaker, but they are two voices, and the difference between them is the whole resolution.

And the deepest blessing arrives in the feminine voice, exactly where shame tried to erase him. That placement is the song's quiet thesis: the divine feminine enters, in full, precisely at the site of the wound. Lucius protects by armoring, become untouchable. The maternal voice protects by blessing, you are already, exactly, right. The arc hinges on that contrast.

## The searching self: Sir Rabbit

<img src="https://plumcreekranch.com/jagwar/fool1.jpeg" style="float: right; width: 155px; margin-left: 16px; margin-bottom: 8px;">

> [!infobox] Sir Rabbit
> - **Also known as:** the seeker · the pre-Lucius self · *"Sir Rabbit pre-Lucius"* *(Roy's own gloss)*
> - **Role:** Roy's initiatory self-image, the searching self before the split
> - **First appeared:** the *Sir Rabbit* poem, 2022 (Roy's own platform), then the *Good Time (1:08)* dream, 2023
> - **Defining line:** *"I do not know… but I must be someone."*
> - **Key symbols:** [[The Rabbit]] · the heartbeat · the violet moon · the spider · the dove
> - **Related:** [[Roy]] · [[Sir Lucius]] · the Ballerina Boy (above) · [[Mary]] · [[The Protector]]

Picture a rabbit driving down a highway at night, looking for something he cannot name. That is the whole figure in one image: not a hero, not a villain, just a self in motion with no fixed identity yet, *"I do not know… but I must be someone."* Roy has put this rabbit on the record twice, and called him, in his own words, *"Sir Rabbit pre-Lucius."* That gloss is the whole thesis. The rabbit is the self *before* the protector-mind hardens into a guide.

One load-bearing note up front, so the rest stays clear: this is the [[Roy]]-self at an earlier stage, not a third character standing beside Roy and Lucius, and not a baby Lucius. In the grounded dream, *Roy* is the rabbit and *Lucius* is the **second** rabbit. So "pre-Lucius" means the self *before* the split. Hold that and everything follows.

What makes him worth his own section is timing. Every other figure in this world arrives *after* something has gone wrong: the mask, the mirror, the shame-voice. Sir Rabbit is the one snapshot we have of the self with the wound but without the armor: forgetful, searching, heart-recovering, and, crucially, still teachable. He is the moment before the second rabbit slides into the seat.

### The two rabbit texts

Roy has put the rabbit on the record in exactly two places. The grounded claim is modest: he pictures himself as a rabbit, across two texts. Reading that rabbit as the seeker-self, the Roy-pole before the split, is interpretation, but it sits very close to both.

**The poem (2022).** A long initiation Roy published on his own Hall of Mirrors platform: a rabbit in *"a garden full of sound"* who takes a walk to count the stars and gets pulled into a full cosmic journey. A strange cat, a violet moon that turns out to be a machine, a fox with a box, a forgotten heartbeat, a dove with a lamp, a grinning spider at the Divide, and a leap *"straight through the Mourning Sun"* that lands him *"back where he begun."* Roy filed it under origins, and later glossed it himself: *"this looks sir rabbit pre-lucius."*

**The dream (2023).** Around the release of [[Good Time (1-08)]], Roy described a dream he'd had, and it is worth telling with the comedy intact, because the cosmic initiation literally begins as a celebrity bit. Roy leaves a party, a white minivan pulls up, and *"guess who's in the white minivan? It's Drake, naturally."* Drake gives him some fame advice (*"things I wish people would have told me when I started"*), then gets out and says *"it's your turn to drive."* The wheel changes hands and the dream proper begins. *"During this whole thing, I'm a rabbit. Somehow I know that."* He is driving, *"looking for something, but I didn't know what I was looking for,"* when a figure drops into the passenger seat, also a rabbit, *"but he's got like clown makeup on, and he says, I'm Sir Lucius… I'm here to help you."* The clown-makeup rabbit has all the answers and is factually right about every turn, *"but I had a weird feeling in my heart… he was telling the truth, but he was manipulating me in some way. And I couldn't explain why."* The dream ends in an involuntary crash, *"a white semi truck with a lightning bolt comes — boom, hits my car,"* and Roy wakes at 1:08. The title is the clock-time.

So the rabbit is not a young Lucius. He is the self that exists *before* Lucius arrives, which is exactly why the counterfeit guide can intercept him.

### The authentic path and the intercepted path

Put the two texts side by side and the same journey is being told twice, once the right way, once hijacked. This is the section's central reading, and it is a strong one.

The **poem is the authentic arc.** A real initiation, endured in order: the rabbit is carried up, trapped, forced inward, recovers his own heartbeat, is given light, is named at the threshold, and *chooses* the leap. He returns transformed, *"back where he begun,"* the same starting point, a different self.

The **dream is the intercepted arc.** Same seeker, same directionless searching, but the wrong guide arrives first. Lucius takes the guide's seat before any real initiation has happened. He is factually right and emotionally wrong, and the rabbit's heart catches the manipulation before his mind can explain it. There is no chosen leap. There is a lightning-crash he did not choose.

The cleanest way to hold the pair: the poem shows the seeker's true initiation; the dream shows that same seeker intercepted at the threshold by the wrong guide. One ends in a leap. The other ends in a wreck.

(The crash, eerily, later showed up in waking life: a semi rear-ended Roy on the freeway. He noted he had been listening to BTS right before, *"surely has nothing to do with the story."* Dry. And it, too, is a bit: the real truck driver climbs out worried about the wrong thing, *"oh no, I can't have TMZ coming here… you're a celebrity, right?"* Roy answers *"everyone's a celebrity,"* the driver squints at him and tries again, *"are you Russian?"* The initiation's most violent beat, delivered deadpan.)

That pair is also the sharpest thing the rabbit texts pin down about how Lucius actually works, and it is subtler than the better-known *"the truth that deceives."* Lucius is the counterfeit guide who gives true answers *without the heartbeat.* He occupies the guide-seat before the initiation has been endured:

> **Lucius doesn't only deceive by lying; he deceives by arriving too early with true answers.**

The poem shows what a real guide costs you: the box, the silence, the forgotten heartbeat you have to find yourself, the leap nobody can make for you. The dream shows the shortcut: a guide who skips all of it, hands you the correct turns, and lets your heart go quietly wrong while your mind nods along. Same destination words, opposite road.

### The wound underneath

Sir Rabbit does not come from nowhere. He is what the childhood wound produces. The Ballerina Boy is the buried *content,* the embodied child told *"you're not a real boy"* and pushed down. Sir Rabbit is the searching *self* that results: identity-vacancy walking around, directionless but intact, still capable of taking a leap. And the armor that eventually forms over him is [[Sir Lucius]] / [[The Protector]].

The line runs: wound → buried child → searching rabbit → armor. Sir Rabbit is the third link, the one easiest to miss, because by the time anyone is looking, the armor is already on.

## Relationships

The tightest way to hold the whole cast is by its relation to the buried child. [[Sir Lucius]] protects by concealment, [[Mary]] protects by blessing, and [[Roy]] heals by witnessing. All three are trying to help, which is the arc's real difficulty.

- **[[Roy]]:** the Ballerina Boy *is* Roy's buried child self, and the age-7 ballet wound is Roy's own autobiography. Sir Rabbit is Roy's picture of himself one stage earlier, the seeker before the split. Grounded that Roy self-identifies as the rabbit; the character-register reading (Sir Rabbit = the Roy-pole seeker) is a strong one. His own open question keeps it live: is the buried ballerina boy the same as what [[Mary]] is reaching toward?
- **[[Sir Lucius]]:** the armor formed the moment the child went under, and the **second** rabbit in the dream, the one in clown makeup who drops into the passenger seat saying *"I'm here to help you."* He mocks *Ballerina Boy* by name to defend his own necessity, yet he *"genuinely held the buried light"* through the years [[Roy]] could not. The resolution is not defeating him. It is earning enough trust that he no longer has to bury what he loves.
- **[[Mary]]:** the divine-feminine blessing voice of the *Ballerina Boy* outro, the clearest Mary-class appearance in the catalogue. Her *"let your light shine"* / *"you were born to shine"* is the structural answer to the shame-voice's *"you're not a real boy."* In the poem she is present *as the Dove,* the guide who gives light and lets go, a Mary-function rather than the named Mary. Whether Mary *is* the one Roy comes back for stays open.
- **[[Weirdness]]:** the healing pair. Its faceless feminine voice, *"you were born to shine,"* answers the wound directly, and its dancing child *"in the rain"* maps onto the same pre-shame freedom as the ballerina.
- **[[Not Your Homie]]:** the opposite pole of one wound. The shadow says *"you don't know me,"* the child says *"come back for me."* To know Lucius, [[Roy]] has to know the Ballerina Boy.
- **[[The Protector]]:** the survival-self that calcifies into Lucius, the armor that forms around the pre-Lucius initiate.
- **[[The Moon]]:** the buried child's tender symbol, light that survives the night, reflected rather than self-generated, preservation-through-darkness.

### The deeper cuts

Everything above stands on its own. What follows is the appendix: the long inventories, the poem's full cast, the frameworks held loosely, the source notes, and the open questions. Open what you want. Ignore all of it and nothing breaks.

> [!note]- The same light, differently oriented
> The tightest single-sentence version of the genesis reading lives on the [[Weirdness]] essay: Lucius is *"the split-off radiance of the buried child — the same light that went underground at seven years old — operating under the wrong orientation."* That is the key move. Lucius is not the *opposite* of the Ballerina Boy; he is the **same light** bent. The child's stage-radiance, sealed off and re-tasked as armor, lit by fear instead of joy. It is also why [[Roy]] can say of the protector that *"the deal may have been worth it,"* because Lucius *"genuinely held the buried light"* through years [[Roy]] could not. The protector kept the light alive by keeping it imprisoned. (See [[The Dweller on the Threshold]] for the mechanism by which a buried self accretes a guardian.)

> [!note]- The counter-algorithm, and the joke on Lucius
> The song refuses the performance economy on its own terms; it declines the metric. It risks being *"low-performing"* precisely because its worth is not denominated in streams. Form and content agree all the way down: the off-album single about the off-album child, kept out of the very spectacle it diagnoses. (The kid who got told he did not belong made a song that did not make the tracklist. Why it was kept off the album is not publicly stated, so that is a wink at the irony, not a claimed intent.) And the joke is on Lucius: he calls it the *"lowest-performing"* song, which is the entire point. It loses by the scoreboard because it refuses to play that game. He is wielding the one metric the song was built to ignore. See [[Communion vs Spectacle]] and [[The Audience]].

> [!note]- The moon, and the Mary-class voice
> The outro's *"the moon reminds us that even in the darkness there is light"* may be the most load-bearing symbolic line in the catalogue. The moon **does not generate light, it reflects it.** That is the signature of borrowed, preserved light: not a source, but proof a source exists somewhere past the dark. (See [[The Moon]], which lists this song as the *tender* pole of the symbol, *"preservation-through-darkness."*) The blessing voice is the clearest [[Mary]]-class appearance in the whole body of work, and possibly the earliest chronologically. The female, motherly voice is **grounded** (audio-confirmed); the identification of *that voice* with the named [[Mary]] of [[No. 1 Champion (la Conquistadora)|#1 Champion]] is the working assumption, held as a strong read until a casting credit settles it. Who sings the outro is not credited: present her as *"a motherly female voice,"* not a named performer.

> [!note]- The esoteric registers (framework, not authorial intent)
> The figure sits cleanly inside several interpretive traditions [[Roy]] openly studies. These are correspondences, not claims about what the song "means."
>
> - **Jungian.** The Ballerina Boy reads as the **Divine Child,** the true embodied self (with anima elements, not merely the anima). [[Sir Lucius]] is the compensatory protector the psyche manufactures; the parental, lunar voice is the witnessing **Self** that blesses. The deepest wound is not that the child was hurt, it is that he was made *incompatible with survival,* which is what forces the psyche to build Lucius.
> - **Gnostic.** *"You're not a real boy"* is demiurgic language: a false authority telling the soul its lived truth is invalid, training it to forget itself. The rescue is *gnosis,* remembering what was true before the world misnamed you. (It rhymes with [[Soul Is A Star]]'s *"do you know who you are?"*) See [[Gnosticism - The World as Trap]].
> - **Kabbalah.** The [[Kabbalah - The Tree of Life|Tree]] in miniature, held lightly: the trauma happens in the world of body and rule, the buried image lives in the lunar memory-realm, and the rescue requires entering that realm without being consumed by it, on the way up toward the heart and the blessing *"let your light shine."* The heavier apparatus is interpretation, kept off this page. See [[Kabbalah - The Tree of Life]].
> - **The loop becomes a circle.** Adult [[Roy]] returns for child Roy, while the child was *already* being held by a future-self and a divine voice in the outro. The recursion stops being a trap and becomes a developmental circle, rhyming with [[The Circle]]'s *"inside of the inside"* and [[The Thread]]'s retrieved feeling. See [[The Loop and the Circle]].

> [!note]- The Pinocchio analogue (curated, held loosely)
> *Pinocchio* sits on [[Roy]]'s own [[Concepts/The I'm 33 Board|board of films]] he says the pattern shows up in, which makes it a **Roy-curated** mythic rhyme, not an inherited template. It gathers all three grounded elements at once: a *made boy* told he is not a *real boy;* a figure defined by *the string* tying him to his maker; and a *feminine voice* that confers realness (the Blue Fairy and the motherly outro, held as the [[Mary]]-function in strict structural rhyme). Discipline matters here: the wound is *also* literal autobiography. The myth coexists; it does not replace. The claim is **not** "the Ballerina Boy is about Pinocchio." (The string-inversion, *I've Got No Strings* selling severance as freedom where [[The Circle]] inverts it to *remembering* the held string, lives mainly on the [[Inversion]] page.)

> [!note]- The wider web (companions, contrasts, brackets)
> - **[[Happy Face]]:** the coping mechanism the wound installs, *"if you're sad don't let it show,"* the perfect-child armor.
> - **[[Welcome to the Circus]]:** the public spectacle whose private origin is this song. The Ballerina Boy is a *structural absence* there. He is never named, with two readings: he is sealed *underneath* the Circus, buried before it was built; or he is what the Circus *cannot absorb,* his absence his protection. *"The most important figure in the project is not in the song, and the absence is the song's shape."* See [[The Circus]].
> - **[[Move To You]]:** the contrast that completes him. Where *Ballerina Boy* interred the child behind armor, *Move To You* lets the tender-self narrate in its *own* voice, because reciprocal, non-possessive love finally made the room safe.
> - **[[Soul Is A Star]]** and **[[Another Way II Heaven]]:** the doctrine and the map. *"Do you know who you are?"* is answered autobiographically here; *"one become two… I was still I"* is the metaphysics this song lives out. **[[SOL]]** is the love-current the rescue moves toward, and the song Lucius overrides in the video series.
> - **[[Loser]]** (2018 debut): the *earliest* anchor. Roy's gloss on the children's choir, *"getting back to that childlike self before people told you you were different… when you were just you, unashamed,"* states the buried-child doctrine seven years before this song names it. The catalogue is bracketed by children's voices with inverted functions: 2018's child-chorus is the *recovered* pre-shame self (awakening); the 2026 [[The Circle (Lucius Version)|Circle (Lucius Version)]] child-voice is the *captured* self sung to sleep (sedation). Same device, opposite vector.
> - **[[Jagwar Twin]]** (the container): the meta-system holds three interior layers, [[Sir Lucius]] (mind and strategy), [[Roy]] (heart and meaning), and the Ballerina Boy (origin and light). The pre-persona human layer collapses *into* the buried-child figure rather than standing apart, which keeps the interior cast lean.

> | **The Violet Moon** | Beautiful violet moon in the sky; on arrival it is *a machine* with *"whirling gears and funny ears"* | Mechanized reflection: charm first, apparatus revealed second (the exact shape of Lucius) |
> | **The Fox** | Offers to teach *"The Game of Many Things,"* invites the rabbit into a box; the lid *slams shut* | A trickster who traps you into yourself. Manipulative in form, medicinal in result |
> | **The heartbeat** | Inside the box, the rabbit recovers a forgotten pulse, *"a heartbeat he forgot"* | The key to the *"other mind,"* the part of you the machine cannot generate |
> | **The Dove** | Waits in an emerald garden tree with a lamp, gives light, then *releases* the rabbit | A guide who helps and lets go, light without a leash |
> | **The Spider** | Grinning, golden-caped, at *"The Divide"*; poses the name question, names the dreaming, offers the leap | The threshold-keeper; selfhood is a *name* problem before it is anything else |
> | **The Mourning Sun** | The rabbit *chooses* to leap *"straight through the Mourning Sun"* and returns *"back where he begun"* | The passage home: transformed return, not a reset |
>
> (The violet moon's mechanized reflection is developed on [[Source vs Reflection]]; the threshold where the Spider stands has its own page, [[The Divide]].)
>
> Three of these do enough work to pull out of the table.
>
> **The Fox, because not every trickster is Lucius.** He is *"ever cunning,"* he baits the rabbit into a box and slams the lid, and then it turns out the box was the point. *"The key is hidden in yourself,"* he says. *"Just listen for the knock."* Trapped and sulking, the rabbit eventually hears the forgotten heartbeat behind the wall, knocks, and a door appears, unlocked. The Fox even arrives carrying **bread,** real provision a machine cannot make. So the Fox is manipulative in form but medicinal in result: the world has a kind of cunning that *heals.* The contrast with Lucius is exact. The Fox is manipulative-form and medicinal-result; Lucius is helpful-form and parasitic-result. One traps you into your own heartbeat; the other offers help and quietly feeds on you. Tell them apart by what they *produce.*
>
> **The Dove, a Mary-style guide, not Mary.** She waits in an emerald garden tree, holds up a lamp so the rabbit can see, and then gently sends him on: *"As much as I love company you must be on your way."* That is help that does not trap you in devotion, the precise opposite of clinging Lucius. It reads as a [[Mary]]-*function,* a light-bearer who transmits and releases. The caution matters: this is a **Mary-register,** not the named Mary. A guide who gives light and lets go *resembles* Mary's gesture without being her.
>
> **The Spider, selfhood as a name problem.** At *"The Divide,"* a grinning, golden-caped Spider stops the rabbit with the question the whole figure turns on: *"Who are you, Rabbit? And where does You come from?"* The rabbit's honest *"I do not know… but I must be someone"* is the seeker's entire condition. The Spider reframes it, *"You are someone asleep in bed, you're dreaming all the time,"* and the rabbit snaps to: *"I must wake up… I must wake up tonight!"* What makes the Spider a *true* guide is what he refuses to do. He names himself a guide, *"You're here at The Divide / I'm here to be your guide,"* he leads the rabbit to the edge, and then, asked *"is this a test? I'm not ready to fly!,"* he hands the choice back: *"that's up to you, it's not for my to pry."* He preserves the seeker's sovereignty. He will not make the leap *for* the rabbit. That refusal is exactly what the counterfeit guide will not do; Lucius answers everything *for* you.

> [!note]- The "other mind," keyed to the heartbeat
> The poem does not just trap the rabbit; it tells him *how* to escape, and the instruction refines the heart/mind split the whole project runs on. A voice on the far side of the box says: *"Now you must use your other mind, dear Rabbit, it's been mapped."* The key to that other mind is the **heartbeat.** The rabbit waits in silence, hears *"a heartbeat he forgot,"* knocks over the spot, and the door opens.
>
> So the formula sharpens. A mind *severed* from the heart becomes Lucius. A mind *returned through* the heartbeat becomes the *"other mind."* This is not heart *against* mind; it is mind *reoriented through the pulse it forgot.* And *"it's been mapped"* is doing quiet work: the faculty was always there. The rabbit is not building a new mind; he is remembering one. (Call it anamnesis, knowledge by recollection.) The same seed-doctrine shows up in the Fox's *"the key is hidden in yourself / just listen for the knock,"* which prefigures Roy's later refrain *"you are the key"* / *"you are it."*

> [!note]- The fox's box as the missing fourth container
> The world's broken-vessel logic ([[Keilim - The Breaking of the Vessels]]) usually sorts containers into a few kinds: the prison that seals the self away, and the husk that is form without seed. The Fox's box is a *third* thing, the missing **initiatory container.** Not a prison and not a husk, but an **interiorization chamber:** an enclosure that isolates the self just long enough to hear the forgotten heart, then opens.
>
> The catch is diagnostic. Boxes *look alike* from the outside; the lid slams shut on the rabbit exactly as a trap would. The difference is not the enclosure, it is what the enclosure *produces:* severance, or the heartbeat. That is the same test that separates the Fox from Lucius, scaled up to cosmology. You cannot read a container by its shape, only by its fruit.

> [!note]- Loop vs Circle, and the Mourning Sun
> The poem ends with the rabbit landing *"back where he begun,"* and that line is the sharpest statement of a distinction the work cares about a lot ([[The Circle]]). A **Loop** is return *without* transformation: the same point, the same self, nothing learned. A **Circle** is return *with memory restored:* the same point, but the subject has changed. Sir Rabbit returns to his starting place *after* the heartbeat, the Dove, the Spider, the Divide, and the chosen leap, so his return is a Circle, not a Loop. The geography is identical; the traveler is not.
>
> One spelling flag worth keeping. The leap is through the *"Mourning Sun,"* transcribed as written from the artifact. If that spelling is intentional and not a transcription slip, the ending reads as *grief-as-solar-passage* rather than a happy dawn, and the mourning/**morning** pun points straight at the **Morning Star,** Lucifer, Lucius. The rabbit passes *through* the morning sun and comes home. Spelling-dependent, so flagged, not asserted.

> [!note]- The child-knight, the White Knight, and the maze cluster
> The poem travels with a small visual cluster found in the Hall of Mirrors. One open-leaf image is a photo of **young Roy** in toy plate-armor with a dragon shield, sitting on a pony. It reads as the *biographical genesis* of [[The Protector]], the survival-self armoring up *before* it calcifies into Lucius. The careful version: the maze *selected* a childhood-armor image that already holds the armoring structure. It is **not** "Roy staged this as Lucius." The resonance is structural, not authored. (A child in a knight costume is also just a kid playing.)
>
> There is a literary rhyme too. Carroll's **White Knight,** the protective-but-absurd guide who saves Alice and then insists on reciting "the name of the song," chimes with both the toy knight *and* with the poem's proto-guides (Cat, Fox, Dove, Spider). It is a lineage note, not an authorial claim. And the poem itself is unusually well-sourced: it survives both on the crown-gated room pages *and* as three consecutive iPhone-Notes screenshots that are themselves open maze rooms, all matching the text verbatim, so it is triple-sourced, not single-witness.

> [!note]- Roy's "silly rabbit" register, and the food chain
> Outside the two canonical texts, Roy uses a whole comic rabbit-vocabulary for the Lucius-prone self. When online critics came for the project, he ran a deadpan food-chain bit: *"jagwars eat rabbits and theyre just being silly,"* *"rabbits are below jagwar on the food chain… its a hint for myself,"* and *"down the rabbit hole."* The trash-talk connects Sir Rabbit to [[The Jaguar]] along a predator/prey axis, but note how fast he folds himself in: *"We're all silly rabbits sometimes,"* and *"ill catch myself being a silly rabbit when i get mad."* Nobody is exempt, including him.
>
> After catching Lucius messing with him one week, he quoted the Trix slogan at his own shadow: *"he totally tried to fuck me up this week but i caught him… silly rabbit, tricks are for kids."* His advice to a fan was to acknowledge Lucius and then *"put that silly rabbit back in the hat,"* the whole integration arc reframed as a magician's hat-trick. (The same night he glossed the poem as "pre-lucius," he also dropped *"I can pull a rabbit out of a hat"* alongside a *"Rudolph Steiner rabbit hole with the Gospel of Luke,"* the name-research and the rabbit imagery bundled into one evening. How the Steiner/Luke thread bears on the rabbit specifically is a documented research vein, not a stated equation.) The 2026 iconography grounds it further: *"The year begins with Janus and the March Hare,"* and a gematria aside resolving to a rabbit emoji.

> [!note]- The maze provenance, Lemon, and the "earliest announcement"
> A few facts the casual reader does not need but the completist will want. The *Sir Rabbit* poem was filed by Roy in the maze's **GENESIS** collection, which holds exactly two origin pieces (the rabbit poem and *Song of the Ring*), so the rabbit is literally catalogued under *origins.* Roy called the piece *"one of my favorite pieces i've ever made."*
>
> The very first public rabbit-Lucius announcement actually *predates* both canonical texts' public canonization: *"Sir Lucius Rabbit is coming"* (2023), tagged to Lucius lore. And there is a **second** named rabbit loose in the maze, *"Lemon the rabbit,"* drawn wielding an *"acorn grenade"* in a different artifact: a militarized cartoon rabbit named after a citrus fruit. Whether Lemon belongs to a "Sir-Rabbit family" of named rabbits is an interpretive grouping, not a Roy-stated link.
>
> One resonance to *hold at arm's length:* Roy's 2023 AI collaborator "angelbaby" used a leopard-patterned, rabbit-eared avatar, fusing jaguar and rabbit-ears about two years before Lucius's rabbit-eared look. That design is the collaborator's, **not Roy's,** so it is a resonance only, not part of the iconography canon.

> [!note]- Source notes
> **Brandon (autobiography and release).** The ballet wound is real and publicly told by [[Roy]], and it is triple-confirmed: the lyric, an interview telling, and an independent venue artist bio noting childhood ballet. The single released June 13, 2025 (on Big Loud Rock under license to Mercury Records / UMG); it was kept off the *[[Lucius Lullaby (album)|Lucius Lullaby]]* album, which is a fact. Roy frames his adult dance videos as shadow-work to reclaim the buried dancer, so the *rescue* is something he is publicly, bodily doing, not just singing about. That [[Sir Lucius]] targets *this* song by name in the video series is on the record; it supports, without proving, the reading that the burial produced him.
>
> **Sir Rabbit (grounded).** Roy pictures himself as a rabbit in two public texts: the *Sir Rabbit* poem (published 2022 on his own Hall of Mirrors platform, in the GENESIS origin collection) and the [[Good Time (1-08)]] dream he narrated publicly (2023), in which he says *"I'm a rabbit"* and Lucius is the *second,* clown-makeup rabbit. Roy's own gloss, *"this looks sir rabbit pre-lucius,"* is the documented basis for reading the rabbit as the self *before* Lucius, and he dated the figure's debut himself: *"sir lucius first appeared in the video and artwork for good time 108."* The poem's text (the cat, the violet-moon machine, the fox's box, the recovered heartbeat, the dove, the spider at the Divide, the leap *"back where he begun"*) is quoted from the artifact's displayed pages. The Spider echoes a companion Hall of Mirrors room, *"I'M THE SPIDER,"* a solved, grounded artifact.
>
> **Strong readings.** That the Ballerina Boy is the system's origin and light, that the burial *generated* the protector, that the two blessings are unequal, and that the feminine voice landing at the wound is the song's thesis, are interpretation, anchored in the lyric and visual lore but not stated outright by the work. So is reading the rabbit *as* the seeker-pole, the poem-as-authentic and dream-as-intercepted pair, the Lucius mechanism (*"arriving too early with true answers"*), the wound → buried child → searching rabbit lineage, and the Fox-versus-Lucius contrast.
>
> **Framework and esoteric.** The Jungian, Gnostic, Kabbalistic, lunar, and Pinocchio readings, the Dove-as-Mary-function, the "other mind" keyed to the heartbeat, the fox's box as a fourth container-type, the Loop-versus-Circle reading, the Mourning/Morning-Star pun, and the child-knight and White-Knight resonances are correspondences drawn from traditions [[Roy]] openly studies, offered as resonance, not the song's stated meaning. The spelling of *"Mourning Sun"* is transcribed as shown and flagged for verification. The simple reading, a sincere trauma-confessional that needs no mythology, is valid and sufficient on its own.
>
> **Register guard.** This page deliberately does **not** say "Roy *is* Sir Rabbit," and does **not** treat Sir Rabbit as a third character peer to Roy, Lucius, and Mary. He is the Roy-register seeker at an earlier stage.

> [!note]- Open questions
> *Why* the *Ballerina Boy* single was kept off the album is not publicly stated. *Who* sings the outro is not credited (a *"motherly female voice,"* not a named performer). The song presses one question on itself: it states the burial outright, yet the adult is plainly a stage creature, and *"come back for that kid"* admits the burial was incomplete, so the child may have been *misidentified* (kept alive as performance, denied recognition as that child) rather than fully buried. And Roy's own open question keeps the family live: is the buried ballerina boy the same as what [[Mary]] is reaching toward? Held open, not asserted. See [[Open Questions]].
