# Water — The Deep End

> [!gist] The comparative floor beneath [[Water]]
> Water as a model of the soul is one of the oldest ideas there is, and in a few traditions it is not a metaphor at all but the stated mechanism. This floor holds the whole comparative apparatus: the older currents the surface page only names, a stage-by-stage map of the soul's water cycle, the Chandogya's three destinies, the Greek and Neoplatonic material, the Orphic thirst, the Christian, Jewish and Islamic circulations, the Taoist and modern comparisons, and the methodological guards that keep the lot honest. Dense by design. Nothing here is required to read [[Water]], and nothing here is a claim about what Roy intends. Start at [[Water]] if you have not.
>
> **One frame, before anything else.** The stage table below is *this guide's comparative construction*, not a doctrine any tradition teaches. These sources can be laid alongside parts of a hydrological sequence; they do not jointly hold one water-cycle system. Their agreements are analogical and partial, and saying so is the price of using the table at all.

---

> [!map]- The older currents (framework, not authorial intent)
> - **Thales.** The first recorded thesis of Western philosophy: the *arche*, the source-principle, is water. (Via Aristotle's report, *Metaphysics* 983b; Thales left no writings, so cite the report, not a quote.) Empedocles then named **Love** the force that draws the elements back into one. *"Love is water"* welds the first physics to the first metaphysics in three words.
> - **The Emerald Tablet.** The One Thing *ascends from the earth to the heaven and again descends to the earth* (Newton's rendering). The Hermetic circulation reads as rain before it reads as anything else: as-above-so-below not as a maxim but as a *mechanism*, sky-water and earth-water exchanging places. The board's own Kybalion **Rhythm** cell opens on flow (see [[Concepts/The Sacred Truths/Hermeticism - As Above So Below]]).
> - **Alchemy.** *Solve et coagula* is a water cycle in Latin, dissolve (scatter) and coagulate (gather), and the laboratory operation *circulatio*, distillation repeated in the pelican flask, is a literal bench-top rain-cycle. The **aqua permanens**, the "permanent water," is Mercurius in liquid phase, and Jung calls it the *medium of conjunction*, the solvent in which separated opposites reunite (*Mysterium Coniunctionis*, CW 14). Note who else Mercurius is: the *caput corvi*, the raven's head of the nigredo (*Psychology and Alchemy*, CW 12 ¶404). **The crow and the water are one spirit in two phases**, the dissolver and the carrier home. On this frame the feed's two 2026 correspondences, *"I would map a crow onto Mercury"* (April 7) and *"Love is water"* (July 8), are two halves of a single formula. See [[The Crow]].
> - **Kabbalah.** **Chesed, Love and Mercy, carries the element water**; Gevurah carries fire. This is not only a Golden-Dawn tabulation: it is attested independently in traditional Jewish Kabbalah, where the Zohar (I:119b–120a) has *"water [chesed, kindness] blended with fire [gevura, din]"*, and the Arizal via Chaim Vital sets **water–Abraham–chesed–South** against **fire–Isaac–gevurah–North**. What *is* Hermetic is the systematization, a full element-column cross-indexed to tarot and astrology. So in the tradition Roy demonstrably shelves, *love is water* is close to a table row, though that the post intends the sephira is never claimed. (One wrinkle worth keeping, illustrating the [[Concepts/The Sacred Truths/Hermeticism - As Above So Below|don't-cross-the-streams]] rule: Roy's own stated directions put **fire** in the South; the Arizal puts **water** there. The systems disagree.)
> - **Campbell.** The *I'm 33* board's Hero's-Journey wheel includes a station Roy hand-labeled **"Water"** (between *Dragon* and *Treasure*), *The Hero with a Thousand Faces* sits on the public shelf, and Campbell's own definition of the returned hero's boon is hydrological, the release again of the flow of life into the body of the world (*Hero*, prologue). One story, a thousand faces; one water, a thousand rivers; or, in the Manifesto's words, *"There's only one song."*
> - **The drop and the ocean.** The authentic Rumi is Masnavi IV:2617–22 (Nicholson), and it states the return-to-source outright: the drop, afraid of wind and earth, *"has leaped into the sea, **which was its source**… its outward form has disappeared in the sea, but its essence is inviolate and permanent."* It closes on a reversal worth holding, *"A Sea has become the suitor for a drop"*: the source courts the fragment, not only the reverse. Attar's Valley of Nothingness gives the other pole, *"A drop sunk in the sea, and it is vain / To search the sea for that one drop again"* (trans. Darbandi & Davis), and his thirty birds arrive to find the Simurgh is themselves. Beside them: Kabir's doha (*that the drop merges into the ocean, everyone knows; that the ocean merges into the drop, rarely does anyone understand*) and Ramakrishna's salt doll, which walked in to measure the sea and dissolved. **Citation guard:** the circulating *"you are the entire ocean in a drop"* Rumi attribution is apocryphal; it appears nowhere in the sourced corpus and *inverts* the direction of the authentic imagery, in which the drop surrenders into the sea. The feed already holds the fragment half in Roy's own words: *"We are drops of light"* (2023).
> - **Tao Te Ching 78 and Bruce Lee.** Nothing in the world is softer than water, and nothing better at wearing down the hard: the Taoist root of Lee's be-water teaching (origin: the 1971 *Longstreet* episode and the Pierre Berton interview, scripted by Lee's own student from Lee's doctrine, *not* Enter the Dragon). Water wins by yielding. Love as the soft thing that outlasts the hard is the same claim in the emotional register.

> [!reading]- The soul's water cycle, stage by stage
> A terminology note first: there is **no established scholarly term** for a water-cycle model of the soul. Eliade's own *"aquatic symbolism"* is the defensible phrase; *hydrotheology* is a real 1734 term meaning something else entirely, and a false friend here.

> [!reading]- The oldest full version, and its third destiny
> The *pañcāgni-vidyā* of the **Chandogya Upanishad 5.3–5.10** (7th–6th c. BCE, pre-Buddhist; parallel at Brihadaranyaka 6.2) teaches rebirth *as* the water cycle. Müller's translation, 5.10.5–6: the returning self passes *"to the ether, from the ether to the air… he becomes smoke, having become smoke, he becomes mist… having become mist, he becomes a cloud, having become a cloud, **he rains down**. Then he is born as rice and corn."*
>
> Two roads are named: the *devayāna*, which does not return, and the *pitṛyāna*, which returns as rain. And then a third, at 5.10.8: *"On neither of these two ways those small creatures are continually returning of whom it may be said, **Live and die.** Theirs is a third place."* (Sanskrit: *jāyasva mriyasva*, "be born and die.") The reason given is the line worth carrying: **"Therefore that world never becomes full."** The source is never full *because* some fragments never complete the return. Of those who land in food the text adds: *"from thence the escape is beset with most difficulties."*

> [!reading]- The Greeks make the soul literally hydrological
> Heraclitus B36: *"For souls it is death to become water, for water death to become earth, but from earth water is born, and from water soul,"* with soul occupying fire's place in the circuit. **Note the direction**, because it cuts against the easy summary: here becoming water is what happens to a soul when it *dies*, not what a soul is.
>
> Zeno takes the exhalation into Stoicism verbatim (Arius Didymus in Eusebius, *Praep. Ev.* XV.20): *"Zeno calls the soul an exhalation endowed with sensation, just as Heracleitus does… and souls likewise are exhaled from moisture."* Stage 1 is a definition for these writers, not an image. (Qualifier: on Chrysippus's account only *wise* souls persist to the conflagration, ordinary souls disperse earlier, so "reabsorption into the cosmic pneuma" is not uniform Stoic doctrine.)
>
> Porphyry then builds the descent on it in *De antro nympharum* 10: *"souls descending into generation **fly to moisture**… Heraclitus says, that moisture appears **delightful** and not deadly to souls,"* and at §5, *"souls proceeding into generation are the nymphs called naiades."* Note the valence: the fall is a *desire*, not a punishment. (That Heraclitus line is **DK B77**, transmitted only through this channel and therefore among the less-secure fragments.)
>
> Plotinus supplies the source-image, *Enneads* III.8.10: *"a spring that has no source outside itself; it **gives itself to all the rivers, yet is never exhausted** by what they take."* Set that beside Roy, March 2026: *"Generosity is not sacrifice. It's proof that you believe supply is infinite."*

> [!reading]- The initiates' version is a thirst
> The Orphic gold tablets (5th–4th c. BCE onward) put the dead soul's first crisis at a choice of water: avoid the spring by the white cypress, drink from **the lake of Mnemosyne**, Memory. The formula: *"I am the child of Earth and starry Heaven… but I am parched with thirst and I perish; give me quickly refreshing water flowing forth from the lake of Memory"* (Edmonds, Petelia tablet). The right water is the one that restores remembrance, which is [[The Circle]]'s mechanism exactly, recovery by remembering rather than acquiring.
>
> **Correction kept in view:** the tablets **never name Lethe**. Only Mnemosyne is named, and the forbidden spring is unnamed, so say *"the unnamed spring, generally identified by scholars with Lethe."* (Note also the rhyme with [[The Crow]]: Corvus, fixed beside the cup it may never drink, is the same crisis with the answer withheld.)

> [!reading]- Christian, Jewish and Islamic circulation
> Eckhart's system is, in McGinn's phrase, a *"metaphysics of flow"*: *"the dynamic reciprocity of the 'flowing forth' of all things from God's hidden* grunt *and the 'flowing-back' or 'breaking-through' of the universe into essential identity with this divine source,"* with Sermon 5b's *"God's ground is my ground, and my ground is God's ground."* Ruusbroec teaches the same eternal out-and-back (the scholarly label *regiratio* is Van Nieuwenhove's, not Ruusbroec's own, since he wrote Middle Dutch).
>
> Teresa of Avila builds the whole ladder of prayer out of irrigation (*Life*, chs. 11–22): the **well** hauled by hand, the **noria**, the diverted **stream**, and finally **rain**, *"when our Lord Himself waters it, without labour on our part."* A gradient of *decreasing effort*, where union is the stage you stop carrying water and it falls on you.
>
> Lurianic Kabbalah runs the circuit both ways at once: ***mayin nukvin***, the "feminine waters" rising from below as human longing, drawing down ***mayin dukhrin***, the "masculine waters" descending in response. And the Islamic register agrees by vocabulary, since in the Quran *rahma*, mercy, **denotes** the rain itself (7:57, 25:48: the winds sent *"before His mercy"*). (Guard: the popular claim of an Arabic etymological link between *rahma* and "moist" is false. The root is r-ḥ-m, as in *raḥim*, womb. The link is theological, not etymological.)

> [!warning]- Where the traditions diverge, which is much earlier than the end
> These sources disagree long before any finish line: on whether the soul is a stable entity at all, on whether incarnation is a fall, a delight, an ignorance, an emanation or a divine play, on whether individuality is real, provisional or illusory, on what return even means (dissolution, communion, remembrance, judgment, rebirth, transformed embodiment), and on whether a cycle is morally neutral, salvific, tragic or meaningless.
>
> The **sharpest single divergence**, and the one nearest this project, concerns the re-scattering. **Vedanta** reads the rain-return as bondage: the point is to reach the sea and stay. **Ruusbroec, the Neoplatonists, and the bodhisattva vow** read the flowing-out as the divine life itself, so that return-and-pour-back-out is the shape of love rather than a failure of it. **Ecclesiastes 1:7** supplies a third position, the cycle as futility, since the dominant scholarly reading of that verse is weariness rather than renewal (it feeds straight into *"all things are full of weariness"*). Worth keeping: the same diagram supports despair.
>
> Roy's *"I am the shore. I am not the wave"* sits interestingly against all three, declining the transient form without claiming the sea. Whether that is an answer to this fork, or simply a different image reached independently, is left open.

> [!warning]- What is uncommon in the Jagwar Twin version
> The familiar **drop-and-ocean** traditions (Rumi, Attar, Kabir, Ramakrishna) cast individual beings as drops within a divine or ultimate sea. That is a claim about *those* traditions, not a summary of every source above: water is elsewhere matter and generation (Porphyry), mercy (the Quran), the unconscious (Jung), grace (Teresa), an elemental attribution (Chesed), or a model of yielding (Tao 78). Heraclitus runs the other way outright, since in B36 it is **death** for a soul to become water.
>
> Set against the drop-and-ocean group specifically, *"Love is water"* shifts the emphasis from the **traveller** to the **medium that joins travellers**: love is the medium, souls are the drops. Re-read the stages with that substitution and each rung says something different. The sun that lifts you is love; the fall into matter is love taking a shape; the flow home is love seeking its own level; dissolution is love rejoining love with the name burned off.
>
> The precedents for *that* move are thinner and grander: Empedocles' **Philotēs**, Love as the cosmic force drawing the four roots into one against Neikos; rain-as-*rahma*; Chesed's element; Rumi's *āb-i ḥayāt ast 'ishq*; and the last line of the *Commedia*, *"l'amor che move il sole e l'altre stelle,"* *"The Love which moves the sun and the other stars"* (Longfellow). Read against the feed, two posts resolve into one sentence: *we are drops of light* plus *love is water* equals **we are drops, and what we are drops of is love.** Which is why the Kush painting Roy captioned *"Egg,"* a sun cracking open over open water, holds both halves of the cycle in a single frame.

> [!map]- The moderns, cited carefully
> Thich Nhat Hanh's *No Death, No Fear* (2002) teaches the wave that stops fearing: *"if the wave bends down and touches her true nature she will realize that she is water"* (p. 23), and *"a wave does not have to die in order to become water"* (p. 106). (**Guard:** *"a cloud never dies"* is the title of the 2022 Plum Village documentary, **not** a sentence in the book. The book's line is that of a cloud *"you do not see a real date of birth and you do not see a real date of death."*)
>
> Alan Watts, a named Roy influence, supplies the whirlpool: *"A human body is like a whirlpool, there seems to be a constant form… but it functions for the very reason that no water stays in it"* (*The Book*, ch. 2), and in the 1960 lecture, *"the whirlpool is something the stream is doing, and exactly the same way, the whole universe is doing each one of us,"* with its published form *"as the ocean 'waves,' the universe 'peoples'"* (*The Book*, ch. 1). (**Guard:** the popular *"you are something the whole universe is doing"* one-liner is an unsourced conflation of those two, and the *Wisdom of Insecurity* page cite attached to it online is fabricated.) A note on register: Roy's *"I am the shore. I am not the wave"* is often read beside Watts, but the images differ. Watts identifies the self with the *water*; the shore is not the water, it is the fixed thing the water breaks against. Hold it as a contrast, not as a correction of Watts, since nothing establishes that Roy is answering him.
>
> Behind all of it, the scholarship: Jung, *"Water is the commonest symbol for the unconscious"* (CW 9i ¶40, distinct from the CW 12 ¶57 sea citation); Eliade on the waters as *"fons et origo… the reservoir of all the possibilities of existence,"* where *"immersion is equivalent to a dissolution of forms"* and *"emersion repeats the cosmogonic act"* (*The Sacred and the Profane*, p. 130; the fuller treatment is *Patterns in Comparative Religion*, ch. V, pp. 188 ff., per Eliade's own cross-reference); and Bachelard's *Water and Dreams* (1942) for the phenomenology.

> [!receipts]- Sources, verification, and the guards
> **Framework tier throughout.** Every tradition above is attributed to its tradition and none is claimed as authorial intent. Primary citations were verified in July 2026 against the translations named in the text: Müller for the Chandogya and Mundaka; Nicholson for Rumi; Darbandi and Davis for Attar; Taylor for Porphyry; MacKenna for Plotinus; Gifford for Eusebius; Edmonds for the Orphic tablets; Lewis for Teresa; Longfellow for Dante; Feng/English and Mitchell for the Tao Te Ching, attributed separately where their wordings differ.
>
> **Misattribution guards, kept live in the body text.** The *"entire ocean in a drop"* Rumi line (apocryphal, and directionally inverted); *"before entering the sea, a river trembles"* (**Osho**, not Gibran, confirmed by full-text search of four Gibran corpora under positive control); *"a cloud never dies"* (a 2022 film title, not a line in *No Death, No Fear*); *"you are something the whole universe is doing"* (unsourced Watts conflation, with a fabricated page cite in circulation); the *rahma*/"moist" etymology (false); "be water" as *Enter the Dragon* (wrong source).
>
> **Three scholarly corrections carried.** The Orphic tablets **never name Lethe**; the Porphyry line is **DK B77**; and Stoic reabsorption is not uniform doctrine, since on Chrysippus ordinary souls disperse before the conflagration.
>
> **How this floor could be wrong.** A comparative apparatus this large can make a three-word post look like the terminus of a tradition it has no demonstrated contact with. The honest position is that the traditions establish *"Love is water"* as a statable, long-standing position, and nothing more than that. If the plain reading accounts for the post as well as this apparatus does, the apparatus is decorative. None of this is the answer; it is one way of seeing it.
