# The Rooms Behind the Gallery

Sixty-eight [[Hall of Mirrors/Hall of Mirrors|Hall of Mirrors]] entries, continuing this guide's gallery sequence.

The Hall's uploaded self takes shape through what it keeps: early songs, childhood art, inherited objects, working notes and borrowed images. Read together, these fragments suggest a self assembled through memory and relationship. The discarded version, the unfinished thought and the people behind the music all have a place in that reading. [[Hall of Mirrors/Artifacts#Why the artifacts matter|Why the artifacts matter →]]

The connections do much of the work. A room's name can open another room; a notebook page can be divided among several doors; a poem can put the same animals into motion that a diagram fixes on a ladder. The entries below follow those relationships into the songs, characters and ideas they help us understand. A shared source gives the fragments context, while a symbolic correspondence remains a reading.

The rooms are listed **in the order they opened**, not in an authored narrative sequence. Their numbers continue the sequence used in this guide.

## Before you start

`A phrase in this typeface` is the exact wording that opens a room, and **exact** means exact. One wrong apostrophe and nothing happens. Where a key ends in <span class="jt-space">␠</span>, there is a single space on the end of it, invisible, and it matters. The maze's own text box quietly strips that space, so those rooms could not be opened by typing at all.

**Filed under** is the Hall's own label for the shelf a room sits on. There are thirteen shelves, and every room sits on one of them: *Concealed Generations*, *Phantoms of the Past*, *Seeds of Infinity*, *Buried Things*, and so on. The shelf is often the most interesting thing about a room.

Card titles open the room in the maze itself, which will want a wallet connected. Images open at full size. **Read the discovery →** jumps to the write-up further down the page.

### 164 · A Commonplace Pathway

<span class="jt-facts">**Key** `The Way To Heaven`  ·  **Filed under** Time Garden (22:1)  ·  Image</span>

The room divides one sentence between lock and title: *“the way to Heaven lies through Hell, but it is a commonplace pathway.”* `The Way To Heaven` opens the page; **A Commonplace Pathway** completes it. The artifact sits inside its own sentence.

That sentence, and the nearby notes on resistance and conduction, closely follow William G. Gray's *The Ladder of Lights*. Gray's argument is not escape from matter but transformation through polarity: without resistance there is no reaction, and the route upward passes through the force that opposes it. The room therefore treats “Hell” as a necessary field of contact rather than a destination to romanticize.

The larger source page extends the operation. A human-shaped Ark carries Heaven, Man, and Earth; animals enter through a side door; a Flaming Sword forms a maze; Love and Sacrifice require a Link; higher balance holds both A and B, while lower balance excludes one side. ## What the maze does with them

**Filing gives an object a second meaning.** The same dove appears as *Venus is a Dove* and [[#215 · How to Steer the Soul|How to Steer the Soul]], an identity in one room and a question of direction in another. Family keepsakes sit beside diagrams and inherited texts. Read as a self-archive, the Hall makes relationship part of what a person contains. The **VI Link** shelf's echo of Grandma Vi is suggestive; its name also belongs to the language around SARAVI.

**Reassembly becomes part of reading.** The [[#158 · Genesis Tree Code|Genesis Tree Code]] joins a creation diagram to a song through the naming of the song. The [[#180 · Fox=Mind|animal relay]] lets a poem's journey be followed through actual doors. Parent pages restore context to their fragments, while songs and drawings can show what an idea does in different forms. The links are demonstrable; the larger story we read through them still needs its own argument.

**The unfinished belongs to the work.** [[#165 · Soul Is A Star, Unreleased Adlibs|Adlibs]], [[#175 · Dreaming on a Golden Couch|rejected cover art]] and [[#177 · 167 Octa|early recordings]] preserve choices and possibilities that the released catalogue cannot show on its own. The Hall makes those earlier states available again. In that sense, remembering is an active part of the project: what was set aside can return with a different use.
