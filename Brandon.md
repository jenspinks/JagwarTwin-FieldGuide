# Brandon

> **One-line reading.** Brandon is the buried child of the whole mythology — the boy interred at seven — and the name is not an invention: **Brandon is Roy's own legal first name**, the name he stopped using. The lore buried the boy; the biography buried the name. This guide reads them as the same burial.

## Start here

Every wound in this world traces to a sentence said over a child: *you're not a real boy.* The child it was said over is this figure. Seven years old, a Christian school, a ballet class where he was the only boy and *"came alive"* on stage — until the taunt, the phone call, the prayer to be made *good*. His response was not grief but an execution: *"I killed that ballerina boy / I buried him inside."* Everything else in the catalogue is built on that grave — the masks, the protector, the decades of searching. The full beat-map lives on [[Ballerina Boy]], the song; this page holds the *figure*, read across the whole cast.

The genealogy the guide uses, one injury with three downstream selves: **wound → buried child (Brandon) → searching self ([[Sir Rabbit]]) → armor ([[Sir Lucius]])**. Lucius is not the child; he is what the psyche built over the child's grave, and he has a demonstrable stake in keeping it shut — the one song he mocks *by name* in the theatrical series is this one. The arc's fork names its own resolution: *"do I kill myself or come back for that kid I let go."* Integration, not achievement. Rescue, not revenge.

## The name, and the guardrail

Two grounded facts, and one reading that joins them — kept apart on purpose, per the [[Source and Interpretation Policy]].

**Grounded:** the artist's legal name is Brandon Roy Wronski; *Roy* — the name he lives, records, and signs under (*El Roy*) — is the middle name. **Grounded:** the buried-child story is literal autobiography, triple-confirmed (the lyric, a filmed interview, an independent venue bio). **The reading:** that the buried child *carries the discarded first name* — that "Brandon" is who went into the ground at seven and "Roy" is who walked away from the grave — is this guide's synthesis, in the same register as [[Mary]] being "this guide's name for a recurring pattern." The work never says it. The two facts simply sit next to each other, and the guide declines to pretend it can't see them.

Note the tenderness in what the name-split implies: going by *Roy* is not concealment, it's survival — and the adult's dance videos, which Roy has publicly described as reclaiming the buried dancer, are the visible half of a recovery whose invisible half would be reclaiming the *name*. Whether that ever happens on a record is one of the openest questions in the project.

> [!note]- Advanced reading — the Divine Child (framework, not authorial intent)
> In the Jungian frame Roy demonstrably reads (see [[Carl Jung]]), Brandon is the **Divine Child**: the original, undefended, embodied creative self that goes underground when visibility becomes dangerous, and whose recovery is the point of the late work. The compensatory protector built over the grave is the shadow-figure; the blessing voice that finds the child (*"you are one of a kind, God's design… my son, let your light shine"*) reads as the Self doing what only the Self can do — and it arrives in [[Mary]]'s feminine register, precisely where the shame tried to erase him. Framework, labeled: "in Jungian terms, Brandon resembles the Divine Child" is a reading; "Brandon *is* the Divine Child" would be overreach.

> [!note]- Advanced reading — the other Brandon (a resonance, catalogued as such — never a claim)
> This drawer records a synchronicity, not a source. No Jagwar Twin material references any of the following; it entered the guide in July 2026 — the week *"Love is water"* was posted — and it is kept because the pattern is too structural to leave uncatalogued, and hedged because that is the only honest way to keep it.
>
> *The Crow* (1994) is a film about a man murdered alongside his beloved and carried back across the threshold by a crow — the psychopomp bird — powered entirely by love, through a city of perpetual rain, to finish what grief left unfinished. When the work completes, he lets go. Its star was **Brandon Lee**, who was killed on set making it (March 31, 1993, a prop-gun accident; the film released May 1994), and who was the son of **Bruce Lee** — the man whose entire philosophy compressed into one teaching: *be water* (origin: the 1971 *Longstreet* episode and Pierre Berton interview, scripted from Lee's own doctrine; he died July 20, 1973). Brandon Lee's grave in Seattle, beside his father's, carries the Paul Bowles passage he had chosen for his own wedding invitations — the one about how we treat life as an *inexhaustible well* when in truth we are given each thing only a few more times. Set that against the *I'm 33* board's red cell — *"To find truth we must plunge into its bottomless well"* — and the two wells answer each other across the decades: truth's well bottomless, life's well not. Drink now.
>
> The chain, laid out flat: a buried boy named Brandon, in a mythology whose author's buried first name is Brandon — a film-Brandon who died making the story of a man love brings back from the dead, guided by a crow — the crow being the bird Roy himself maps onto Mercury — Mercurius being, in the alchemy Jung catalogued, both the *caput corvi* (the raven's head, the nigredo) and the *aqua permanens* (the divine water, the *medium of conjunction*) — and the film-Brandon's father being the man who taught *be water*, dead on a July 20th — with *"Love is water"* posted to the feed on July 8, 2026. Two halves of one Mercurius formula, the crow and the water, and a name running down the middle of it.
>
> What this drawer does **not** say: that Roy intends any of it, references the film, or named the child for the actor (the name is his own). What it does say: the synchronicity is real *as a synchronicity* — Jung's own category for it, a meaningful coincidence that no causal thread explains — and the guide's discipline is to catalogue the pattern, label the register, and let it be exactly what it is. The mind-bird flying the heart's errand is *"my mind must serve my heart"* with wings and rain; the film got there thirty years early, and nobody had to intend that for it to be true. See [[Water]] and [[The Crow]] for the halves of the formula.
